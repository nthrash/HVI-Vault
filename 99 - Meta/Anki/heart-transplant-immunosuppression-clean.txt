What cell types mediate Acute Cellular Rejection (ACR) after heart transplant?	CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes that recognize donor MHC antigens on APCs	rejection pathophysiology
What is the peak risk window for Acute Cellular Rejection (ACR) after OHT?	Weeks 2–8 post-transplant (highest risk in the first 3–6 months overall)	rejection pathophysiology
What three findings are required to diagnose Antibody-Mediated Rejection (AMR)?	(1) Histopathology showing capillary injury / macrophage infiltrates, (2) C4d deposition, (3) circulating donor-specific antibodies (DSA)	rejection pathophysiology
What is the leading cause of death beyond 5 years post-heart transplant?	Cardiac allograft vasculopathy (CAV)	rejection pathophysiology
What is the mechanism of CAV?	Diffuse concentric intimal proliferation throughout the coronary tree driven by both immune and non-immune mechanisms	rejection pathophysiology
How is hyperacute rejection prevented in modern transplant practice?	Pre-transplant crossmatch to identify preformed antibodies; exceedingly rare if crossmatch is negative	rejection pathophysiology
What is the standard induction agent for heart transplant at most centers?	Basiliximab (Simulect) — IL-2 receptor antagonist (anti-CD25); 20 mg IV pre-op, repeat day 4	induction immunosuppression
What is the mechanism of basiliximab?	Blocks IL-2 mediated T-cell clonal expansion by antagonizing the CD25 (IL-2 receptor alpha) subunit	induction immunosuppression
When is rATG (Thymoglobulin) preferred over basiliximab for induction?	High PRA / sensitized recipients, hemodynamic instability preventing early CNI, renal failure at transplant; more potent but carries greater infection risk	induction immunosuppression
What is the dose of rATG for induction in heart transplant?	1.5 mg/kg/day IV × 3–5 days	induction immunosuppression
What intraoperative steroid is given universally at time of heart transplant?	Methylprednisolone 500–1000 mg IV at time of reperfusion	induction immunosuppression
At what creatinine threshold do most centers delay tacrolimus initiation post-OHT?	Cr > 2.5–3.0 mg/dL (institution-dependent)	induction immunosuppression
What are the three components of standard maintenance triple therapy after OHT?	(1) Calcineurin inhibitor (tacrolimus or cyclosporine), (2) Antiproliferative (MMF or MPA), (3) Corticosteroid (prednisone)	maintenance immunosuppression
What is the mechanism of tacrolimus?	Binds FK-binding protein → inhibits calcineurin phosphatase → prevents NFAT dephosphorylation → blocks IL-2 transcription → inhibits T-cell clonal expansion	maintenance immunosuppression
What is the tacrolimus trough target in the first 3 months post-OHT?	10–15 ng/mL	maintenance immunosuppression tacrolimus
What is the tacrolimus trough target from 3–12 months post-OHT?	8–12 ng/mL	maintenance immunosuppression tacrolimus
What is the tacrolimus trough target beyond 12 months post-OHT?	5–10 ng/mL	maintenance immunosuppression tacrolimus
When should tacrolimus levels be rechecked after starting an interacting medication?	Within 48–72 hours	maintenance immunosuppression tacrolimus
What is the IV-to-oral dose conversion ratio for tacrolimus?	IV dose = 1/3 to 1/4 of the oral dose	maintenance immunosuppression tacrolimus
Name four key toxicities of tacrolimus	Nephrotoxicity, neurotoxicity (tremors, PRES), hyperglycemia (PTDM), hypomagnesemia	maintenance immunosuppression tacrolimus
Why is cyclosporine now rarely used instead of tacrolimus?	Tacrolimus shows superior acute rejection prevention (30–40% relative reduction) in meta-analyses	maintenance immunosuppression
What is the early trough target for cyclosporine?	200–350 ng/mL	maintenance immunosuppression
What toxicities distinguish cyclosporine from tacrolimus?	Hirsutism and gingival hyperplasia (cyclosporine-specific); less diabetogenic than tacrolimus	maintenance immunosuppression
What is the mechanism of mycophenolate mofetil (MMF)?	MMF is converted to mycophenolic acid (MPA), which inhibits IMPDH — the rate-limiting enzyme in de novo purine synthesis — selectively blocking T and B cell proliferation	maintenance immunosuppression MMF
What is the standard MMF dose for heart transplant maintenance?	1,000–1,500 mg PO BID (total 2–3 g/day)	maintenance immunosuppression MMF
At what WBC should MMF be dose-reduced?	WBC < 2,500/µL or ANC < 1,300/µL	maintenance immunosuppression MMF
What should be done for significant GI intolerance to MMF before reducing the dose?	Switch to enteric-coated mycophenolic acid (EC-MPA / Myfortic) — significantly reduces GI side effects	maintenance immunosuppression MMF
What test should be checked before starting azathioprine?	TPMT enzyme activity — severe myelosuppression risk in poor metabolizers	maintenance immunosuppression
What is the mechanism of mTOR inhibitors (sirolimus, everolimus)?	Inhibits mTOR kinase → blocks T and B cell proliferation AND vascular smooth muscle cell proliferation	maintenance immunosuppression mTOR
Name four indications to switch from MMF to an mTOR inhibitor after OHT	(1) Progressive CAV, (2) CNI nephrotoxicity, (3) CMV disease, (4) Post-transplant malignancy / PTLD	maintenance immunosuppression mTOR
Why should mTOR inhibitors not be started within 4–6 weeks of OHT?	They significantly impair wound healing — risk of sternal dehiscence, wound breakdown	maintenance immunosuppression mTOR
What is the trough target for everolimus?	3–8 ng/mL	maintenance immunosuppression mTOR
What is the trough target for sirolimus?	5–10 ng/mL (early); 4–8 ng/mL (late)	maintenance immunosuppression mTOR
What is the maintenance prednisone dose goal long-term after OHT?	5–10 mg/day	maintenance immunosuppression steroids
Which patients may be considered for steroid withdrawal after OHT?	Low-risk patients (no prior rejection, low PRA, excellent graft function) at ≥ 12–24 months — not universally attempted	maintenance immunosuppression steroids
Name five toxicities of long-term corticosteroids in transplant recipients	Hyperglycemia (PTDM), hypertension, dyslipidemia, osteoporosis, adrenal suppression	maintenance immunosuppression steroids
How often is tacrolimus checked in the first month post-OHT?	Weekly	monitoring
When is a tacrolimus trough drawn?	12 hours after the last dose (pre-dose / trough)	monitoring
What labs are checked weekly for the first month post-OHT?	Tacrolimus level, CBC, BMP, LFTs	monitoring
What ISHLT ACR grade requires treatment?	Grade 2R (moderate) and above	rejection ACR
What is the treatment for ACR Grade 2R or 3R?	Methylprednisolone 500–1000 mg IV q24h × 3 days, followed by prednisone 1 mg/kg/day with taper; repeat biopsy in 4–6 weeks	rejection ACR
What is the endomyocardial biopsy frequency in weeks 1–6 post-OHT?	Weekly	rejection biopsy surveillance
What is the biopsy frequency from months 3–6 post-OHT?	Monthly	rejection biopsy surveillance
What is the biopsy frequency after year 2 post-OHT?	Annually	rejection biopsy surveillance
What is added to pulse steroids for hemodynamically compromising rejection?	rATG (1.5 mg/kg/day × 3–5 days) ± plasmapheresis/IVIG if AMR suspected	rejection ACR
What is the primary initial treatment for AMR to rapidly reduce DSA levels?	Plasmapheresis	rejection AMR
What agent is used for B-cell depletion in AMR treatment?	Rituximab 375 mg/m² × 1–4 doses (anti-CD20)	rejection AMR
What agents are used for plasma cell depletion in AMR?	Bortezomib (1.3 mg/m² × 4 doses) or carfilzomib	rejection AMR
What complement inhibitor is used off-label in severe AMR?	Eculizumab (anti-C5)	rejection AMR
Which immunosuppressive drug is contraindicated in pregnancy post-transplant?	MMF/MPA — teratogenic (Category D); must switch to azathioprine at least 3–6 months before planned conception	special situations pregnancy
How should immunosuppression be modified for PTLD?	Reduce CNI by 25–50%, reduce/hold MMF, switch antiproliferative to mTOR inhibitor (sirolimus/everolimus)	special situations PTLD
What is the first-line perioperative antibiotic for heart transplant surgery?	Cefazolin 2 g IV (3 g if > 120 kg) within 60 minutes before incision, repeated every 4 hours intraoperatively	antimicrobial bacterial
How long should perioperative antibiotic prophylaxis be continued post-OHT?	24 hours post-operatively (no benefit beyond 24–48h per STS guidelines)	antimicrobial bacterial
When can a heart from a bacteremic donor be transplanted?	After the donor has received ≥ 24–48 hours of targeted antimicrobial therapy against the identified organism	antimicrobial bacterial
How long should the recipient of a bacteremic donor heart receive targeted antimicrobials?	Typically 7–14 days targeting the donor isolate	antimicrobial bacterial
What is the highest-risk CMV serostatus combination after OHT?	D+/R– (seropositive donor, seronegative recipient)	CMV prophylaxis
What is the recommended CMV prophylaxis duration for D+/R– recipients per ISHLT 2023?	12 months of valganciclovir	CMV prophylaxis
What is the recommended CMV prophylaxis duration for D+/R+ or D–/R+ recipients?	6 months of valganciclovir	CMV prophylaxis
What is the drug of choice for CMV prophylaxis in heart transplant recipients?	Valganciclovir (Valcyte) 900 mg PO daily (renally adjusted)	CMV prophylaxis
What is the valganciclovir dose when CrCl is 40–59 mL/min?	450 mg daily	CMV prophylaxis renal dosing
What is the valganciclovir dose when CrCl is 25–39 mL/min?	450 mg every 2 days	CMV prophylaxis renal dosing
What CBC abnormality does valganciclovir commonly cause?	Leukopenia (dose-dependent myelosuppression — also anemia, thrombocytopenia)	CMV prophylaxis
What is the treatment for mild-moderate CMV disease post-transplant?	Valganciclovir 900 mg PO BID × 3–6 weeks	CMV treatment
What is the treatment for severe/organ-invasive CMV disease?	IV ganciclovir 5 mg/kg BID × minimum 2–3 weeks	CMV treatment
What mutation causes ganciclovir resistance in CMV, and what agents are used instead?	UL97 mutation → foscarnet or cidofovir (consult ID)	CMV treatment
Are separate HSV/VZV prophylaxis agents needed if a patient is already on valganciclovir?	No — valganciclovir provides incidental coverage for HSV and VZV	HSV VZV prophylaxis
What is the highest-risk EBV serostatus combination for PTLD after OHT?	EBV D+/R– (donor seropositive, recipient seronegative)	EBV PTLD
What is the primary monitoring strategy for EBV post-OHT in D+/R– recipients?	Monthly EBV viral load (PCR) for the first year; reduce immunosuppression at first sign of rising viral load	EBV PTLD
What is the primary management strategy for BK virus in heart transplant recipients?	Reduction of immunosuppression (CNI reduction is the primary strategy)	BKV
What is the preferred agent for PCP prophylaxis after OHT, and how long is it continued?	TMP-SMX (Bactrim) — SS tablet daily or DS tablet 3×/week; lifelong at most centers	PCP prophylaxis
What additional organisms does TMP-SMX cover beyond PCP?	Toxoplasma gondii, Nocardia, Listeria	PCP prophylaxis
Why is heart transplant uniquely high risk for Toxoplasma compared to other solid organ transplants?	The donor myocardium contains Toxoplasma cysts that can reactivate in the immunosuppressed seronegative recipient (D+/R–)	toxoplasma prophylaxis
What is the alternative prophylaxis for Toxoplasma D+/R– recipients who cannot tolerate TMP-SMX?	Pyrimethamine 25 mg PO daily + leucovorin 5 mg PO weekly × 6 months	toxoplasma prophylaxis
Which PCP alternative does NOT cover Toxoplasma?	Dapsone and aerosolized pentamidine — only atovaquone provides dual PCP + Toxoplasma coverage	PCP prophylaxis alternatives
What effect does TMP-SMX have on serum creatinine, and why?	Causes an apparent rise of 0.1–0.3 mg/dL by inhibiting tubular secretion of creatinine — NOT true nephrotoxicity; GFR is unchanged	TMP-SMX creatinine
What is the strongest risk factor for invasive Candida infection post-OHT?	Multi-site Candida colonization	antifungal prophylaxis
What is the first-line antifungal prophylaxis for low-risk OHT recipients?	Fluconazole 100–200 mg PO/IV daily × 4–6 weeks	antifungal prophylaxis
What antifungal is preferred for high-risk OHT recipients (e.g., Candida colonization, re-exploration)?	Micafungin 150 mg IV daily (echinocandin; no CNI interaction)	antifungal prophylaxis
What antifungal is preferred for Aspergillus prophylaxis/treatment post-OHT?	Voriconazole 200 mg PO BID	antifungal prophylaxis
What is the recommended prophylaxis for coccidioidomycosis in endemic areas (SW US) post-OHT?	Fluconazole 400 mg PO daily during the first year post-transplant	antifungal prophylaxis endemic
What happens to tacrolimus levels when an azole antifungal is started, and what action is required?	Levels roughly double (fluconazole) to triple/quadruple (voriconazole) — reduce tacrolimus dose by 50–75% preemptively and recheck trough in 48–72h	drug interactions
What drug dramatically decreases tacrolimus levels and why?	Rifampin — potent CYP3A4 inducer; dramatically increases tacrolimus metabolism; check levels daily if unavoidable	drug interactions
Which macrolide antibiotic is preferred in transplant patients to avoid CNI interactions?	Azithromycin (clarithromycin and erythromycin significantly increase CNI levels via CYP3A4 inhibition)	drug interactions
Why should lovastatin and simvastatin be avoided in heart transplant recipients?	Both are heavily CYP3A4 metabolized — CNI co-administration markedly increases statin levels, raising myopathy/rhabdomyolysis risk	drug interactions
Which statins are preferred in heart transplant recipients?	Pravastatin (preferred), rosuvastatin, or fluvastatin — less CYP3A4 dependent	drug interactions
What OTC supplement is a potent CYP3A4 inducer that can drop tacrolimus levels dangerously?	St. John's Wort	drug interactions
What effect does diltiazem have on CNI levels, and how can this be used clinically?	Increases CNI levels via CYP3A4 inhibition — can be used intentionally to allow lower tacrolimus doses (cost-saving strategy)	drug interactions
Before adjusting a tacrolimus dose, what must always be done first?	Check a tacrolimus trough level — empiric adjustments without levels risk over- or under-immunosuppression	clinical pearl
A heart transplant patient on valganciclovir develops leukopenia. What should be considered before holding MMF?	Valganciclovir-induced myelosuppression — check ANC; if ANC < 1,000 reduce valganciclovir or add G-CSF first	clinical pearl
A patient's creatinine rises by 0.2 mg/dL after starting TMP-SMX. What should you do?	Nothing — this is a tubular secretion effect, not nephrotoxicity; GFR is unchanged; do not stop TMP-SMX	clinical pearl
A Toxoplasma D+/R– heart transplant patient cannot tolerate TMP-SMX. What is the alternative?	Pyrimethamine 25 mg PO daily + leucovorin 5 mg PO weekly × 6 months (or atovaquone 1500 mg daily)	clinical pearl
What is the ISHLT 2023 Class IIa recommendation for managing progressive CAV?	Switch antiproliferative from MMF to sirolimus or everolimus (mTOR inhibitor) with reduced-dose CNI	clinical pearl
