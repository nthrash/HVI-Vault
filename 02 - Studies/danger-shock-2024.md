# DanGer Shock (2024)

## Danish-German Cardiogenic Shock Trial

**Specialty:** Cardiology — Cardiogenic Shock / Mechanical Circulatory Support

---

## Clinical Question

In patients with STEMI complicated by cardiogenic shock undergoing PCI, does routine implantation of a microaxial flow pump (Impella CP) in addition to standard care reduce 180-day all-cause mortality compared to standard care alone?

---

## Key Takeaway

Routine use of the Impella CP microaxial flow pump in STEMI-related cardiogenic shock reduced 180-day all-cause mortality by 12.7% absolute (NNT = 8) compared to standard care alone, making it the first mechanical circulatory support device RCT to demonstrate a survival benefit in AMI cardiogenic shock. This benefit came at the cost of significantly higher rates of bleeding, limb ischemia, renal replacement therapy, and sepsis.

---

## Major Points

- First RCT to demonstrate a survival benefit with a mechanical circulatory support device in AMI-related cardiogenic shock
- 180-day all-cause mortality: 45.8% (Impella CP) vs. 58.5% (standard care); HR 0.74 (95% CI 0.55–0.99; P = 0.04); NNT = 8
- Composite secondary outcome (escalation to additional MCS, heart transplantation, or death): 52.5% vs. 63.6% (HR 0.72; 95% CI 0.55–0.95)
- Composite safety endpoint (severe bleeding, limb ischemia, hemolysis, device failure, worsening aortic regurgitation): 24.0% vs. 6.2% (RR 4.74; NNH = 6)
- Higher rates of moderate/severe bleeding (21.8% vs. 11.9%), limb ischemia (5.6% vs. 1.1%), renal replacement therapy (41.9% vs. 26.7%), and sepsis (11.7% vs. 4.5%) in the Impella group
- ACC/AHA upgraded Impella to a Class 2a recommendation based on these results
- Long-term follow-up (up to 10 years) confirmed a sustained mortality benefit with an absolute reduction of 16.3%

---

## Design

Multicenter, international, randomized, open-label, controlled trial conducted across 14 sites in Denmark, Germany, and the United Kingdom. Enrollment occurred from 2013 to 2023 (360 randomized; 355 analyzed). Median age 67 years, 79.2% male.

---

## Limitations

- Open-label design with no sham control, introducing potential performance and detection bias
- Enrollment took approximately 10 years, during which standard-of-care practices evolved
- Strict inclusion criteria limited enrollment to roughly one-third of STEMI-related cardiogenic shock patients (and only ~5% of all cardiogenic shock admissions), limiting generalizability
- Exclusively enrolled STEMI patients; results should not be extrapolated to NSTEMI, out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, or non-ischemic cardiogenic shock
- Excluded patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (GCS < 8), who represent ~50% of cardiogenic shock presentations
- Higher early mortality in the standard-care group may introduce survival bias in complication rates (e.g., renal replacement therapy)
- Conducted only in European centers with experienced Impella operators, potentially limiting applicability in less experienced settings
- Small sample size (355 patients) with a primary outcome P-value at the margin of significance (P = 0.04)

---

## Inclusion Criteria

- STEMI or STEMI-equivalent (new ST-segment depression or LBBB < 36 hours with acute proximal coronary artery occlusion on angiography) within 36 hours of onset
- Cardiogenic shock developing within 12 hours post-revascularization and < 24 hours duration, defined by **all three** of the following:
  - **Tissue hypoperfusion:** Arterial blood lactate >= 2.5 mmol/L and/or SvO2 < 55%
  - **Hypotension:** Persistent (> 30 min) systolic blood pressure < 100 mmHg and/or need for vasopressor therapy (dopamine, norepinephrine, or epinephrine)
  - **Reduced LVEF:** Left ventricular ejection fraction < 45%

---

## Exclusion Criteria

- Cardiogenic shock lasting > 24 hours
- Cardiogenic shock unrelated to STEMI
- Mechanical complication of myocardial infarction
- Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest with persistent GCS < 8
- Severe right ventricular failure (assessed by echocardiography)
- Significant aortic valve disease
- Mechanical aortic valve prosthesis
- Vascular anatomy unsuitable for Impella placement
- Severe dementia

---

## Interventions

- **Intervention group:** Impella CP microaxial flow pump implantation plus standard care (including PCI, vasopressors/inotropes, and other guideline-directed therapies)
- **Control group:** Standard care alone (PCI, vasopressors/inotropes, and guideline-directed therapies; escalation to other MCS devices permitted per clinical judgment)

---

## Outcomes

### Primary

- All-cause mortality at 180 days: **45.8% vs. 58.5%** (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.55–0.99; P = 0.04)

### Secondary

- Composite of escalation to additional MCS, heart transplantation, or death at 6 months: **52.5% vs. 63.6%** (HR 0.72; 95% CI 0.55–0.95)
- Composite safety endpoint (severe bleeding, limb ischemia, hemolysis, device failure, worsening aortic regurgitation): **24.0% vs. 6.2%** (RR 4.74; 95% CI 2.36–9.55)
- Moderate or severe bleeding: **21.8% vs. 11.9%** (RR 2.06)
- Limb ischemia: **5.6% vs. 1.1%** (RR 5.15)
- Renal replacement therapy: **41.9% vs. 26.7%** (RR 1.98)
- Sepsis with positive blood culture: **11.7% vs. 4.5%** (RR 2.79)
