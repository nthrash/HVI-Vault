## Heart Failure, Mechanical Circulatory Support, Critical Care Cardiology & Cardiovascular Medicine

# SECTION I: HEART FAILURE WITH REDUCED EJECTION FRACTION — MEDICAL THERAPY

---

## A. ACE Inhibitors

**1. CONSENSUS (1987)** — Enalapril vs. placebo in NYHA IV HF (n=253). ~40% mortality reduction at 6 months. First proof ACE-I saves lives in severe HF.

**2. SOLVD-Treatment (1991)** — Enalapril in symptomatic HFrEF, EF ≤35% (n=2,569). 16% mortality reduction. Established ACE-I as cornerstone of HF therapy.

**3. SOLVD-Prevention (1992)** — Enalapril in asymptomatic LV dysfunction, EF ≤35%. Reduced HF hospitalizations but not mortality. Rationale for treating Stage B HF.

**4. AIRE (1993)** — Ramipril post-MI with clinical HF. 27% mortality reduction. Confirmed ACE-I benefit in post-MI HF.

**5. SAVE (1992)** — Captopril post-MI with EF ≤40% (n=2,231). 19% mortality reduction. Extended ACE-I to post-MI population.

**6. ATLAS (1999)** — High-dose vs. low-dose lisinopril in HFrEF (n=3,164). High dose reduced death/hospitalization by 12%. Supports dose optimization.

## B. Angiotensin Receptor Blockers

**7. V-HeFT II (1991)** — Enalapril vs. hydralazine-ISDN. Enalapril superior for mortality. ACE-I > vasodilator therapy.

**8. Val-HeFT (2001)** — Valsartan added to ACE-I in HFrEF (n=5,010). No mortality benefit but 27.5% reduction in HF hospitalization.

**9. CHARM-Alternative (2003)** — Candesartan in ACE-I-intolerant HFrEF (n=2,028). 23% reduction in CV death/HF hospitalization.

**10. CHARM-Added (2003)** — Candesartan added to ACE-I in HFrEF. 15% reduction in composite endpoint.

**11. CHARM-Preserved (2003)** — Candesartan in HFpEF (n=3,023). Marginal HF hospitalization reduction. No mortality benefit.

**12. ELITE II (2000)** — Losartan vs. captopril in elderly HF. No superiority. ARB not superior to ACE-I.

## C. Beta-Blockers

**13. CIBIS-II (1999)** — Bisoprolol in HFrEF (n=2,647). 34% mortality reduction. Stopped early for benefit.

**14. MERIT-HF (1999)** — Metoprolol succinate XL in HFrEF (n=3,991). 34% mortality reduction. Established metoprolol succinate.

**15. COPERNICUS (2001)** — Carvedilol in severe HF, EF <25% (n=2,289). 35% mortality reduction. Extended BB to severe HF.

**16. CIBIS-I (1994)** — Early bisoprolol feasibility trial.

**17. US Carvedilol Heart Failure Study (1996)** — Carvedilol mortality benefit established.

**18. COMET (2003)** — Carvedilol vs. metoprolol tartrate (n=3,029). Carvedilol reduced mortality 17%. Use succinate or carvedilol, not tartrate.

**19. SENIORS (2005)** — Nebivolol in elderly HF (n=2,128). 14% reduction in death/CV hospitalization.

## D. Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists

**20. RALES (1999)** — Spironolactone in NYHA III/IV HF, EF ≤35% (n=1,663). 30% mortality reduction. Established MRA in severe HF.

**21. EPHESUS (2003)** — Eplerenone post-MI with LV dysfunction EF ≤40% (n=6,632). 15% mortality reduction.

**22. EMPHASIS-HF (2011)** — Eplerenone in NYHA II, EF ≤30% (n=2,737). 37% reduction in CV death/HF hospitalization. Extended MRA to milder HF.

**23. TOPCAT (2014)** — Spironolactone in HFpEF (n=3,445). Primary endpoint not met. Americas subgroup showed benefit.

**24. FINEARTS-HF (2024)** — Finerenone in HFmrEF/HFpEF (n=6,001). Reduced HF events and CV death. Non-steroidal MRA enters HFpEF.

## E. ARNI (Angiotensin Receptor-Neprilysin Inhibitor)

**25. PARADIGM-HF (2014)** — Sacubitril/valsartan vs. enalapril in HFrEF (n=8,442). 20% reduction in CV death/HF hospitalization. Stopped early. Landmark.

**26. PIONEER-HF (2019)** — In-hospital initiation of sacubitril/valsartan vs. enalapril in acute decompensated HF. Greater NT-proBNP reduction. Supported early initiation.

**27. PARAGON-HF (2019)** — Sacubitril/valsartan in HFpEF (n=4,822). Narrowly missed primary endpoint. Signal in HFmrEF range.

**28. PARADISE-MI (2021)** — Sacubitril/valsartan post-MI. Neutral.

**29. TRANSITION (2019)** — Sacubitril/valsartan pre-discharge vs. post-discharge initiation. Both safe.

## F. SGLT2 Inhibitors

**30. DAPA-HF (2019)** — Dapagliflozin in HFrEF (n=4,744). 26% reduction in CV death/worsening HF (HR 0.74, P<0.001). Benefit regardless of diabetes. Landmark.

**31. EMPEROR-Reduced (2020)** — Empagliflozin in HFrEF (n=3,730). 25% reduction (HR 0.75, P<0.001). Confirmed SGLT2i class effect.

**32. DAPA-HF + EMPEROR-Reduced Meta-Analysis (2020)** — Zannad et al., Lancet. Pooled: 26% reduction in CV death/HHF, 38% reduction in renal composite.

**33. EMPEROR-Preserved (2021)** — Empagliflozin in HFpEF (n=5,988). 21% reduction. First therapy proven in HFpEF.

**34. DELIVER (2022)** — Dapagliflozin in HFmrEF/HFpEF (n=6,263). 18% reduction. Confirmed benefit across EF spectrum.

**35. SOLOIST-WHF (2021)** — Sotagliflozin in acute decompensated HF with diabetes (n=1,222). 33% reduction.

**36. SCORED (2021)** — Sotagliflozin in diabetes with CKD. CV and renal benefits.

**37. EMPULSE (2022)** — Empagliflozin in-hospital for acute HF. Clinical benefit at 90 days.

## G. Other HFrEF Therapies

**38. VICTORIA (2020)** — Vericiguat in recently decompensated HFrEF (n=5,050). 10% reduction in CV death/HHF (HR 0.90, P=0.02). Modest but significant. sGC stimulator.

**39. GALACTIC-HF (2021)** — Omecamtiv mecarbil (cardiac myosin activator) in HFrEF (n=8,256). 8% reduction (HR 0.92, P=0.03). Greater benefit in lower EF.

**40. DIG (1997)** — Digoxin in HFrEF (n=6,800). No mortality reduction. 28% reduction in HF hospitalization.

**41. V-HeFT I (1986)** — Hydralazine-ISDN vs. prazosin vs. placebo. First vasodilator mortality benefit in HF.

**42. A-HeFT (2004)** — Hydralazine-ISDN in Black patients with HF (n=1,050). 43% mortality reduction. Stopped early.

**43. SHIFT (2010)** — Ivabradine in HFrEF with HR ≥70 (n=6,558). 18% reduction in CV death/HHF. HR reduction as target.

## H. Iron Therapy in HF

**44. FAIR-HF (2009)** — IV ferric carboxymaltose in iron-deficient HFrEF (n=459). Improved symptoms and functional capacity.

**45. CONFIRM-HF (2015)** — IV iron in iron-deficient HF. Sustained benefit at 52 weeks.

**46. AFFIRM-AHF (2020)** — IV iron in iron-deficient acute HF (n=1,108). 26% reduction in HF hospitalizations.

**47. IRONMAN (2022)** — IV iron derisomaltose in HFrEF (n=1,137). Modest benefit in COVID-adjusted analysis.

**48. HEART-FID (2023)** — IV ferric carboxymaltose in HFrEF (n=3,065). Narrowly missed primary endpoint.

# SECTION II: ACUTE DECOMPENSATED HEART FAILURE

---

**49. DOSE (2011)** — High-dose vs. low-dose furosemide, bolus vs. continuous (n=308). No significant difference. High dose trended toward more fluid removal.

**50. CARRESS-HF (2012)** — Ultrafiltration vs. stepped pharmacologic therapy in cardiorenal (n=188). Pharmacologic superior.

**51. UNLOAD (2007)** — Ultrafiltration in acute decompensated HF. Early signal of benefit.

**52. ASCEND-HF (2011)** — Nesiritide in acute HF (n=7,141). No benefit. Nesiritide largely abandoned.

**53. RELAX-AHF (2013)** — Serelaxin in acute HF (n=1,161). Dyspnea improvement and mortality signal.

**54. RELAX-AHF-2 (2019)** — Serelaxin in AHF (n=6,545). Failed to replicate. Neutral.

**55. TRUE-AHF (2017)** — Ularitide in acute HF (n=2,157). Neutral.

**56. ADVOR (2022)** — Acetazolamide added to loop diuretics (n=519). Enhanced decongestion. Practice-relevant for diuretic resistance.

**57. STRONG-HF (2022)** — Rapid up-titration of GDMT post-AHF discharge (n=1,078). 34% reduction in HF readmission/death. Practice-changing for transitions of care.

**58. EVEREST (2007)** — Tolvaptan in acute HF (n=4,133). Symptom relief, no long-term mortality benefit.

**59. TRANSFORM-HF (2022)** — Torsemide vs. furosemide in HF (n=2,859). No difference in mortality. Settled a long debate.

**60. COACH (2010)** — Intensive vs. standard follow-up after HF hospitalization.

# SECTION III: CARDIOGENIC SHOCK — FOUNDATIONAL TRIALS

---

**61. SHOCK Trial (1999)** — Early revascularization vs. medical stabilization in AMI-CS (n=302). 6-month survival benefit (50.3% vs. 63.1%, P=0.027). Established revascularization as standard of care. The most important CS trial.

**62. SHOCK Trial — 6-Year Follow-Up (2006)** — Durable survival benefit confirmed.

**63. CULPRIT-SHOCK (2017)** — Culprit-only vs. multivessel PCI in AMI-CS with multivessel disease (n=706). Culprit-only reduced 30-day death/RRT composite.

**64. CULPRIT-SHOCK — 1-Year (2018)** — Similar long-term mortality. Reinforced culprit-only strategy.

**65. SCAI Cardiogenic Shock Classification (2019)** — Baran et al. Standardized stages A–E. Universally adopted.

**66. SCAI Classification — Updated (2022)** — Refined definitions. Added hemodynamic/lactate criteria.

**67. CardShock Study (2015)** — European CS registry. Lactate as strongest independent mortality predictor.

**68. SOAP II (2010)** — Dopamine vs. norepinephrine in shock (n=1,679). Dopamine: more arrhythmias, harmful in CS subgroup.

**69. OptimaCC / Levy et al. (2018)** — Epinephrine vs. norepinephrine in AMI-CS (n=57). Epinephrine → more refractory shock. NE-first.

**70. DOREMI (2021)** — Milrinone vs. dobutamine in CS (n=192). Neutral. No difference in LCOS composite.

**71. SURVIVE (2007)** — Levosimendan vs. dobutamine in decompensated HF. No mortality difference.

**72. Cardiogenic Shock Working Group Registry** — Kapur et al. 11,000+ patients. Largest contemporary real-world CS registry.

# SECTION IV: MECHANICAL CIRCULATORY SUPPORT

---

## A. Left Ventricular Assist Devices (LVAD)

**73. REMATCH (2001)** — Pulsatile LVAD vs. OMT in transplant-ineligible Class IV HF (n=129). 48% mortality reduction. 1-year: 52% vs. 25%. The foundational LVAD trial.

**74. INTrEPID (2007)** — Novacor LVAD vs. OMT in inotrope-dependent patients. Confirmed LVAD benefit with different platform.

**75. HeartMate II DT Trial (2009)** — Slaughter et al., NEJM. Continuous-flow vs. pulsatile LVAD for DT. 2-year composite: 46% vs. 11% (HR 0.38). Launched continuous-flow era.

**76. HeartMate II BTT Trial (2009)** — Pagani et al., JACC. Pivotal BTT trial. 91% 6-month survival. FDA approval.

**77. MOMENTUM 3 — 6-Month (2017)** — Mehra et al., NEJM. HM3 vs. HM II. Zero pump thrombosis. Primary composite: 86% vs. 77%.

**78. MOMENTUM 3 — 2-Year (2018)** — Full cohort. HM3 superiority confirmed. Lower stroke, GI bleeding, pump thrombosis.

**79. MOMENTUM 3 — Final Report (2019)** — Mehra et al., NEJM. 1,028 patients. Definitive superiority (77% vs. 65%, P<0.001). Largest LVAD RCT.

**80. MOMENTUM 3 — 5-Year (2022)** — Mehra et al., JAMA. ~58% vs. ~44% survival. Median survival >5 years. Driven by hemocompatibility gains.

**81. MOMENTUM 3 — CAP (2021)** — Mehra et al. 2,200 HM3 implants. Real-world post-trial outcomes replicated pivotal results. 2-year survival ~80%.

**82. ENDURANCE (2017)** — HeartWare HVAD vs. HM II for DT. Non-inferior, but HVAD recalled (2021) for stroke/mortality.

**83. ROADMAP (2015)** — HM II vs. OMM in ambulatory HF (INTERMACS 4–7). Improved survival/function, more AEs.

**84. LATERAL Trial (2019)** — Less invasive lateral thoracotomy for HM3. Feasibility and safety demonstrated.

**85. ELEVATE Registry (2020+)** — Post-market HM3 real-world registry. Excellent 2-year survival.

**86. INTERMACS Registry (2006–present)** — Definitive US durable MCS registry. Established INTERMACS profiles 1–7. Universal classification.

**87. STS-INTERMACS Annual Reports** — Combined STS-INTERMACS database. Gold standard US LVAD benchmarking.

**88. ARIES-HM3 (2023)** — Mehra et al., JAMA. Aspirin-free (warfarin only) in HM3 patients. Non-inferior with less bleeding. Practice-changing for LVAD anticoagulation.

**89. MAGENTUM 1 (2023)** — Lower INR targets (1.5–2.0) with HM3. Exploratory reduced anticoagulation.

## B. Impella (Microaxial Flow Pump)

**90. DanGer Shock (2024)** — Møller et al., NEJM. Impella CP vs. standard care in STEMI-CS (n=355). 180-day mortality: 45.8% vs. 58.5% (HR 0.74, P=0.04). NNT=8. First MCS RCT to show mortality benefit in AMI-CS. Guideline-changing (Class 2a).

**91. DanGer Shock — Long-Term (2025)** — Møller et al., NEJM. 16.3% absolute mortality reduction at up to 10 years. 600 additional days alive. Durable benefit.

**92. PROTECT II (2012)** — Impella 2.5 vs. IABP in high-risk PCI (n=452). 30-day MAE similar; 90-day per-protocol trend toward Impella.

**93. ISAR-SHOCK (2008)** — First Impella RCT in AMI-CS (n=26). Superior hemodynamics, no mortality difference.

**94. IMPRESS in Severe Shock (2017)** — Impella CP vs. IABP (n=48). No mortality difference. 92% cardiac arrest — brain damage main cause of death.

**95. National Cardiogenic Shock Initiative (2023)** — Basir et al., JAHA. 80-site US registry. Protocolized early Impella before PCI. 71% survival vs. historical ~50%.

**96. Detroit Cardiogenic Shock Initiative (2019)** — Phase 1 NCSI pilot. Established MCS-first protocol.

**97. RECOVER IV** — Halted 2024. DSMB stopped enrollment after DanGer Shock showed benefit.

**98. IMPELLA-STIC** — Small RCT. Impella added to IABP. No hemodynamic benefit, more complications.

## C. Intra-Aortic Balloon Pump (IABP)

**99. IABP-SHOCK II (2012)** — Thiele et al., NEJM. IABP vs. no IABP in AMI-CS (n=600). No mortality benefit (39.7% vs. 41.3%). Practice-changing.

**100. IABP-SHOCK II — 12-Month (2013)** — No benefit (52% vs. 51%). ESC downgraded to Class III.

**101. IABP-SHOCK II — 6-Year (2019)** — No benefit at 6.2 years (66.3% vs. 67.0%). Two-thirds dead despite revascularization.

**102. BCIS-1 (2010/2012)** — IABP in high-risk PCI. No 28-day benefit, but unexpected 5-year mortality reduction.

**103. CRISP-AMI (2011)** — Prophylactic IABP in anterior STEMI without shock. No benefit.

## D. VA-ECMO (Cardiogenic Shock)

**104. ECLS-SHOCK (2023)** — Thiele et al., NEJM. Largest VA-ECMO RCT (n=420). No 30-day mortality benefit (47.8% vs. 49.0%). More bleeding/vascular complications. Definitive negative.

**105. ECMO-CS (2023)** — Ostadal et al., Circulation. Immediate vs. conservative VA-ECMO (n=122). No difference. High crossover (39%).

**106. EURO SHOCK (2023)** — Banning et al. VA-ECMO vs. standard care in AMI-CS. No benefit. Third negative trial.

**107. VA-ECMO IPD Meta-Analysis (2023)** — Thiele et al., Lancet. 4 RCTs, 567 patients. No mortality benefit (OR 0.93). More bleeding (OR 2.44), vascular complications (OR 3.53).

**108. SAVE Score (2015)** — Schmidt et al. Survival prediction for VA-ECMO in CS. Validated bedside tool.

**109. ENCOURAGE Score (2016)** — Muller et al. AMI-CS on VA-ECMO predictors. Lactate >5, low-flow >60 min, age >60.

**110. HELP-ECMO Registry (2020)** — French multicenter, 414 patients. 30-day survival 37.5%. Early initiation (<6h) improved outcomes.

**111. ELSO Registry Reports (Annual)** — Largest global ECMO registry. ~40% adult cardiac, ~60% adult respiratory survival-to-discharge.

## E. ECpella (VA-ECMO + LV Unloading)

**112. Pappalardo et al. (2017)** — First large ECpella series (n=157, propensity-matched). Hospital mortality: 47% vs. 80% (P<0.001). Established concept.

**113. Patel et al. (2019)** — ECpella vs. ECMO alone. 30-day mortality: 57% vs. 78% (HR 0.51, P=0.02).

**114. ECpella Meta-Analysis (2021)** — Schrage et al. 972 patients. Improved survival (RR 0.86, P=0.009). More hemolysis/RRT. No increase in bleeding/CVA.

**115. Grandin et al. (2022)** — JACC. LV unloading during VA-ECMO. Early unloading preferred over bailout.

## F. VV-ECMO (ARDS / Respiratory Failure)

**116. EOLIA (2018)** — Combes et al., NEJM. VV-ECMO in severe ARDS (n=249). 60-day mortality: 35% vs. 46% (P=0.09). 28% crossover. Stopped for futility. Bayesian reanalysis: >95% probability of benefit.

**117. CESAR (2009)** — Peek et al., Lancet. Transfer to ECMO center improved 6-month survival (63% vs. 47%). Only 75% received ECMO.

**118. EOLIA + CESAR IPD Meta-Analysis (2020)** — Combes et al., ICM. 429 patients. 90-day mortality: 36% vs. 48% (RR 0.75, P=0.013). Most definitive VV-ECMO evidence.

**119. RESP Score (2014)** — Schmidt et al. Respiratory ECMO survival prediction. Validated pre-ECMO prognostication.

## G. ECPR (Extracorporeal CPR)

**120. ARREST Trial (2020)** — Yannopoulos et al., Lancet. ECPR vs. standard ACLS for refractory OHCA VF (n=30). Survival: 42.9% vs. 6%. Stopped early for efficacy.

**121. INCEPTION (2023)** — Suverein et al., NEJM. ECPR vs. conventional CPR (n=160). No difference (20% vs. 16%). 66% never received ECPR. Highlighted cannulation timing.

**122. Prague OHCA (2022)** — Rob et al., NEJM. Hyper-invasive approach vs. standard (n=264). No overall difference. Shockable rhythm subgroup signal.

**123. RESCUE-IHCA Score (2022)** — Shah et al. ECPR mortality prediction for IHCA. 6-variable tool (AUC 0.72).

## H. RVAD / Right Heart Support

**124. RECOVER RIGHT (2015)** — Anderson et al. Impella RP for RV failure post-LVAD/post-cardiotomy (n=30). 73.3% 30-day survival. FDA approval.

**125. TandemHeart Studies (2005)** — Thiele et al. TandemHeart vs. IABP in CS. Superior hemodynamics, no mortality difference, more complications.

**126. Protek Duo Studies (2018+)** — Percutaneous RVAD via IJ. Growing evidence base, no RCTs yet.

# SECTION V: DEVICE THERAPY — ICD AND CRT

---

**127. MADIT (1996)** — ICD in post-MI with low EF and NSVT. Mortality benefit.

**128. MADIT-II (2002)** — ICD in post-MI with EF ≤30% (n=1,232). 31% mortality reduction.

**129. SCD-HeFT (2005)** — ICD vs. amiodarone vs. placebo in HFrEF (n=2,521). ICD reduced mortality 23%.

**130. DEFINITE (2004)** — ICD in non-ischemic cardiomyopathy. Trend toward benefit.

**131. DANISH (2016)** — ICD in non-ischemic HF (n=1,116). No all-cause mortality benefit. Age <68 subgroup benefited. Challenged routine ICD in non-ischemic DCM.

**132. AVID (1997)** — ICD vs. antiarrhythmic drugs in VT/VF survivors.

**133. COMPANION (2004)** — CRT±ICD in advanced HF (n=1,520). CRT-D reduced all-cause mortality 36%.

**134. CARE-HF (2005)** — CRT in HF with EF ≤35% and dyssynchrony (n=813). 36% mortality reduction.

**135. MADIT-CRT (2009)** — CRT-D in NYHA I-II with wide QRS (n=1,820). 34% reduction in HF events.

**136. RAFT (2010)** — CRT-D vs. ICD in NYHA II/III, QRS ≥120 (n=1,798). Reduced death/HHF.

**137. REVERSE (2008)** — CRT in asymptomatic/mildly symptomatic HF.

# SECTION VIII: ATRIAL FIBRILLATION & ANTICOAGULATION

**159. RE-LY (2009)** — Dabigatran vs. warfarin in AF. Established DOAC era.

**160. ROCKET-AF (2011)** — Rivaroxaban vs. warfarin in AF.

**161. ARISTOTLE (2011)** — Apixaban vs. warfarin in AF. Superior efficacy and safety.

**162. ENGAGE AF-TIMI 48 (2013)** — Edoxaban vs. warfarin in AF.

**163. AFFIRM (2002)** — Rate vs. rhythm control in AF (n=4,060). No mortality benefit to rhythm control (pre-ablation era).

**164. RACE (2002)** — Rate vs. rhythm control in AF. Confirmed AFFIRM.

**165. CASTLE-AF (2018)** — Catheter ablation for AF in HFrEF (n=363). 38% reduction in death/HHF. Supports ablation in HF with AF.

**166. EAST-AFNET 4 (2020)** — Early rhythm control in AF (n=2,789). 21% reduction in CV outcomes. Revived rhythm control.

**167. CABANA (2019)** — Catheter ablation vs. drug therapy for AF (n=2,204). No ITT difference; per-protocol favored ablation.

**168. AUGUSTUS (2019)** — Apixaban vs. VKA and aspirin vs. placebo in AF with ACS/PCI.

**169. COMMANDER HF (2018)** — Rivaroxaban in HFrEF with CAD, sinus rhythm (n=5,022). No benefit. Routine anticoagulation in HF not supported.

**170. RAFT-AF (2022)** — Ablation vs. rate control in HF with persistent AF. Improved EF and BNP.

# SECTION XI: HYPERTENSION

---

**190. SPRINT (2015)** — Intensive (<120) vs. standard (<140) BP (n=9,361). 25% CV reduction, 27% mortality reduction. Paradigm shift.

**191. ALLHAT (2002)** — Chlorthalidone vs. amlodipine vs. lisinopril (n=33,357). Thiazide-like as effective as newer agents.

**192. HOPE (2000)** — Ramipril in high-CV-risk (n=9,297). 22% reduction in CV death/MI/stroke.

**193. LIFE (2002)** — Losartan vs. atenolol in HTN with LVH (n=9,193). Losartan superior.

**194. VALUE (2004)** — Valsartan vs. amlodipine. Similar outcomes.

**195. ACCOMPLISH (2008)** — Benazepril-amlodipine vs. benazepril-HCTZ. CCB combination superior.

**196. ASCOT-BPLA (2005)** — Amlodipine-perindopril vs. atenolol-thiazide. Modern combination superior.

**197. ACCORD-BP (2010)** — Intensive (<120) vs. standard (<140) in T2DM (n=4,733). No primary benefit. Contrast with SPRINT.

**198. PATHWAY-2 (2015)** — Spironolactone best 4th-line agent for resistant HTN.

**199. ONTARGET (2008)** — Telmisartan vs. ramipril vs. both (n=25,620). ARB = ACE-I. Combination not beneficial.

# SECTION XIV: CRITICAL CARE RELEVANT TO HF ICU

---

## A. Ventilation & ARDS

**216. ARDSNet / ARMA (2000)** — Low TV (6 mL/kg IBW) vs. 12 mL/kg (n=861). 22% mortality reduction. The single most important ventilator trial.

**217. PROSEVA (2013)** — Prone positioning in severe ARDS (n=466). 16-hour proning: mortality 16% vs. 33%. Practice-changing.

**218. ACURASYS (2010)** — Early NMB (cisatracurium) in severe ARDS (n=340). 90-day benefit. But later ROSE did not replicate.

**219. ROSE (2019)** — Early NMB vs. usual care in ARDS (n=1,006). No benefit. NMB no longer routine.

**220. ART (2017)** — Lung recruitment + high PEEP in ARDS. Increased mortality.

**221. OSCILLATE (2013)** — HFOV in ARDS. Increased mortality. HFOV largely abandoned.

**222. LOCO2 (2020)** — Liberal vs. conservative O₂ in ARDS. Liberal oxygen harmful.

## B. Sepsis & Vasopressors

**223. VASST (2008)** — Vasopressin + NE vs. NE alone in septic shock (n=778). No overall difference. Subgroup: vasopressin beneficial in less severe shock.

**224. ATHOS-3 (2017)** — Angiotensin II for vasodilatory shock (n=344). Improved BP response. Niche refractory shock.

**225. ADRENAL (2018)** — Hydrocortisone in septic shock (n=3,658). No 90-day mortality difference.

**226. APROCCHSS (2018)** — Hydrocortisone + fludrocortisone in septic shock (n=1,241). Mortality benefit (43% vs. 49%).

**227. ANDROMEDA-SHOCK (2019)** — Capillary refill time vs. lactate-guided resuscitation in septic shock. CRT-guided non-inferior. Simpler bedside assessment.

**228. ProCESS / ARISE / ProMISe (2014-2015)** — EGDT vs. usual care in septic shock. No benefit to protocolized EGDT. Usual care had improved.

## C. Renal / Cardiorenal

**229. STARRT-AKI (2020)** — Accelerated vs. standard RRT in AKI (n=3,019). No benefit to early initiation.

**230. AKIKI (2016)** — Early vs. delayed RRT in critically ill AKI (n=620). No benefit. 49% in delayed never needed RRT.

**231. IDEAL-ICU (2018)** — Early vs. delayed RRT in septic shock AKI. No difference. Stopped for futility.

## D. Transfusion & Fluids

**232. TRICC (1999)** — Restrictive (Hb 7–9) vs. liberal (10–12) in ICU (n=838). Restrictive non-inferior.

**233. TRISS (2014)** — Restrictive vs. liberal in septic shock (n=998). No difference.

**234. SAFE (2004)** — Albumin vs. saline in ICU (n=6,997). No difference overall.

**235. CHEST (2012)** — HES vs. saline in ICU (n=7,000). HES: more RRT. Avoid in critically ill.

## E. Temperature Management

**236. TTM (2013)** — 33°C vs. 36°C after OHCA (n=939). No difference.

**237. TTM2 (2021)** — Hypothermia vs. normothermia in OHCA (n=1,900). No benefit. Active fever prevention sufficient.

**238. HYPERION (2019)** — Hypothermia for non-shockable OHCA (n=584). Improved neurologic outcome (10.2% vs. 5.7%).

## F. Cardiac Arrest

**239. HACA (2002)** — Therapeutic hypothermia after cardiac arrest. Early landmark.

**240. PARAMEDIC2 (2018)** — Adrenaline vs. placebo in OHCA (n=8,014). Improved survival but no benefit in neurologic outcome.
