# Hepatic Labs & RUQ Ultrasound in Heart Failure ICU: Cardiohepatic Interactions and Biliary Mimics

## Overview

Abnormal hepatic laboratory tests are present in 20–40% of patients hospitalized with acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF) and up to 50% of those requiring mechanical circulatory support (MCS). In the HF-ICU setting, these abnormalities arise not from primary liver disease, but from **cardiohepatic interactions** — the complex pathophysiology linking right ventricular failure, elevated systemic venous pressure, and impaired hepatic perfusion. Understanding this distinction is critical because it changes management: treating the heart (not the liver) is the primary intervention.

Equally important is the ability to distinguish between true hepatobiliary pathology (acute cholecystitis, choledocholithiasis, pancreatitis) and **congestive mimics** that present with similar sonographic findings but require different management. This topic integrates the seven critical hepatic lab parameters, landmark cardiohepatic trials, and RUQ ultrasound interpretation into a unified clinical framework for HF-ICU practice.

---

## Part 1: Hepatic Lab Abnormalities in HF-ICU

### Etiology & Pathophysiology of Cardiohepatic Interactions

**Two-Hit Model of Acute Liver Injury in HF:**

1. **Hit 1: Hepatic Congestion** — Elevated right atrial pressure (RAP) causes venous backup into the hepatic sinusoids. Histologically, this results in centrilobular congestion and hepatocyte compression. The **portal vein pulsatility index (PVPI) >0.5** has 81% sensitivity and 87% specificity for congestion-driven hyperbilirubinemia.

2. **Hit 2: Hepatic Hypoperfusion** — Inadequate cardiac output (CI <2.2) reduces splanchnic perfusion, causing ischemic hepatitis. This is *not* hypotension alone — the two-hit mechanism requires both congestion AND hypoperfusion. Ischemic hepatitis (acute cardiogenic liver injury, or ACLI) is characterized by massive transaminase elevation (often >1000 U/L), rising over 1–3 days, with ALT:LDH ratio <1.5 (distinguishing it from viral hepatitis, where ALT:LDH >1.5).

**Hemodynamic Context Matters:**
- Pure congestion (elevated RAP, preserved CI) → cholestatic pattern (↑TBili, ↑ALP, ↑GGT)
- Pure shock (low CI, preserved RAP) → unlikely to cause hepatobiliary injury alone
- Two-hit (congestion + shock) → ACLI with mixed hepatocellular and cholestatic features

---

### Section 1: Total Bilirubin (TBili)

**Clinical Significance:**
TBili is an independent predictor of cardiovascular death and HF hospitalization in HFrEF patients, even after adjustment for NT-proBNP. It also independently predicts RV failure post-LVAD, making it prognostically important beyond its diagnostic role.

**Step 1: Fractionate — Direct vs. Indirect**

**Indirect TBili elevation (unconjugated):**
- Hemolysis: mechanical (LVAD, ECMO, prosthetic valves), DIC, MAHA
- Gilbert syndrome: benign, TBili rarely >6 mg/dL
- Hematoma resorption
- Drug effects (atazanavir)

Workup: CBC + differential, reticulocyte count, LDH, haptoglobin, direct Coombs test, review LVAD/ECMO hemolysis parameters.

**Direct TBili elevation (conjugated) — R Ratio:**
Calculate R Ratio = (ALT÷ULN) ÷ (ALP÷ULN):
- R >5 = hepatocellular pattern
- R 2–5 = mixed pattern
- R <2 = cholestatic pattern (typical in congestive hepatopathy)

**Step 2: Biliary Imaging**
RUQ ultrasound to evaluate for extrahepatic obstruction:
- Dilated ducts + stone → extrahepatic obstruction (MRCP or ERCP)
- Non-dilated ducts → intrahepatic cause (hemodynamic assessment, medication review, sepsis)

**Step 3: Hemodynamic Phenotyping**
Echo ± PA catheter:
- **PVPI >0.5**: 81% Sn, 87% Sp for congestion-driven TBili
- **Two-hit model**: ALT:LDH <1.5 indicates ischemic hepatitis (ACLI); peak d1–3, ≥50% fall within 72h of hemo stabilization

---

### Section 2: Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP)

**Key Distinction: Is ALP Hepatic or Bone?**
Check GGT — it is NOT present in bone, making this the critical discriminator.

**GGT elevated** → Hepatic ALP:
- Congestive hepatopathy (most common in HF-ICU): correlates with TR severity, RAP, right-sided pressures
- Drug-induced (amiodarone, antibiotics, TPN)
- Infiltrative (amyloidosis, sarcoidosis, hepatic mets)
- PBC (check AMA, ANA), PSC (check pANCA)
- Sepsis-associated cholestasis
- Biliary obstruction (RUQ ultrasound mandatory)

**GGT normal** → Non-hepatic ALP:
- Bone: Paget's disease, bony metastases, healing fracture
- Pregnancy, intestinal source (post-prandial)

**Low ALP:**
Uncommon but significant when present. Consider acute severe illness (ICU epiphenomenon), malnutrition, Zn or Mg deficiency, hypothyroidism, bisphosphonates, hypophosphatasia (rare genetic condition associated with low alkaline phosphatase, musculoskeletal symptoms, and dental anomalies — check pyridoxal-5-phosphate and phosphoethanolamine).

**Prognosis:**
The SURVIVE trial found that elevated ALP in ADHF predicted 180-day mortality of 34.9% vs. 23.5% (p=0.001) in patients without ALP elevation, reflecting the burden of systemic congestion.

---

### Section 3: Gamma-Glutamyl Transferase (GGT)

**Primary Role: Confirms ALP is Hepatic**

GGT is NOT present in bone, making it the only reliable way to distinguish hepatic from bone-derived ALP elevation when ALP is high.

**HF-Specific Interpretation:**
- GGT + ALP + TBili = **cholestatic triad** of congestive hepatopathy
- Correlates with JVD, peripheral edema, TR grade, NYHA class, and right-sided filling pressures
- GGT is inversely associated with survival in stable HF patients

**Non-Cardiac Causes:**
- **Alcohol**: GGT elevated in >50% of heavy drinkers even without overt liver disease — most sensitive marker of alcohol intake
- Medications: phenytoin, barbiturates, carbamazepine
- Pancreatic disease, diabetes, renal failure
- NAFLD / NASH
- Biliary obstruction

**Low GGT:**
Not a recognized clinical entity. Requires no workup.

---

### Section 4: AST and ALT (Transaminases)

**Stratified by Degree of Elevation:**

**>20× ULN (Massive Elevation):**
- Acute cardiogenic liver injury (ACLI) from two-hit mechanism (congestion + hypoperfusion)
- Acetaminophen toxicity, acute viral hepatitis, Budd-Chiari syndrome

Key clues for ACLI:
- ALT:LDH <1.5 (distinguishes cardiogenic from viral hepatitis)
- Rapid rise over 1–3 days
- ≥50% fall in transaminases within 72 hours of hemodynamic stabilization
- ~80–84% show ≥50% drop in prothrombin activity (distinguishes from viral hepatitis)

Management: Restore cardiac output (inotropes, MCS escalation, ensure oxygenation).

**3–20× ULN (Moderate Elevation):**
Broader differential: DILI, viral hepatitis, autoimmune hepatitis, ischemic hepatitis with partial recovery, sepsis.

Workup: ALT:LDH ratio, hepatitis panel, ANA, ASMA, medication review.

**<3× ULN (Mild Elevation):**
Nonspecific: congestive hepatopathy, NAFLD/NASH, thyroid disease, celiac disease, strenuous exercise.

Context-dependent: if persistent or rising, pursue full workup; if isolated mild, monitor.

**Kinetics & Prognosis:**
ACLI shows rapid rise over 1–3 days and normalization within 7–10 days with hemodynamic stabilization. Failure to decline despite hemo improvement suggests ongoing ischemia, DILI, viral hepatitis, or primary liver disease. The SURVIVE trial found that elevated transaminases predicted 31-day mortality of 17.6% vs. 8.4% and 180-day mortality of 31.6% vs. 22.4%, reflecting hypoperfusion burden.

**Low AST/ALT:**
Pyridoxine (B6) deficiency (ALT is pyridoxal-phosphate dependent) or advanced sarcopenia/cachexia.

---

### Section 5: Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH)

**Context-First Interpretation — LDH is Ubiquitous:**

LDH is released from any tissue undergoing ischemia, hemolysis, or inflammation. Always correlate with clinical picture.

**Acute Cardiogenic Liver Injury (ACLI):**
- LDH rises rapidly 10–20× ULN within 1–3 days
- ALT:LDH <1.5 → favors cardiogenic over viral hepatitis
- Often precedes or parallels transaminase elevation
- Falls ≥50% within 72 hours of hemodynamic stabilization

**Hemolysis (Mechanical):**
- ↑LDH + ↓haptoglobin + ↑indirect bilirubin + ↑reticulocytes
- LVAD, ECMO, mechanical valves, DIC

**Tissue Ischemia / Infarction:**
- MI: LDH-1 > LDH-2 (flipped isoenzyme pattern)
- PE: LDH-3 predominance
- Mesenteric ischemia: LDH-5

**Critical Distinction: LDH vs. Serum Lactate**

| Parameter | LDH | Serum Lactate |
|-----------|-----|---|
| What it is | Enzyme (tissue injury marker) | Metabolite (hypoperfusion marker) |
| Clinical use | Identifies ischemic tissue | Assesses perfusion/shock state |
| Prognostic value in HF | Higher LDH = worse outcomes | Trajectory predicts shock reversibility |
| Interpretation | Single value acceptable | Serial measurements more useful |

In cardiogenic shock, serial lactate clearance predicts shock reversibility: early clearance indicates reversible shock; persistent elevation suggests refractory shock or impaired hepatic lactate metabolism.

**Prognosis:**
The GALACTIC-HF trial found that higher LDH independently predicted worse outcomes in HFrEF (Q4 vs Q1: HR 1.84 for HF event or CV death). LDH improves risk prediction when added to the PREDICT-HF model.

**Low LDH:**
Rare. LDH-A or LDH-B genetic deficiency (rare genetic conditions). Not actionable in the ICU.

---

### Section 6: Albumin

**Key Concept: Albumin Half-Life ~20 Days = Reflects Chronic Dysfunction**

In contrast to INR (t½ hours–days, reflects acute synthetic failure), albumin reflects chronic hepatic synthetic impairment, malnutrition, inflammation, and hemodilution.

Hypoalbuminemia is present in ~32% of HF patients.

**Contributing Mechanisms (Multifactorial):**

1. **Hepatic Congestion** → impaired synthesis
2. **Systemic Inflammation** → cytokine-mediated suppression of synthesis (primary driver, more significant than malnutrition alone)
3. **Malnutrition / Cardiac Cachexia** → inadequate substrate
4. **Hemodilution** → volume overload
5. **Protein-Losing Enteropathy** → gut edema from elevated RAP
6. **Nephrotic Syndrome** → urinary losses (check urine protein)

**Workup:**
- LFTs + INR (synthetic function panel)
- Urine protein / albumin:creatinine ratio
- CRP, ESR (inflammation burden)
- Nutritional assessment (prealbumin if available)
- Echo: IVC, RAP (hemodilution vs. true hepatic impairment)

**Management:**
- Optimize decongestion (reduces congestion + hemodilution simultaneously)
- Optimize oral nutrition (improved outcomes in malnourished HF patients)
- IV albumin: controversial; routine use does NOT improve outcomes. Consider selectively for refractory edema/ascites + profound hypoalbuminemia on max diuretics

**Elevated Albumin:**
Almost always dehydration or hemoconcentration. Reassess volume status.

**Prognosis:**
In-hospital mortality is 4× higher with hypoalbuminemia in HF (OR 3.77). Predictive accuracy is comparable to BNP (pooled AUC 0.73). Hypoalbuminemia is an independent predictor of worse outcomes post-LVAD.

---

### Section 7: INR / Prothrombin Time

**Step 1: Exclude Anticoagulation First**

- Warfarin → INR elevation expected
- Anti-Xa DOACs may falsely elevate INR on some assays

**Step 2: Non-Anticoagulated Elevated INR — Three Most Common ICU Causes**

**1. Vitamin K Deficiency (39.6%):**
- Low FVII, FX, Protein C, Protein S
- Causes: poor nutrition, antibiotics, biliary obstruction
- Rx: Vitamin K IV or PO; plasma generally avoidable

**2. Liver Disease Pattern (35.4%):**
- Low FV, low or normal fibrinogen, high FVIII
- Indicates impaired hepatic synthetic function
- HF-specific: correlates with TBili, GGT, IVC diameter, TR severity, neurohormonal activation
- Rx: Treat underlying cause (decongestion in HF); plasma NOT appropriate for routine INR correction in liver disease

**3. Occult DOAC Effect (14.6%):**
- Anti-Xa DOAC detected on assay despite no reported anticoagulant use

**ACLI-Specific Finding:**
~80–84% of ACLI patients show ≥50% drop in prothrombin activity, distinguishing cardiogenic from viral hepatitis.

**Persistent Coagulopathy:**
Despite hemodynamic optimization → suggests progression to cardiac cirrhosis.

**Prognosis:**
INR is an independent predictor of all-cause mortality in ADHF (HR 1.89 per 0.1 increase) — higher prognostic value than MELD or MELD-XI. INR + TBili + Albumin together comprise the **hepatic synthetic function triad**.

---

### Synthetic Function Panel — Apply to All Patterns

| Parameter | Interpretation | Clinical Pearl |
|-----------|---|---|
| **Albumin** (t½ ~20 days) | Reflects CHRONIC dysfunction, inflammation, malnutrition, hemodilution | Low = congestion + inflammation + malnutrition; takes weeks to improve |
| **INR** (t½ hours–days) | Reflects ACUTE synthetic failure | INR >1.5 + encephalopathy = ALF criteria → hepatology + transplant consult |
| **MELD-Na** (TBili + INR + Cr + Na) | Prognostic score + transplant listing tool | Calculate on admission and trend serially |
| **Child-Pugh** (Albumin + INR + ascites + bili + encephalopathy) | Grades A/B/C hepatic reserve | Useful for cirrhosis assessment |
| **Platelets + Splenomegaly** | Reflects portal hypertension / cardiac cirrhosis | Low platelets + splenomegaly = suspect fibrosis progression |

**Pre-LVAD / Pre-Transplant Consideration:**
Persistent hyperbilirubinemia + abnormal imaging → liver biopsy may be required. Noninvasive tools (liver stiffness, MRE) cannot reliably differentiate congestion from fibrosis in this population.

## Part 2: RUQ Ultrasound Interpretation in HF-ICU

### The Critical Challenge: Gallbladder Wall Thickening is Nonspecific

Gallbladder wall thickening (≥3 mm) occurs in numerous non-cholecystitis conditions and is the most common source of diagnostic confusion in HF-ICU patients. The finding appears nearly expected in patients with CHF, hypoalbuminemia, and ascites, yet is often reflexively attributed to cholecystitis.

**Nonspecific Causes of GB Wall Thickening:**

| Cause | Mechanism | Key Distinguishing Features |
|-------|-----------|---|
| **Congestive Heart Failure** | Venous congestion, elevated RAP | Dilated IVC/hepatic veins, abnormal hepatic vein Doppler, NO stones, NO Murphy's sign, PVPI >0.5 |
| **Hypoalbuminemia** | Decreased oncotic pressure → wall edema | Diffuse stratified (layered) thickening, serum albumin <3 g/dL, NO stones, NO Murphy's sign |
| **Ascites** | Extrinsic fluid pressure on GB wall | Peritoneal fluid visible, wall thickening resolves with paracentesis |
| **Acute Hepatitis** | Inflammatory edema | Markedly elevated transaminases (often >1000), positive hepatitis serologies |
| **Portal Hypertension / Cirrhosis** | Venous congestion of GB wall | Stigmata of cirrhosis, splenomegaly, varices, reversed portal flow |
| **Renal Failure** | Volume overload, decreased oncotic pressure | Elevated creatinine, volume overloaded appearance |
| **Acute Cholecystitis** | Inflammatory infiltration, cystic duct obstruction | Stones + Murphy's sign + wall thickening ± focal striations (gangrenous changes) |

---

### Section 1: Acute Calculous Cholecystitis

**Sonographic Findings (Classic):**

- **Gallstones** — echogenic foci with posterior acoustic shadowing (95–99% of cases)
- **GB wall thickening ≥3–5 mm**
- **Pericholecystic fluid** — anechoic fluid surrounding gallbladder
- **Sonographic Murphy's sign** — focal tenderness over gallbladder with probe pressure (PPV 92% when combined with stones)
- **GB distension** — anteroposterior diameter >4 cm
- **Impacted stone at GB neck** — strongly associated with complicated cholecystitis

**Diagnostic Accuracy:**
- Stones + Murphy's sign: PPV 92%
- Stones + wall thickening: PPV 95%

**Important Caveat:**
Gallbladder wall changes (thickening, pericholecystic fluid) may not be present early — they can develop dynamically over a median of ~4 hours after presentation.

---

### Section 2: Acalculous Cholecystitis (AAC)

**The More Common Form in ICU (5–10% of cholecystitis, but disproportionately ICU):**

AAC is the predominant form of cholecystitis in critically ill patients. The challenge: many sonographic features overlap with findings routinely present in ICU patients (volume overload, hypoalbuminemia, systemic illness).

**SCCM Diagnostic Criteria:**

| Finding | Criterion |
|---------|-----------|
| GB wall thickening | >3 mm (some reports up to 9 mm) |
| GB distension (hydrops) | Short-axis diameter >40 mm |
| Pericholecystic fluid | Present |
| Sludge/echogenic bile | Minor criterion |
| Sonographic Murphy's sign | Often unreliable in sedated/intubated patients |
| Gallstones | **Absent** (defining feature) |

**Diagnostic Performance:**
A prospective ICU study requiring 3 major criteria (wall thickness >4 mm, hydrops, sludge) yielded:
- Specificity: 94%
- Sensitivity: 50%

The SCCM guidelines emphasize that many sonographic features of AAC are routinely present in ICU patients from volume overload, hypoalbuminemia, etc., making diagnosis particularly difficult.

**Key Distinguishing Features from Calculous Cholecystitis:**
- NO stones (by definition)
- Sludge is more prominent
- Murphy's sign often unobtainable
- Clinical context critical: prolonged fasting, TPN, mechanical ventilation, vasopressors, severe systemic illness

---

### Section 3: Biliary Obstruction / Choledocholithiasis

**Primary Finding: CBD Dilation**

- **CBD diameter >6 mm** (or >8 mm post-cholecystectomy; add 1 mm per decade after age 60) suggests obstruction
- **Intrahepatic biliary dilation** — "parallel channel" sign (dilated ducts running alongside portal veins)
- **CBD stone visualization** — echogenic focus within duct ± shadowing
- **Gallbladder distension** — Courvoisier sign if distal obstruction and GB intact

**Limitations:**
Ultrasound sensitivity for CBD stones only 50–75% (due to duodenal gas obscuration). If clinical suspicion is high despite negative ultrasound:
- MRCP: 84% sensitivity, 92% specificity
- Endoscopic ultrasound: 94% sensitivity, 89% specificity

---

### Section 4: Acute Pancreatitis

**Ultrasound as Initial Modality — Primarily to Identify Etiology**

Ultrasound is recommended to identify the underlying cause (gallstones) rather than to diagnose pancreatitis itself. The pancreas may appear sonographically normal in up to 23–27% of cases.

**When Abnormal:**

- **Enlarged, hypoechoic (edematous) pancreas** — classic finding of interstitial edematous pancreatitis
- **Peripancreatic edema/fluid** — most frequently identified (up to 63%)
- **Pancreatic duct dilation** — cited as reliable but identified in only ~12–14%
- **Peripancreatic fluid collections** — anechoic or complex
- **Gallstones in GB or CBD** — identifying etiology
- **CBD dilation** — may indicate passed or impacted stone

**Limitation:**
Bowel gas frequently obscures pancreas; ultrasound may underestimate extrapancreatic complications. CT with IV contrast is gold standard for assessing severity, necrosis, and complications.

---

### Section 5: Congestive Hepatopathy (The Critical HF-ICU Mimic)

**Most Important Entity to Distinguish from Primary Biliary/GB Disease:**

This is the diagnosis most often mimicked in HF-ICU patients. Characteristic findings:

- **Dilated IVC** with reduced or absent respiratory variation
- **Dilated hepatic veins** (>1 cm)
- **Loss of normal triphasic hepatic venous waveform** → monophasic or reversed flow on Doppler
- **Portal vein pulsatility** — abnormal pulsatile flow (PVPI >0.5) reflects transmitted right-sided pressures
- **Hepatomegaly** with heterogeneous parenchymal echotexture
- **Ascites** (often SAAG ≥1.1, supporting cardiac/portal hypertensive etiology)
- **Gallbladder wall thickening** — from venous congestion and/or hypoalbuminemia, NOT cholecystitis

**Doppler Key — Hepatic Venous Waveform:**
Normal: triphasic (S, D, A waves reflecting systolic, diastolic, and atrial components)
Abnormal in CHF: monophasic or completely reversed (indicating elevated RAP transmitted back through IVC → hepatic veins)

## Clinical Pearl: Integrating Lab Findings and Ultrasound

### Quick Decision Framework

**When confronted with abnormal hepatic labs + GB wall thickening:**

1. **Presence/absence of gallstones?** → Largest decision point
   - Stones present + Murphy's sign → calculous cholecystitis
   - No stones + wall thickening → consider AAC vs. congestive mimic

2. **Hepatic vein and IVC Doppler pattern?**
   - Abnormal (monophasic/reversed) → congestion is likely explanation
   - Normal → primary biliary disease more likely

3. **Clinical context?**
   - Fever, leukocytosis, RUQ pain → favor cholecystitis
   - No fever, no pain, stable WBC → favor congestive mimic

4. **Synthetic function?**
   - Low albumin + high INR + high TBili → hepatic congestion pattern
   - Isolated transaminase elevation → ACLI if ALT:LDH <1.5

5. **HIDA scan?**
   - Equivocal ultrasound + clinical uncertainty → hepatobiliary scintigraphy shows GB filling (rules out AAC)
   - Limited value in prolonged fasting or severe hepatic dysfunction

## Evidence & Landmark Trials

### Cardiohepatic Interactions

**Samsky MD et al. (JACC 2013):** Cardiohepatic Interactions in Heart Failure
- Seminal framework establishing the pathophysiology of liver dysfunction in HF
- Introduced the concept of congestive hepatopathy and its prognostic implications

**Xanthopoulos A et al. (JACC: HF 2019):** Heart Failure and Liver Disease
- Comprehensive review of cardiohepatic pathophysiology in modern HF therapy
- Established prevalence of abnormal LFTs and their association with poor outcomes

**Styczynski G et al. (JASE 2016):** Echocardiographic Correlates of LFTs in HF
- Demonstrated PVPI >0.5: 81% sensitivity, 87% specificity for congestion-driven hyperbilirubinemia
- Validated echo-based hemodynamic assessment as prognostic tool

### Hepatic Markers in ADHF

**Nikolaou M et al. (Eur Heart J 2013, SURVIVE Trial):** LFT Abnormalities in ADHF
- Elevated ALP: 180-day mortality 34.9% vs 23.5% (p=0.001)
- Elevated transaminases: 31-day mortality 17.6% vs 8.4%; 180-day mortality 31.6% vs 22.4%
- Established independent prognostic value of hepatic markers

**Okada A et al. (Circ J 2016):** Prognostic Value of INR in ADHF
- INR: independent predictor of all-cause mortality (HR 1.89 per 0.1 increase)
- Higher prognostic value than MELD or MELD-XI

**Ono R et al. (JACC: HF 2026, GALACTIC-HF):** LDH and Outcomes in HFrEF
- Higher LDH independently predicts worse outcomes (Q4 vs Q1: HR 1.84)
- LDH improves PREDICT-HF risk model

**El Iskandarani M et al. (Medicine 2021):** Albumin in HF — Systematic Review
- In-hospital mortality 4× higher with hypoalbuminemia (OR 3.77)
- Predictive accuracy comparable to BNP (AUC 0.73)

**Suzuki K et al. (Eur J HF 2020, PARADIGM-HF):** Sacubitril/Valsartan Effects on LFTs
- Sacubitril/valsartan reduced TBili by 2.4% vs enalapril
- Suggests that improved neurohormonal biology reduces hepatic congestion

### RUQ Ultrasound in Cholecystitis

**Strasberg SM (NEJM 2008):** Acute Calculous Cholecystitis
- Classic review of sonographic findings and diagnostic accuracy
- Stones + Murphy's sign: PPV 92%

**Ivanov D et al. (J Emerg Med 2025):** Evolution of Secondary Findings in Acute Cholecystitis
- Wall changes may develop dynamically over median ~4 hours
- Emphasizes importance of serial imaging if initial study equivocal

**Mariat G et al. (Intensive Care Med 2000):** Acalculous Cholecystitis in ICU
- Prospective study showing specificity 94%, sensitivity 50% for 3-criteria SCCM approach
- Highlights difficulty of AAC diagnosis in ICU setting

## Key Guidelines

**ACC/AHA Guidelines for Heart Failure (2022):**
- Recommend assessment of liver function at baseline and during optimization in all HF patients
- Highlight prognostic importance of hepatic markers

**Society of Critical Care Medicine (SCCM) Bedside Ultrasound Guidelines (2015):**
- Diagnostic criteria for acalculous cholecystitis in critical care
- Emphasis on clinical correlation given overlap with ICU-related findings

**American College of Emergency Physicians (ACEP) Ultrasound Guidelines (2023):**
- Recommendations for RUQ ultrasound in acute biliary disease
- Diagnostic accuracy of gallstone detection and Murphy's sign

## Clinical Pearls

1. **Gallbladder wall thickening in HF-ICU is almost expected** — the combination of CHF, hypoalbuminemia, and ascites frequently coexist. Wall thickening alone should NOT reflexively be attributed to cholecystitis. Always integrate stone presence, Murphy's sign, hepatic vein Doppler, and clinical context.

2. **ALT:LDH <1.5 is your friend in ACLI** — when transaminases are markedly elevated and you need to distinguish cardiogenic from viral hepatitis, this ratio is more reliable than absolute LDH level. Ischemic hepatitis shows ALT:LDH <1.5; viral hepatitis typically >1.5.

3. **PVPI >0.5 is the echo finding that seals the deal for congestion** — when you see 81% sensitivity and 87% specificity for congestion-driven hyperbilirubinemia, it's practically diagnostic. Portal vein pulsatility directly reflects right-sided pressure transmission.

4. **Albumin t½ ~20 days — plan for long recovery** — don't expect rapid improvement with decongestion alone. Nutritional optimization and time are required; IV albumin is controversial and doesn't improve outcomes routinely.

5. **INR >1.5 + encephalopathy in HF is ALF until proven otherwise** — this is the one time you call hepatology emergently. Acute Liver Failure criteria require both INR >1.5 AND hepatic encephalopathy in the absence of pre-existing cirrhosis. Don't miss this.

6. **The two-hit model explains why some ADHF patients develop ACLI and others don't** — congestion alone (elevated RAP) causes cholestasis. Shock alone (low CI) doesn't cause hepatobiliary injury. You need BOTH. Watch for ALT:LDH <1.5 as the tip-off.

7. **Murphy's sign is often unobtainable in sedated/vented patients** — don't dismiss AAC just because you can't elicit tenderness. Rely more heavily on imaging criteria and clinical context.

8. **HIDA scan has limited value in HF-ICU** — prolonged fasting + severe hepatic congestion reduce sensitivity. Use when ultrasound is truly equivocal and clinical suspicion is high, but understand the limitations.
