## Systematic Approach: ABCDE

| | Category | Structures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | Airway | Trachea, carina, bronchi, hila |
| **B** | Breathing | Lungs, pleura |
| **C** | Circulation | Heart, mediastinum, pulmonary vasculature |
| **D** | Diaphragm | Hemidiaphragms, costophrenic angles, upper abdomen |
| **E** | Everything else | Bones, soft tissues, lines/tubes/devices |

---

## Anatomy

### Airway

Trachea should be midline. Carina is the bifurcation into left and right mainstem bronchi. Right mainstem is wider and more vertical — foreign bodies and malpositioned ETTs preferentially enter the right side.

![Trachea and carina labeled](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-06-optimized.webp)

![Bronchial tree anatomy](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-07-optimized.webp)

### Breathing — Lung Fields

Lung fields should be symmetrically lucent. Trace from apices to costophrenic angles bilaterally.

![Lung fields outlined](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-09-optimized.webp)

### Circulation — Cardiac & Vascular

Right heart border = right atrium. Left heart border = left ventricle + left atrial appendage. Normal CTR < 50% on PA film.

![Cardiac anatomy overlay](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-11-optimized.webp)

### Hilar Anatomy

Left hilum is normally slightly higher than right. Key structures: pulmonary arteries/veins, bronchi, hilar points, paratracheal stripe.

![Hilar anatomy](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-16-optimized.webp)

### Diaphragm

Right hemidiaphragm normally slightly higher than left (liver). Gastric bubble visible under left hemidiaphragm. Costophrenic angles should be sharp.

![Diaphragm with stomach bubble](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-20-optimized.webp)

### Bony Anatomy

Posterior ribs are more horizontal (easier to count). Anterior ribs angle downward. Count ribs at the mid-clavicular line.

![Anterior vs posterior ribs](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-22-optimized.webp)

## Technique: RIPE

### Rotation

Medial clavicles should be equidistant from spinous processes. Rotation distorts heart size and can mimic lung pathology.

- Rotated left — heart size **exaggerated**
- Rotated right — heart size **underestimated**

![Rotation assessment](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-29-optimized.webp)

### Inspiration

Count anterior ribs at the mid-clavicular line above the diaphragm:

- **< 5 ribs** — under-inflated (heart looks big, bases look hazy)
- **5–6 ribs** — adequate
- **> 6 ribs** — hyperinflated (think COPD)

![Poor inspiration — 3 ribs](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-33-optimized.webp)

![Good inspiration — 6 ribs](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-34-optimized.webp)

![Flattened vs normal diaphragms](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-36-optimized.webp)

### Projection — AP vs PA

| | AP | PA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beam direction | Front to Back | Back to Front |
| Heart size | **Magnified** (further from detector) | **Accurate** (closer to detector) |
| Scapulae | Projected within lung fields | Rotated out |
| Use | Portable/bedside | Standard upright |

![AP vs PA diagram](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-38-optimized.webp)

### Exposure

Adequate exposure: vertebral bodies visible through the cardiac silhouette; left hemidiaphragm visible to the spine.

![Exposure assessment](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-42-optimized.webp)

## Pathology

### Hemithorax Whiteout — Atelectasis vs Effusion

The trachea tells you everything:

| Finding | Tracheal Shift | Mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Atelectasis** | **Toward** the whiteout | Collapse pulls structures in |
| **Large effusion** | **Away from** the whiteout | Fluid pushes structures away |

![Tracheal shift comparison](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-47-optimized.webp)

### Pneumonia

Consolidation = opacification within lung parenchyma. Look for air bronchograms. On lateral film, normal lung gets darker inferiorly — if it gets whiter, suspect basilar pneumonia.

![Pneumonia PA and lateral](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-49-optimized.webp)

### Pneumothorax

Look for: visceral pleural line + **absent lung markings** peripheral to it.

![Pneumothorax — pleural margin and absent vessels](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-52-optimized.webp)

**Tension pneumothorax (EMERGENCY):** Large pneumothorax + tracheal deviation **away** from affected side + mediastinal shift — immediate needle decompression.

![Tension pneumothorax](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-53-optimized.webp)

### CHF

Classic CXR findings:

- Cardiomegaly (CTR > 50%)
- Cephalization (upper lobe venous distension)
- Bilateral pleural effusions
- Kerley B lines (thickened interlobular septa at periphery)
- Perihilar "bat-wing" edema pattern

![CHF at admission](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-57-optimized.webp)

![CHF after treatment](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-58-optimized.webp)

![Kerley B lines](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-59-optimized.webp)

### Pleural Effusion

Earliest sign: blunted costophrenic angles. Large effusions opacify the lower hemithorax with a meniscus sign.

![Bilateral pleural effusions](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-62-optimized.webp)

### Pneumoperitoneum

Free air under the diaphragm on upright film — suspect bowel perforation — **surgical emergency**.

![Pneumoperitoneum](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-64-optimized.webp)

### Aortic Injury / Dissection

CXR signs: widened mediastinum (>8 cm), loss of aortic knob contour, left apical cap, rightward tracheal deviation.

![Widened mediastinum](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-76-optimized.webp)

![Normal vs dissection — loss of aortic knob](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-77-optimized.webp)

### ETT Positioning

ETT tip should be **2–6 cm above the carina** (approximately at the aortic knob level). Too low — right mainstem intubation — left lung atelectasis.

![ETT anatomy reference](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-70-optimized.webp)

![Right mainstem intubation — left lung collapse](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-71-optimized.webp)

### Rib Fractures

Look for cortical disruption, step-offs, or lucent lines. Easy to miss — inspect all ribs systematically.

![Rib fractures](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-67-optimized.webp)

### Mediastinal Lymphadenopathy

Bulky hila + widened mediastinum. DDx: lymphoma, TB, sarcoidosis.

![Normal vs lymphadenopathy](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/cxr_images/page-79-optimized.webp)

## Key Takeaways

1. **Technique first** — poor quality film? Ask for a repeat before interpreting
2. **ABCDE every time** — systematic approach prevents missed findings
3. **Know the emergencies** — tension pneumothorax, aortic dissection, pneumoperitoneum
4. **Tracheal position** is your best friend for whiteouts
5. **AP films exaggerate heart size** — don't call cardiomegaly on a portable
