# Chronic Lung Allograft Dysfunction (CLAD)

---

## 1. Overview

CLAD is the umbrella term for significant, persistent decline in lung function after lung transplantation in the absence of other identifiable causes. It encompasses two primary phenotypes: **Bronchiolitis Obliterans Syndrome (BOS)** and **Restrictive Allograft Syndrome (RAS)**.

**Definition:** ≥20% decline in FEV1 from the post-transplant baseline (average of the two best FEV1 values at least 3 weeks apart).

**Epidemiology:** ~50% of recipients develop BOS by 5 years; ~74% by 10 years post-transplant.

**Pathophysiology:** Manifestation of chronic alloimmune response and airway-centered rejection, compounded by infection, aspiration, and other secondary insults.

---

## 2. Diagnostic Stages

| Stage | Definition |
|-------|-----------|
| **Possible CLAD** | FEV1 decline ≥20% from baseline, lasting **<3 weeks** |
| **Probable CLAD** | FEV1 ≥20% below baseline, persisting **3 weeks – 3 months** |
| **Definitive CLAD** | FEV1 ≥20% below baseline, persisting **>3 months** |

---

## 3. Phenotype Classification

Requires FEV1, FVC, TLC, and chest CT for accurate classification.

| Phenotype | Spirometry | TLC | CT Findings |
|-----------|-----------|-----|-------------|
| **BOS (Obstructive)** | FEV1/FVC <70%; FEV1 ↓ | Normal | No persistent fibrotic opacities |
| **RAS (Restrictive)** | No obstruction | <90% of baseline | Persistent parenchymal/pleural fibrotic opacities |
| **Mixed** | Both obstructive + restrictive | Varies | Mixed features |

---

## 4. Severity Staging (Applies to Both BOS and RAS)

| Grade | FEV1 as % of Baseline |
|-------|----------------------|
| CLAD 0 | >80% |
| CLAD 1 | >65–80% |
| CLAD 2 | >50–65% |
| CLAD 3 | >35–50% |
| CLAD 4 | ≤35% |

---

## 5. Risk Factors

| Risk Factor | Notes |
|------------|-------|
| Donor-specific antibodies (DSA) | Particularly anti-HLA-DQ; chronic alloimmune driver |
| Acute rejection episodes | Recurrent episodes strongly increase risk |
| CMV infection | Direct viral and immunologic contribution |
| Bacterial/fungal colonization | Pseudomonas and Aspergillus increase risk |
| Primary graft dysfunction (PGD) | Early post-transplant injury correlates with later CLAD |
| GERD / aspiration | Chronic microaspiration implicated |
| Single lung transplant | Higher risk vs bilateral (mixed PFT interpretation challenge) |

---

## 6. Differential Diagnosis

Before diagnosing CLAD, exclude:
- Active infection (bacterial, viral, fungal)
- Acute rejection
- Airway anastomotic stricture
- Drug toxicity
- Pulmonary embolism
- Aspiration
- Post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder (PTLD)
- Diaphragmatic dysfunction

---

## 7. Diagnostic Workup

| Study | Purpose |
|-------|---------|
| PFTs (FEV1, FVC, TLC) | Establish decline, classify phenotype |
| HRCT chest | Identify bronchiectasis, hyperinflation (BOS) or fibrotic opacities (RAS) |
| Bronchoscopy + BAL | Rule out infection; assess for rejection |
| Immunosuppression levels | Ensure therapeutic tacrolimus/cyclosporine levels |
| Infection screening | CMV PCR, respiratory viral panel, fungal cultures |

---

## 8. Management

### Initial Assessment
- Rule out infection, rejection, anastomotic complications
- Optimize immunosuppression levels
- Consider switching cyclosporine → tacrolimus if on cyclosporine

### Azithromycin (First-Line for BOS)
- **250 mg daily × 5 days**, then **250 mg three times weekly**
- Most effective in neutrophilic-predominant BAL (NRAD phenotype — neutrophilic reversible allograft dysfunction)
- Mechanism: anti-inflammatory + immunomodulatory, not purely antimicrobial

### Progressive BOS — Additional Options

| Therapy | Evidence Level | Notes |
|---------|---------------|-------|
| Montelukast | Limited (small studies) | Leukotriene receptor antagonist; may slow FEV1 decline |
| mTOR inhibitors (sirolimus, everolimus) | Limited | Calcineurin-independent immunosuppression; some BOS slowing reported |
| Extracorporeal photopheresis (ECP) | Single-center data | Reduced rate of FEV1 decline reported |
| Total lymphoid irradiation | Small observational only | Some improvement in decline rate |
| Aerosolized cyclosporine or tacrolimus | Preliminary case reports | Not standard practice |
| Anti-lymphocyte/thymocyte therapy | 3-month benefit only | All patients eventually progressed |

### Less Established / Ineffective Therapies
- **Systemic glucocorticoids:** Not beneficial in BOS unless biopsy demonstrates lymphocytic bronchiolitis or eosinophilic airway infiltration
- **Alemtuzumab:** No difference in overall survival

### Surgical
- **Retransplantation:** For refractory CLAD 3–4 in appropriate candidates

---

## 9. Prognosis

- Mortality rate: **25–55%** depending on phenotype and stage
- RAS generally carries worse prognosis than BOS
- Regular spirometry monitoring is essential for early detection and staging

---

## 10. Future Directions (Investigational)

Trials ongoing for: belumosudil, belatacept, plasmapheresis, antifibrotic agents (nintedanib, pirfenidone in RAS).

Emerging biomarkers under study: donor-derived cell-free DNA, exhaled nitric oxide, BAL neutrophilia with IL-12/IL-17, circulating fibrocytes.
