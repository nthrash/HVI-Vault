### Hyperammonemia After Lung Transplantation (HALT)

## Overview
Rare but often fatal complication: elevated serum ammonia → encephalopathy, cerebral edema, seizures, coma.

| Stat | Value |
|------|-------|
| Incidence | 0.99–4% of lung transplant recipients |
| Historical mortality | 57.5–75% |
| Mortality with early recognition | **~25%** |
| Time to peak ammonia | Mean 11–14 days post-transplant (range 1–45 days) |
| Normal ammonia | ≤ 60 μmol/L |

---
## Diagnostic Criteria
**≥ 3 of 4 criteria required:**
1. Ammonia > 60 μmol/L (or above local ULN)
2. No cirrhosis, liver failure, or prior liver transplant
3. Encephalopathy present
4. Specific treatment for hyperammonemia administered

---
## Clinical Presentation
- Altered mental status / encephalopathy *(most common)*
- Confusion, agitation
- Seizures
- Coma
- Distributive shock (severe cases)
- PRES

> Some patients are **asymptomatic** — identified only by routine screening

---
## Proposed Etiologies

**1. Glutamine Synthetase (GS) Deficiency** ⭐ Most supported
- GS normally detoxifies ~35% of ammonia
- Near-complete loss of hepatic GS expression documented
- Amino acid profiles typically **normal** (unlike urea cycle disorders)

**2. Ureaplasma/Mycoplasma Infection**
- Urea-splitting organisms → excess ammonia
- PCR often **negative** despite empiric treatment

**3. Urea Cycle Disorders** *(less likely)*
- Normal amino acid profiles argue against this
- Normal citrulline, arginine, ornithine, glutamine
- Normal/negative urinary orotic acid

**4. Medication-Induced**
- Calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus, cyclosporine)
- Valproic acid (inhibits carbamoyl phosphate synthase)

---
## Screening

> **Early detection dramatically improves survival**

- Daily ammonia levels × first week post-transplant
- Check ammonia with **any** mental status change
- Specimen handling: place on ice immediately, process within 15 minutes

---
## Management Algorithm

**Ammonia > 60 μmol/L + Encephalopathy → HALT Suspected**

### 🔴 Immediate (Within 1–2 Hours)

**Step 1 — Stop all protein intake (24–48h)**
- IV dextrose 10% (continuous)
- IV lipid emulsion
- Micronutrients (see below)

**Step 2 — Antimicrobials** *(empiric; continue regardless of PCR)*

| Agent | Dose |
|-------|------|
| Levofloxacin | 750 mg IV/PO daily |
| Azithromycin | 500 mg IV/PO daily |
| Doxycycline | 100 mg IV/PO BID |

**Step 3 — Bowel Decontamination**

| Agent | Dose | Purpose |
|-------|------|---------|
| Lactulose | 15–30 mL PO/NG Q6–8H | Acidify colon, reduce ammonia absorption |
| Rifaximin | 550 mg PO BID–TID | Reduce ammonia-producing gut bacteria |
| Metronidazole | 500 mg IV/PO Q8H | Anaerobic coverage |
| Neomycin | 500–1000 mg PO Q6H | Alternative |

**Step 4 — Ammonia Scavengers** ⭐ Key intervention

*Oral/enteral (preferred):*

| Agent | Dose | Mechanism |
|-------|------|-----------|
| Sodium phenylbutyrate | 5.5 g/m² PO divided BID–QID | → phenylacetylglutamine (renal excretion) |
| Sodium benzoate | 5.5 g/m² PO divided BID–QID | → hippurate (renal excretion) |

> Can use **both simultaneously** if needed

*IV (if unable to take PO):*
- Sodium phenylacetate + sodium benzoate: 5.5 g/m² IV over 90–120 min, then continuous infusion

**Step 5 — Arginine + Dextrose + Micronutrients**
- Arginine HCl: 200–600 mg/kg/day IV
- Dextrose 10%: continuous infusion
- L-Carnitine: 50–100 mg/kg/day IV
- Vitamin B complex, Vitamin C, Magnesium, Zinc, Selenium

**Step 6 — Send Labs**
- Plasma amino acids
- Urinary orotic acid
- Ureaplasma / Mycoplasma PCR (blood, BAL)
- Bacterial and fungal cultures

---
### Check Ammonia Q6–12H

**If decreasing:**
- Continue therapy
- Monitor Q6–12H
- Reintroduce protein at 48h starting at **0.25 g/kg/day**
- Increase by 0.25 g/kg/day; target **1.5–2.0 g/kg/day**

**If not decreasing → Escalate:**

### 🟡 Escalation — Renal Replacement Therapy

**Indications:**
- Ammonia > 180 μmol/L (> 3× ULN) despite medical management
- Rapidly rising ammonia
- Symptomatic hyperammonemia

| Situation | RRT Choice | Parameters |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Hemodynamically **stable** | **Intermittent HD (iHD)** | 6h daily; blood flow 400 mL/min; dialysate 800 mL/min |
| Hemodynamically **unstable** | **CVVHD** | 40–50 mL/kg/h; add iHD between sessions |
| **Refractory** | **iHD + CVVHD** | iHD 6h daily; CVVHD between sessions |

> iHD is **more efficient** than CRRT for ammonia clearance

---
## Diagnostic Workup

**Plasma Amino Acids:**

| Test | Normal | Interpretation |
|------|--------|----------------|
| Citrulline | 10–60 μmol/L | Low in urea cycle defects |
| Arginine | 40–160 μmol/L | Low in urea cycle defects |
| Ornithine | 20–135 μmol/L | Elevated in some UCDs |
| Glutamine | 410–700 μmol/L | **Elevated in UCDs; normal/low in HALT** |
| Urinary orotic acid | 0.2–1.5 mmol/mol Cr | Elevated in OTC deficiency |

> **Normal profile = less likely urea cycle disorder**

## Monitoring During Treatment

| Parameter | Frequency | Target |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| Ammonia | Q6–12H → daily | ≤ 60 μmol/L |
| Mental status | Continuous | Alert, oriented |
| Electrolytes | Daily | K⁺ 3.5–5.0, Mg > 2.0 |
| Renal function | Daily | Baseline |
| Liver function | Daily | Monitor for hepatotoxicity |
| ABG | PRN | pH > 7.35 |

---
## Prognosis

| Era | Mortality | Key Factor |
|-----|-----------|------------|
| Literature review (1995–2020) | 57.5% | Late recognition, delayed treatment |
| University of Florida (2016–2018) | **25%** | Early recognition + multimodal protocol |

**Predictors of better outcome:**
- Early diagnosis (routine screening)
- Prompt multimodal therapy
- Lower peak ammonia
- Shorter time to peak

---
## Clinical Pearls

**Do:**
- Screen ALL lung transplant recipients daily × first week
- Check ammonia with ANY mental status change
- Start treatment early — don't wait for confirmatory tests
- Use multimodal approach from the start
- Prefer **oral scavengers** over IV (equally effective, >90% cost savings)
- Use both sodium phenylbutyrate AND sodium benzoate if needed
- Prefer **intermittent HD over CRRT** if hemodynamically stable
- Use extended HD sessions (6 hours > standard 4 hours)

**Don't:**
- Delay treatment waiting for Ureaplasma/Mycoplasma PCR
- Assume normal amino acids rule out HALT (they're typically normal)
- Continue protein feeding once diagnosis suspected
- Rely on CRRT alone if patient can tolerate iHD
