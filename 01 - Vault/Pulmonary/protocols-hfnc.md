# High Flow Nasal Cannula (HFNC)

---

## 1. How It Works

HFNC delivers **heated, humidified** oxygen at high flow rates (20–60 LPM), with FiO₂ controlled by blending 100% wall oxygen with room air.

| Mechanism | Effect |
|-----------|--------|
| **Reduces air entrainment** | Unlike conventional NC or NRB — actual FiO₂ can exceed 90% |
| **Modest positive pressure** | Oropharyngeal pressure 2–6 cmH₂O (mouth closed); 0–1 cmH₂O (mouth open); ~2 cmH₂O transmitted to trachea — flow-dependent, peaks at end-expiration |
| **↑ Functional residual capacity (FRC)** | Increases FRC ~25% → improved alveolar recruitment |
| **CO₂ washout** | Anatomic dead space flushed with fresh gas → ↓ work of breathing |
| **Conditioned air** | 37°C at 100% humidity → reduces the metabolic cost of air conditioning |

---

## 2. Indications

| Indication | Notes |
|------------|-------|
| Acute hypoxemic respiratory failure | First-line or bridge before escalation |
| Post-extubation support | Reduces reintubation vs conventional oxygen in high-risk patients |
| Pre-oxygenation for intubation | Superior to NRB for apneic oxygenation extension |
| Step-down from NIPPV | When BiPAP no longer needed but O₂ support still required |

**Prefer NIPPV (BiPAP/CPAP) over HFNC for:** COPD exacerbation, cardiogenic pulmonary edema, hypercapnic respiratory failure, hemodynamic shock. These conditions represent the majority of HFNC failures in trials.

---

## 3. Settings & Titration

| Parameter | Recommendation |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Flow rate** | Start at **60 LPM** — patients tolerate well; titrate down as tolerated |
| **FiO₂** | Titrate to **SpO₂ 90–94%** — avoid targeting 100% (O₂ toxicity) |
| **Temperature** | 37°C standard |

---

## 4. Monitoring & Reassessment

Reassess at **2–12 hours** after HFNC initiation:

| Parameter | Action if Worsening |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Respiratory rate | ↑ RR is earliest sign of failure |
| Breathing pattern | Thoracoabdominal dyssynchrony = failure predictor |
| PaO₂ or SpO₂ | Drop from baseline at 2h = failure predictor |
| **ROX Index** | SpO₂/FiO₂ ÷ RR — **<4.88 at 12 hours** = high risk of failure → escalate |

**ROX Index Formula:**
```
ROX = (SpO₂ ÷ FiO₂) ÷ Respiratory Rate
```
ROX <4.88 at 12h → consider early intubation

---

## 5. Clinical Evidence

| Trial | Key Finding |
|-------|------------|
| **FLORALI (NEJM 2015)** | 310 patients: HFNC vs standard O₂ vs NIPPV; intubation rates 38% vs 47% vs 50% (p=0.18, NS); 90-day mortality HR 2.01 favoring HFNC vs standard O₂ (p=0.046). Post-hoc: benefit most pronounced in PaO₂/FiO₂ <200 |
| **AJRCCM 2014 (Maggiore et al.)** | Post-extubation: HFNC vs Venturi mask → reintubation 8% vs 35% (p=0.001); better P:F ratio, fewer desaturations |
| **JAMA 2016 (Hernández et al.)** | Low-risk post-extubation patients: HFNC 24h after extubation → reintubation 5% vs 12% at 72h (p=0.04) |
| **Early vs Late application (ICM 2015)** | HFNC applied <48h: mortality 39% vs 67% when applied >48h — early application associated with better outcomes |

---

## 6. Predicting Failure (~25–30% of patients fail HFNC)

| Predictor at 2 Hours | Significance |
|---------------------|-------------|
| ↑ Respiratory rate | Independent predictor |
| Thoracoabdominal dyssynchrony | Independent predictor |
| Drop in PaO₂ from baseline | Independent predictor |
| ROX index <4.88 at 12h | Validated failure predictor — escalate |

⚠️ **Beware of prolonged HFNC at 60 LPM / 100% FiO₂** — patients left on maximal settings while deteriorating tend to desaturate severely during intubation. Don't delay intubation.

---

## 7. Post-Extubation Protocol

- All patients: Consider HFNC 60 LPM immediately post-extubation
- High-risk patients (obese, prolonged intubation, COPD, CHF): HFNC superior to standard O₂
- Low-risk patients (simple weaning, <65 yo, no CHF/COPD): HFNC for ≥24h reduces reintubation
