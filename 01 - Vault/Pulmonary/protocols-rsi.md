# Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI)

---

## 1. The 9 Ps of RSI

| Step | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Plan** | Decide on approach, equipment, backup strategies before starting |
| **Preparation** | Drugs, equipment, people, place |
| **Protect cervical spine** | MILS if indicated (trauma) |
| **Positioning** | Optimal head/neck position; ramp obese patients |
| **Pre-oxygenation** | Minimum 3–5 min NRB 15 LPM; HFNC preferred if available |
| **Pretreatment** | Optional agents to blunt physiologic response (see below) |
| **Paralysis & Induction** | Simultaneous administration of induction agent + paralytic |
| **Placement with proof** | EtCO₂ confirmation; chest rise; auscultation |
| **Post-intubation management** | Sedation, analgesia, hemodynamic support, vent settings |

*Cricoid pressure (10th P) is optional and controversial — may worsen laryngoscopic view.*

---

## 2. Team Roles

**Minimum 3 people:**

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|---------------|
| Airway proceduralist | Performs laryngoscopy and intubation |
| Airway assistant | Applies BURP, holds head, passes equipment |
| Drug administrator | Pushes induction agent and paralytic on command |

Additional roles: MILS provider, scribe, cricoid pressure (if used).

*In failed airway: clearly communicate role re-allocation to team.*

---

## 3. Pre-RSI Setup — SOAPME

| Letter | Component |
|--------|-----------|
| **S** | Suction — Yankauer, hooked up, max suction, place between mattress and bed rail |
| **O** | Oxygen — NRB + BVM at 15 LPM; nasal prongs for apneic oxygenation during laryngoscopy |
| **A** | Airways — ETT × 2 (7.5 most adults; 7.0 smaller females; 8.0 larger males), stylet curved 30–35° at cuff, Mac 3/4 blade, video laryngoscope, LMA, bougie |
| **P** | Pre-oxygenate — 15 LPM NRB × 3–5 min; apneic oxygenation via nasal prongs at 15 LPM during attempt |
| **M** | Monitoring + Medications — cardiac monitor, pulse ox, BP cuff; all drugs drawn and labeled |
| **E** | End-tidal CO₂ — colorimetric or waveform capnography ready before intubation |

## 4. Induction Agents

*All doses IV unless specified. Adjust downward in hemodynamic instability.*

| Agent | Dose | Onset | Duration | Best Use | Key Drawbacks |
|-------|------|-------|----------|----------|---------------|
| **Ketamine** | 1.5–2 mg/kg IBW (4 mg/kg IM) | 60–90 sec | 10–20 min | **Preferred in hemodynamic instability**; reactive airway disease (bronchodilation); any RSI | ↑ Secretions; caution in severe CAD (↑ HR/BP); ↑ IOP; laryngospasm (rare). *Note: does NOT raise ICP despite historical teaching* |
| **Etomidate** | 0.3 mg/kg TBW | 10–15 sec | 5–10 min | Hemodynamically unstable; most situations except sepsis/seizures | Adrenal suppression; myoclonus; pain on injection. *Recent meta-analyses suggest safer in sepsis than originally thought* |
| **Propofol** | 1–2.5 mg/kg IBW + (0.4 × TBW) | 15–45 sec | 5–10 min | Hemodynamically stable; reactive airway; status epilepticus | Hypotension; myocardial depression; ↓ cerebral perfusion; pain on injection; variable response |
| **Fentanyl** | 2–10 mcg/kg TBW | <60 sec (peak ~5 min) | Dose-dependent | Low-dose sympatholytic premedication (TBI, SAH, vascular); modified RSI in cardiogenic shock | Respiratory depression; ↓ BP; bradycardia; chest wall rigidity at high doses |
| **Midazolam** | 0.1–0.3 mg/kg TBW | 60–90 sec | 15–30 min | Not recommended as primary agent; occasionally low-dose adjunct in shocked patients | Respiratory depression; hypotension; variable/slow onset; paradoxical agitation |
| **Thiopental** | 3–5 mg/kg TBW | 30–45 sec | 5–10 min | Hemodynamically stable; status epilepticus | Histamine release; myocardial depression; vasodilation; contraindicated in porphyria; **never** inject intra-arterially |

## 5. Neuromuscular Blocking Agents (Paralytics)

| Agent | Dose | Onset | Duration | Notes |
|-------|------|-------|----------|-------|
| **Rocuronium** ⭐ *First-line* | 1.2–1.6 mg/kg IBW (up to 2.0 mg/kg at some centers) | 60 sec | 30–60 min | Reversible with **sugammadex**; fewer contraindications than succinylcholine; ideal in CICV situations (patient remains immobile for cric); no fasciculations → ↓ O₂ consumption |
| **Succinylcholine** | 1.5 mg/kg TBW (2 mg/kg if myasthenia gravis; 4 mg/kg IM in extremis) | 45–60 sec | 6–10 min | Short duration useful for neurologic assessment or elective extubation; **multiple contraindications** (see below) |
| **Vecuronium** | 0.15 mg/kg IBW (0.01 mg/kg priming dose 3 min prior optional) | 120–180 sec | 45–60 min | Not recommended for RSI unless succinylcholine and rocuronium contraindicated; reversible with sugammadex; slow onset |

### Succinylcholine Contraindications
- Hyperkalemia (or risk of hyperkalemia)
- Burns or crush injury **>5 days** after event
- Denervation injury / prolonged immobility >5 days
- Neuromuscular disorders (muscular dystrophy, myopathy)
- Malignant hyperthermia history or susceptibility
- Personal/family history of pseudocholinesterase deficiency
- Elevated intraocular pressure (relative)

### Sugammadex Reversal of Rocuronium
| Degree of Block | Sugammadex Dose |
|----------------|----------------|
| Routine reversal | 2–4 mg/kg IV |
| Immediate reversal (RSI dose rocuronium) | **16 mg/kg IV** |

## 6. Pre-Treatment (Optional)

| Agent | Dose | Indication | Purpose |
|-------|------|-----------|---------|
| Fentanyl | 1–3 mcg/kg IV (3 min before induction) | TBI, SAH, aortic aneurysm, HTN emergency | Blunt sympathetic response to laryngoscopy |
| Lidocaine | 1.5 mg/kg IV (3 min before) | TBI, reactive airway | Attenuate ICP rise and airway reactivity (limited evidence) |
| Atropine | 0.02 mg/kg IV | Pediatric RSI; bradycardia risk | Prevent succinylcholine-induced bradycardia |

---

## 7. Post-Intubation Management

- Confirm tube position: **waveform EtCO₂** (gold standard) + chest rise + bilateral breath sounds
- Chest X-ray to confirm depth
- Start sedation + analgesia infusion immediately
- Hemodynamic support if post-intubation hypotension (push-dose epinephrine 10–20 mcg or phenylephrine 100–200 mcg)
- Set ventilator: initial settings per clinical context (ARDS protocol, COPD protocol, or standard)
- Document tube depth at teeth/lip
