## Overview & Clinical Significance

> [!tip] **Why This Matters in the Cardiac ICU**
> **Tricuspid regurgitation (TR) is the most common valvular lesion in the cardiac ICU**, yet remains historically undertreated. In the right-heart-focused ICU caring for advanced heart failure, pulmonary hypertension, RV failure, LVAD, and post-cardiac surgery patients, **the burden of TR is a critical determinant of outcomes**. Unlike left-sided valve disease, functional TR often improves when the underlying cause (pulmonary hypertension, RV dilation) is addressed—making it fundamentally different from organic mitral or aortic disease. However, severe untreated TR perpetuates the vicious cycle of RV dilation, annular stretching, and progressive RV dysfunction, leading to hemodynamic collapse, multiorgan failure from systemic venous congestion, and death.

**Tricuspid valve disease** encompasses a spectrum of pathology:
- **Tricuspid regurgitation (TR)**: Backflow of blood from the right ventricle into the right atrium during systole
  - **Primary TR**: Organic valve pathology (leaflet deformity, endocarditis, carcinoid)
  - **Functional/Secondary TR**: Annular dilation and RV dysfunction with normal valve leaflets; caused by RV/RA pressure or volume overload
- **Tricuspid stenosis (TS)**: Fixed obstruction to RV filling; uncommon and usually occurs with mitral stenosis

## 1. Etiology & Risk Factors

### Primary (Organic) Tricuspid Regurgitation

**Organic valve pathology affecting leaflet coaptation:**

| Etiology | Key Features | ICU Relevance |
|----------|--------------|---------------|
| **Infective endocarditis (IE)** | IV drug use (most common in ICU), septic embolism to tricuspid leaflets, perforation, vegetation | High mortality if untreated; may require emergency TV surgery; hemodynamic instability from sepsis + severe TR |
| **Carcinoid syndrome** | Plaque-like thickening of leaflets → contracture, fixed TR; right-sided valves only (serotonin produces endocardial fibrosis) | Carcinoid crisis intraop; workup with 24-hr urine 5-HIAA; consider somatostatin analogs preop |
| **Rheumatic disease** | Commissural fusion, leaflet thickening; almost always accompanied by mitral disease | Rare in developed countries; consider in immigrant populations |
| **Radiation** | History of mediastinal/chest radiation (breast cancer, lymphoma); fibrosis of leaflets | Delayed presentation years after exposure; often combined with pericardial disease |
| **Ebstein's anomaly** | Congenital: downward displacement of septal leaflet → apical translation, atrialization of RV | Cyanosis, arrhythmias (WPW), RV dysfunction; may present acutely with decompensation |
| **Pacemaker/ICD lead trauma** | Perforating lead through leaflet, entrapment, friction-induced damage | Increasingly common; consider in any patient with PM/ICD and new severe TR; lead extraction may help |
| **Traumatic** | Blunt chest trauma, iatrogenic during cardiac procedures | Acute presentation; assess for RV infarction (inferior MI) |

---

### Functional (Secondary) Tricuspid Regurgitation — **Most Common in the ICU**

**Normal valve leaflets but severe annular dilation and/or RV dysfunction from pressure/volume overload:**

| Primary Cause | Mechanism | ICU Context |
|---------------|-----------|------------|
| **Pulmonary hypertension** | Elevated RVP → RV dilation → annular dilation → leaflet tethering | Most common cause in the HVI ICU; can be group 1 (PAH), 2 (PH-LHD), 3 (hypoxic), 4 (thrombotic), or 5 |
| **Left-sided heart failure** | Mitral stenosis, severe MR, AS, dilated cardiomyopathy → LA/LV dysfunction → pulmonary HTN → RV strain → RV dilation → TR | Fixing mitral stenosis or reducing MR can improve TR |
| **RV infarction** | Inferior MI with RV involvement → RV dysfunction, dilation, elevated RA → TR | Acute presentation; reperfusion may improve RV function and TR |
| **Post-cardiac surgery** | Annular dilation from CPB, myocardial injury, increased RV afterload | Present in recovery; may improve with time and RV-PA coupling optimization |
| **Chronic lung disease** | COPD, ILD, OSA → chronic hypoxia → pulmonary vasoconstriction → pulmonary HTN → RV failure → TR | Progressive; treat underlying lung disease |
| **LVAD implantation** | LVAD unloads LV → increased systemic venous return to right atrium; upward IVS shift → RV dilation and altered geometry | **Critical ICU issue**: LVAD-induced TR worsens outcomes; consider TV repair at time of LVAD implant if severe TR |

---

### Tricuspid Stenosis — **Rare, Usually with Mitral Stenosis**

| Etiology | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| **Rheumatic disease** | ~90% of TS cases; virtually always associated with mitral stenosis and/or aortic valve disease |
| **Carcinoid syndrome** | Carcinoid plaque extends to tricuspid valve, causing both TS and TR |
| **Congenital** | Rare; associated with other anomalies (ASD, VSD) |
| **Pacemaker lead obstruction** | Paradoxical: lead can obstruct RV inflow creating functional TS |

## 2. Pathophysiology

### Tricuspid Regurgitation: The Vicious Cycle

```
RV volume overload (from TR backflow)
         ↓
   RV dilation
         ↓
TV annular dilation
         ↓
Worsening leaflet tethering
         ↓
More severe TR
         ↓
[CYCLE REPEATS—> RV failure, systemic congestion, death]
```

**Hemodynamic Consequences:**

1. **Increased RV afterload & volume overload**
   - Regurgitant fraction increases RV stroke volume demand
   - RV wall stress increases (Laplace law: σ = Pr/2h)
   - RV oxygen consumption rises

2. **RV dilation & geometric distortion**
   - Annular dilation (normal tricuspid annulus ~25 mm; pathologic >40 mm in systole)
   - Leaflet tethering: increased interpapillary distance, decreased coaptation
   - D-shaped septum from RV pressure → septal shift → LV underfilling (ventricular interdependence)
   - Loss of RV longitudinal function (TAPSE decreases)

3. **Elevated right atrial (RA) pressure & systemic venous congestion**
   - RA acts as a "compliant dump" for regurgitant flow
   - Elevated CVP (RA pressure >8–10 mmHg becomes pathologic)
   - **Hepatic congestion**: elevated hepatic venous pressure causes hepatomegaly, ischemic hepatitis, hepatic dysfunction
   - **Renal venous hypertension**: splanchnic congestion, reduced renal perfusion pressure, worsened renal function
   - **Peripheral edema & ascites**: fluid transudation into interstitial and peritoneal spaces
   - **Reduced cardiac output**: despite increased RV volume, LV filling is compromised → systemic hypoperfusion

4. **RV-PA coupling deterioration**
   - RV systolic function decline (EF, strain) outpaces pulmonary afterload reduction
   - Negative slope of RV-PA coupling curve → hemodynamic instability

---

### Distinction: Functional vs. Primary Tricuspid Regurgitation

> [!warning] **Critical ICU Pearl**
> **Functional TR caused by RV pressure/volume overload may IMPROVE or RESOLVE when the underlying cause is treated** (e.g., weaning pulmonary HTN therapy, optimizing LV function, reducing mechanical ventilation-induced afterload). This is fundamentally different from organic primary TR, which requires surgical intervention. Always ask: "What is causing the RV to fail?" rather than reflexively pursuing TV surgery.

---

### Tricuspid Stenosis: Fixed Obstruction

**Pathophysiology:**

1. **Fixed RV inflow obstruction**
   - Reduced diastolic filling of RV
   - Elevated RA pressure (compensatory)
   - RA dilation, atrial fibrillation

2. **Impaired cardiac output**
   - CO cannot increase with demand (fixed by valve obstruction)
   - Exercise intolerance, dyspnea on exertion
   - In ICU: unstable hemodynamics, reduced ability to respond to physiologic stress

3. **Systemic venous congestion** (similar to TR but due to elevated RA pressure, not regurgitation)
   - Hepatomegaly, ascites, peripheral edema
   - Hepatic and renal dysfunction from chronic venous congestion

---

### ICU-Specific Pathophysiology: RV Failure + Severe TR

**Critical concepts for cardiac ICU management:**

1. **RV-PA coupling & mechanical ventilation**
   - Positive pressure ventilation increases RV afterload (reduces venous return, compresses pulmonary vessels)
   - PEEP >10 cmH₂O significantly impairs RV function in patients with baseline RV dysfunction
   - Hypoxia, hypercarbia, acidosis → pulmonary vasoconstriction → increased RVP → worsened RV failure

2. **Preload dependence**
   - RV is highly preload-dependent (Frank-Starling curve operates on steeper slope)
   - Excessive diuresis in severe TR can precipitate acute RV failure and cardiogenic shock
   - CVP alone is misleading in functional TR; assess RV function (TAPSE, FAC, echo strain)

3. **Septal mechanics & LV function**
   - D-shaped septum reduces LV diastolic compliance
   - LV underfilling reduces LV stroke volume and systemic cardiac output
   - This is why β-blockers and negative inotropes may worsen hemodynamics in acute RV failure with TR

4. **LVAD & TR interaction**
   - LVAD unloads LV → IVS shifts rightward → RV geometry worsens
   - Increased systemic venous return to RA from LVAD → increased RV filling → TR worsens
   - **LVAD patients with severe TR have significantly worse long-term outcomes**
   - Consider TV repair at time of LVAD implant if ≥moderate-severe TR

## 3. Clinical Presentation

### Signs & Symptoms of Tricuspid Regurgitation

| Clinical Finding | Mechanism | ICU Significance |
|------------------|-----------|------------------|
| **Fatigue, weakness** | Reduced systemic cardiac output; hepatic congestion impairs metabolism | May be sole presenting symptom in chronic TR |
| **Dyspnea on exertion** | RV failure, pulmonary HTN, LV underfilling from D-shaped septum | Orthopnea less common than in LV failure |
| **Lower extremity edema** | Systemic venous congestion, elevated hydrostatic pressure | Often symmetrical; may precede ascites |
| **Ascites** | Hepatic venous congestion, reduced synthetic function, protein malnutrition | Indicates chronic elevated CVP; tense ascites worsens RV function (increased afterload) |
| **Hepatomegaly, pulsatile liver** | RA dilation from TR; pulsatile means hepatic vein reversal with regurgitant flow | Hepatic pulse pathognomonic for severe TR |
| **Peripheral edema** | Venous hypertension + low oncotic pressure (if hepatic dysfunction, proteinuria) | Masked in ICU by fluid resuscitation |
| **Jugular venous distension (JVD)** | Elevated RA pressure | Assess JVP carefully; may be difficult in mechanically ventilated patients |
| **Prominent CV waves in neck veins** | Large regurgitant V wave from TR backflow | S wave (x descent) may be prominent too |
| **Kussmaul's sign** | Paradoxical rise in JVP with inspiration (stiff, noncompliant RV) | Seen in constrictive physiology or severe RV failure with TR |

---

### Cardiac Auscultation

**Holosystolic (pansystolic) murmur:**
- Location: **left lower sternal border** (4th–5th intercostal space)
- Quality: Low-pitched, blowing (heard best with diaphragm)
- **Increases with inspiration (Carvallo's sign)**: physiologic consequence of increased venous return to RV during inspiration
- May be soft or inaudible if RA pressure is very high (no pressure gradient for flow to generate sound) or if CO is very low

---

### History & Risk Factor Pearls

- **IV drug use**: Screen for endocarditis; assess for stigmata (track marks, skin infections, septic thrombophlebitis)
- **Pacemaker/ICD history**: When did device placed? Any complications? New TR could be lead-related
- **Carcinoid syndrome**: Ask about flushing, diarrhea, wheezing; family history of neuroendocrine tumors
- **Mediastinal radiation**: History of Hodgkin lymphoma, breast cancer, or lymphoma treated in past decades
- **Recent cardiac surgery**: Is TR new post-op or progressive? (Functional TR from post-CPB RV dysfunction improves in weeks to months)

---

### Red Flags & Acute Presentations in the ICU

1. **RV failure + severe TR in LVAD patient**: Hemodynamic instability despite inotropic support; worsening renal function; may need escalation to mechanical support
2. **Carcinoid crisis**: Sudden hypotension, flushing, bronchospasm; life-threatening; requires aggressive vasopressor support and somatostatin analogs
3. **Severe TR + pulmonary hypertension crisis**: Acute RV pressure spike → RV ischemia → sudden RV failure; manage with iNO, inhaled prostacyclins, vasopressors
4. **Acute TR from pacemaker lead perforation**: Hemoptysis, paradoxical embolism risk, acute right heart failure
5. **Septic tricuspiditis** (IE): Fever, new murmur, septic emboli to lungs (hemoptysis); requires broad-spectrum antibiotics and possible urgent TV surgery

## 4. Diagnostic Workup

### Echocardiography: Gold Standard for TR Assessment

**Transthoracic echo (TTE)** is first-line; **transesophageal echo (TEE)** improves image quality in mechanically ventilated or post-op patients.

#### Tricuspid Regurgitation Severity Grading

| Parameter | Trace/Mild | Moderate | Moderate-Severe | Severe |
|-----------|-----------|----------|-----------------|--------|
| **Vena contracta (VC)** | <3 mm | 3–6 mm | 6–8 mm | >8 mm |
| **PISA radius** | <0.5 cm | 0.5–0.75 cm | 0.75–1.1 cm | >1.1 cm |
| **Regurgitant volume** | <45 mL | 45–89 mL | 90–119 mL | ≥120 mL |
| **RA/RV size** | Normal | Mildly dilated | Moderately dilated | Severely dilated |
| **Hepatic vein flow** | Systolic dominant | Blunted systole | Reversal in systole | Systolic reversal ≥30 cm/s |
| **IVC diameter & collapse** | <21 mm, >50% collapse | Mildly dilated, moderate collapse | 21–27 mm, minimal collapse | >27 mm, <50% collapse |

> [!tip] **Integrated Assessment**
> Use **multiparameter grading**: no single measurement (VC, PISA, regurgitant volume) is 100% accurate. **Severe TR = large VC + elevated RA/RV + systolic hepatic vein reversal + IVC dilation.**

---

### Right Ventricular Function Assessment

| Parameter | Measurement | Normal | RV Dysfunction |
|-----------|-------------|--------|-----------------|
| **TAPSE** | Tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (M-mode) | >16 mm | <16 mm = impaired |
| **RV S'** | Tissue Doppler at tricuspid annulus (TDI) | >10 cm/s | <10 cm/s |
| **FAC** | RV fractional area change (RV area change from ED to ES) | >35% | <35% |
| **RV free wall strain** | Speckle tracking (negative value in systole) | <-20% | -10 to -20% = mildly impaired; >-10% = severely impaired |
| **RV GLS** | Global longitudinal strain (all segments) | <-20% | >-10 to -20% = moderate impairment |

> [!warning] **ICU Caveat**
> TAPSE is load-dependent; interpret cautiously in patients on high vasopressor/inotrope doses or with severe pulmonary HTN (afterload may artificially depress TAPSE). 3D RV echo and strain imaging are more robust in critical illness but require good image quality.

---

### Pulmonary Artery Catheter (Swan-Ganz) Findings in Severe TR

| Finding | Significance |
|---------|-------------|
| **Large CV waves** | Systolic RA pressure wave elevated; >8–10 mmHg suggests severe TR |
| **Rapid Y descent** | Quick drop after regurgitant phase; "W" or "M" shaped (combining V and Y) |
| **Elevated CVP** | RA pressure >12 mmHg at rest, >15 mmHg with volume challenge |
| **Kussmaul's sign** | Paradoxical rise in CVP with inspiration = poor RV compliance; ominous sign |
| **Reduced cardiac output** | CO may be low despite elevated filling pressures (RV cannot augment CO with volume) |
| **Elevated V wave** | Can exceed systolic PA pressure when TR is severe |

> [!tip] **Controversy: PAC Use in Cardiac ICU**
> According to PubMed, Brown et al. (2021) found that **PAC use in cardiac surgery (including tricuspid insufficiency subgroup) was not associated with improved operative mortality compared to CVP monitoring alone** (DOI: 10.1016/j.jtcvs.2021.01.086). PAC may provide useful hemodynamic data in complex cases (e.g., RV-PA uncoupling with severe TR) but should not be routine. Prioritize **clinical exam, echocardiography, and lactate trends** for real-time assessment.

---

### Additional Diagnostic Testing

| Test | Indication | What It Shows |
|------|-----------|---------------|
| **Cardiac CT** | Surgical planning, assess RV size and geometry, pulmonary artery morphology | Better spatial resolution than echo for annular size, branch PA anatomy |
| **Cardiac MRI** | RV function quantification, assess for myocardial scar/fibrosis | 3D imaging of RV; useful for risk stratification |
| **24-hr urine 5-HIAA** | Suspected carcinoid syndrome | Elevated in carcinoid; ~10× normal = active carcinoid |
| **Plasma chromogranin A** | Carcinoid workup | Nonspecific but elevated in neuroendocrine tumors |
| **Chest X-ray** | Assess for carcinoid lung deposits, RV enlargement signs | May show RV enlargement (straight or convex left heart border) |
| **ECG** | Baseline, assess for arrhythmias | May show right axis deviation, RV strain pattern; atrial fibrillation common |

## 5. Clinical Pearls for the HVI Cardiac ICU

> [!tip] **Pearl #1: TR Causes RV Failure; RV Failure Causes TR—The Vicious Cycle**
> This bidirectional relationship means that improving RV function (weaning mechanical ventilation, optimizing pulmonary HTN, enhancing RV contractility) may improve functional TR. Don't assume TR severity is static; reassess after changes in RV loading or contractility.

> [!warning] **Pearl #2: Diuresis Can Be Harmful in Severe TR**
> The RV is preload-dependent; excessive diuresis (trying to reduce JVD or ascites) can precipitate acute RV failure and cardiogenic shock in severe TR. Instead, use targeted diuretics at lower doses, optimize RV contractility with inotropes, and reduce pulmonary afterload with inhaled agents or PAH drugs. Monitor CVP, urine output, and lactate closely.

> [!tip] **Pearl #3: Fixing Left-Sided Disease May Resolve Functional TR**
> If a patient has mitral stenosis + functional TR, percutaneous mitral balloon valvuloplasty or surgical mitral valve repair can reduce pulmonary HTN and reverse RV dilation, improving or resolving TR. This is a distinct strategy from TV surgery and should be pursued first.

> [!tip] **Pearl #4: Carvallo's Sign (TR Murmur Increases with Inspiration)**
> This is the sine qua non of TR; use it clinically to distinguish from MR (which often decreases with inspiration due to decreased LV volume). Simple bedside maneuver; don't forget to assess it during rounds.

> [!warning] **Pearl #5: Carcinoid TR Always Affects the Right Side**
> Serotonin produced by carcinoid tumors causes endocardial fibrosis on the right side of the heart only (lung circulation filters serotonin; left side spared unless there's a patent foramen ovale or lung disease allowing bypass). Expect TV + PV disease; mitral and aortic valves usually normal.

> [!tip] **Pearl #6: Pacemaker/ICD Lead–Related TR Is Increasingly Common**
> With over 3 million pacemakers implanted globally, lead-induced TR (from perforation, entrapment, friction) is becoming a leading cause of primary TR in the ICU. Always review device history; consider lead extraction or extraction + TV repair if severe TR develops post-device placement.

> [!warning] **Pearl #7: LVAD Patients with Severe TR Have Worse Outcomes**
> LVAD-induced RV dilation + increased systemic venous return → worsening TR. LVAD recipients with ≥moderate-severe TR should undergo concomitant TV repair (annuloplasty) at implant time. If TR develops or worsens post-LVAD, escalate to mechanical support (RVAD, ProtekDuo, VA-ECMO) early.

> [!tip] **Pearl #8: Mechanical Ventilation Settings Matter in RV Failure + TR**
> PEEP >10 cmH₂O, high plateau pressures, and hypoxia/hypercarbia all increase RV afterload. Lung-protective ventilation (Vt 6–8 mL/kg, PEEP 5–8 cmH₂O in most cases, pH goal >7.30, SpO₂ >90%) is essential. Consider early extubation or spontaneous breathing trials to reduce afterload. Permissive hypercarbia (pH 7.25–7.30) is acceptable if it reduces PEEP.

> [!warning] **Pearl #9: Renal Failure in Severe TR**
> Systemic venous congestion (elevated CVP, hepatic congestion) → renal venous HTN → reduced renal perfusion pressure → acute kidney injury (cardiorenal syndrome type 2). Diuretics alone won't help; require inotropic support, afterload reduction, and possibly mechanical support to restore cardiac output and renal perfusion.

> [!tip] **Pearl #10: Hepatic Dysfunction from Chronic Severe TR**
> Ischemic hepatitis from chronic venous congestion impairs hepatic metabolism and synthetic function. Expect elevated transaminases, bilirubin, and coagulopathy. IV drugs (pain meds, antibiotics) and oral medications will accumulate. Consider lower doses of sedatives, opioids, and antibiotics; monitor INR/PT closely; transfuse FFP cautiously (fluid overload worsens TR).

## 6. Management

### Medical Management of Tricuspid Regurgitation

#### General Principles

1. **Identify and treat the underlying cause**
   - Pulmonary HTN: PAH-specific drugs (phosphodiesterase inhibitors, endothelin antagonists, prostacyclins), oxygen therapy, diuretics
   - Left-sided heart disease: ACE-I/ARBs, β-blockers, diuretics for mitral stenosis or LV dysfunction; valve intervention if needed
   - RV infarction: reperfusion (PCI), avoid negative inotropes
   - Carcinoid: somatostatin analogs, consider chemotherapy
   - IE: antibiotics, possible TV surgery
   - LVAD: concomitant TV repair at implant

2. **Optimize RV function and reduce afterload**
   - Maintain sinus rhythm (A-fib worsens RV function); consider IV amiodarone or rate control
   - Avoid negative inotropes (β-blockers, CCBs) in acute RV failure; use cautiously in stable patients
   - Ensure adequate oxygenation and ventilation (avoid hypoxia, hypercarbia, acidosis)
   - Target HR 60–90 bpm; bradycardia reduces CO, tachycardia reduces filling time

3. **Fluid management** (nuanced in severe TR)
   - Severe TR with elevated CVP: cautious diuresis; target CVP 4–6 mmHg if possible, but risk acute RV collapse
   - Oliguria despite elevated CVP: true volume depletion from low CO + venous congestion (cardiorenal); may need inotropic support + low-dose inotrope (dobutamine) + vasopressor (norepinephrine) combination
   - Monitor: CVP, urine output, lactate, renal function, electrolytes q4–6h

---

### ICU Management of Acute RV Failure with Severe TR

#### Pharmacotherapy for RV Support

| Drug | Mechanism | Dose (IV) | Rationale in RV Failure + TR |
|------|-----------|-----------|------|
| **Norepinephrine (Levophed)** | α1 (vasoconstriction) + β1 (inotropy); maintains systemic/pulmonary perfusion pressure | 0.5–2.0 mcg/kg/min | First-line; maintains diastolic BP (RV perfusion pressure). Avoid dopamine (can cause tachycardia, arrhythmias) |
| **Milrinone** | Phosphodiesterase-3 inhibitor: inotropy + pulmonary vasodilation + lusitrope (improves diastolic function) | 0.25–0.75 mcg/kg/min | Excellent for RV failure; reduces afterload; improves diastolic compliance. Watch for systemic hypotension; add norepinephrine if needed |
| **Dobutamine** | β1 > α1; inotropy + systemic/pulmonary vasodilation | 2.5–10 mcg/kg/min | Can cause tachycardia, arrhythmias, systemic hypotension; use with caution. Milrinone often preferred |
| **Epinephrine** | α1 + β1; high-dose inotropy but more vasoconstriction | 0.1–0.5 mcg/kg/min | Reserve for refractory hypotension/cardiogenic shock; increases heart rate and arrhythmia risk |
| **Inhaled nitric oxide (iNO)** | Selective pulmonary vasodilator; reduces RV afterload; improves RV-PA coupling | 10–20 ppm | Gold standard for pulmonary HTN crisis; use with low-dose sildenafil to prevent rebound; requires ICU monitoring |
| **Inhaled epoprostenol (Flolan) or treprostinil** | Pulmonary vasodilation + antiplatelet; synergistic with iNO | Epoprostenol: 50 ng/kg/min ramp; treprostinil: 3–45 ng/kg/min | Bridge to long-term therapy; require special nebulizers; monitor platelet count |
| **IV milrinone + inhaled iNO** | Combination approach | Milrinone 0.25–0.5 mcg/kg/min + iNO 15 ppm | Superior RV function improvement vs. single agent; allows lower vasopressor doses |
| **Sildenafil (PDE-5 inhibitor)** | Oral pulmonary vasodilation; synergizes with iNO (prevents rebound when weaning iNO) | Oral: 20 mg TID; IV: 12.5 mg q6h | Chronic PAH therapy; useful during iNO weaning to prevent rebound hypertension |
| **Oxygen therapy** | Hypoxia → pulmonary vasoconstriction | SpO₂ target >90%, PaO₂ >60 mmHg | Essential; hyperoxia not beneficial but don't be hypoxic; assess A-a gradient |

---

#### Mechanical Ventilation Strategy in RV Failure + Severe TR

**Goal: Minimize RV afterload while maintaining oxygenation/ventilation.**

| Ventilator Parameter | Recommendation | Rationale |
|---------------------|-----------------|-----------|
| **PEEP** | 5–8 cmH₂O (avoid >10 unless necessary for oxygenation) | High PEEP increases RV afterload; reduces venous return |
| **FiO₂** | Wean aggressively; target SpO₂ >90% (PaO₂ >60 mmHg) | Hyperoxia → pulmonary vasoconstriction rebound |
| **Minute ventilation** | Permissive hypercarbia acceptable (pH 7.25–7.30, PaCO₂ 45–55) | Hypercarbia → ↑ PVR; but slightly elevated PaCO₂ reduces PEEP requirement |
| **Plateau pressure** | <30 cmH₂O (lung protective) | High driving pressure increases RV afterload |
| **RR** | Lower is better; allow time for exhalation; avoid breath-stacking | Allows adequate exhalation, reduces intrinsic PEEP (auto-PEEP) |
| **Mode** | Pressure-controlled or synchronized assist-control | Reduce patient effort (WOB); spontaneous ventilation not always better in RV failure |
| **Sedation** | Adequate but avoid excessive (maintains spontaneous breathing if possible) | Dexmedetomidine preferred (maintains respiratory drive); avoid propofol (myocardial depression) |
| **Extubation readiness** | As soon as possible (reduces RV afterload) | Even brief periods of spontaneous breathing improve RV function |

---

#### Renal Replacement Therapy (RRT) Considerations

**Cardiorenal syndrome in severe TR + RV failure:**
- **Indication**: Persistent oliguria, hyperkalemia, acidosis refractory to medical management
- **Mode**: Continuous veno-venous hemofiltration (CVVH) or CVVHDF preferred in hemodynamically unstable patients (vs. IHD which causes rapid fluid shifts, worsening RV afterload)
- **Ultrafiltration rate**: Start low (200 mL/hr), titrate based on hemodynamics, CVP response
- **Anticoagulation**: Regional citrate if coagulopathy present (avoid systemic heparin if high bleeding risk)

### Surgical Intervention for Tricuspid Regurgitation

#### Indications for TV Repair (Preferred Over Replacement)

> [!tip]
> **TV annuloplasty is the procedure of choice for functional TR.** Replacement (bioprosthetic or mechanical) is reserved for primary (organic) valve disease not amenable to repair.

| Scenario | Timing | Procedure |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **Moderate-severe functional TR + severe pulmonary HTN or RV failure** | Elective (after medical optimization) | Annuloplasty (suture or ring) ± additional TV procedures if anatomy abnormal |
| **Severe primary (organic) TR** (IE, carcinoid, rheumatic, Ebstein's) | Semi-urgent | Repair if possible (leaflet augmentation, papillary muscle repositioning); replacement if unrepairable |
| **Moderate-severe TR at time of LV valve surgery** | Intraoperative | Concomitant TV annuloplasty recommended (CTSNET guidelines) |
| **Severe TR in LVAD candidate** | At LVAD implant | Concomitant TV repair significantly improves post-LVAD outcomes |
| **Severe TR post-cardiac surgery (persistent despite optimization)** | Days to weeks post-op | Consider delayed TV repair if persistent after initial recovery |

**Annuloplasty techniques:**
- **Suture annuloplasty** (De Vega): simple, reproducible, effective; used for TV annular dilation up to ~45–50 mm
- **Ring annuloplasty** (Carpentier, Duran): more durable; allows remodeling; preferred for larger annuli or complex anatomy

### Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Intervention — Emerging Landscape

**Percutaneous techniques are revolutionizing TR treatment**, especially for high-risk surgical patients or those with extensive comorbidities. Based on PubMed literature, key trials include:

#### TriClip (Edge-to-Edge Repair)

- **TRILUMINATE Pivotal trial**: Primary edge-to-edge repair for symptomatic TR; growing evidence for efficacy and safety
- **Mechanism**: Leaflet coaptation improves via clip application (similar to MitraClip for mitral regurgitation)
- **Candidates**: Symptomatic TR (≥moderate), elevated CVP/systemic congestion, high surgical risk
- **Outcomes**: Reduces TR severity, improves symptoms, lowers CVP; may improve renal/hepatic function over time
- **Limitations**: Requires transvenous access via IVC; not suitable if IVC >28 mm, thrombus, or severe hepatic cirrhosis

#### TRISCEND (Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Repair System)

- **TRISCEND II trial** ongoing; early data promising
- **Mechanism**: Self-expanding device, direct annular reduction + leaflet coaptation
- **Candidates**: Functional TR with annular dilation
- **Access**: Transvenous (IVC, femoral)

#### CLASP TR and EVOQUE

- **CLASP TR**: Catheter-based leaflet approximation; reduces regurgitation via mechanical leaflet opposition
- **EVOQUE**: Transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (bioprosthetic); option for complex anatomy or failed repair

> [!warning] **Transcatheter TR Intervention: Expanding but Evolving**
> Percutaneous approaches are rapidly expanding; outcomes and case selection criteria continue to evolve. In the ICU, reserve transcatheter intervention for patients who:
> 1. Achieve hemodynamic stability on medical therapy
> 2. Have high surgical risk (age, comorbidities, frailty, RV dysfunction)
> 3. Have failed optimal medical management with persistent symptoms/organ dysfunction
> 4. Meet anatomic criteria (valve anatomy, vascular access)
> Discuss candidacy with interventional cardiologist + cardiac surgeon (Heart Team approach).

## 7. Management Summary: Stepwise Approach in the HVI Cardiac ICU

### Acute RV Failure + Severe Functional TR (Hemodynamically Unstable)

**Immediate management (first 1–2 hours):**
1. Optimize mechanical ventilation (PEEP ≤8, permissive hypercarbia)
2. Initiate vasopressor: norepinephrine (0.5–2 mcg/kg/min) to maintain MAP >65 mmHg, systolic RVP support
3. Add inhaled pulmonary vasodilator: iNO 15 ppm ± inhaled epoprostenol
4. Consider inotrope: milrinone 0.25–0.5 mcg/kg/min for RV contractility + pulmonary vasodilation
5. Aggressive oxygen therapy: target SpO₂ >90%
6. Assess reversibility: Is pulmonary HTN driving RV failure? Ongoing sepsis? LVAD in place?

**Concurrent medical optimization (hours to days):**
- Treat underlying pulmonary HTN (escalate to IV/inhaled PAH meds if on oral agents)
- Optimize LV function if mitral/aortic disease present
- Manage fluid status cautiously: target CVP 4–8 mmHg; avoid excessive diuresis
- Maintain sinus rhythm (IV amiodarone if A-fib)

**Reassess at 24–48 hours:**
- If improving: continue weaning PEEP, iNO, vasopressors; transition to oral PAH meds
- If stable/worsening: consider advanced imaging (TEE, CT), invasive monitoring (PAC), escalation to mechanical support (RVAD, ProtekDuo, VA-ECMO)

### Chronic/Functional TR with Organ Dysfunction (Hepatic, Renal) — Stable Baseline

**Outpatient/floor-level strategy:**
1. Diuretics: furosemide or bumetanide (loop) ± aldosterone antagonist; target euvolemia without over-diuresis
2. ACE-I/ARBs: if tolerated (renal function, BP permitting); reduce preload
3. Treat underlying cause aggressively:
   - PAH: escalate to triple therapy if needed (inhaled iNO, PDE-5 inhibitor, endothelin antagonist)
   - Mitral stenosis: refer for mitral valve intervention
   - LVAD patient: ensure concomitant TV repair was done; if not, consider delayed repair or transcatheter approach
4. Rhythm control: β-blocker or IV amiodarone if A-fib
5. Surveillance: TTE q3–6 months; monitor renal function, bilirubin, INR monthly
6. **Surgical/transcatheter intervention**: Refer if ≥moderate-severe TR + persistent symptoms, worsening organ dysfunction, or new LVAD requirement

## 8. Evidence & Landmark Trials

### Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Intervention & RV Failure Management

Based on PubMed search results retrieved:

1. **Prognostic Modeling in Heart Failure with TR**
   - Li et al. (2025) developed an integrated prognostic model for all-cause mortality in heart failure incorporating tricuspid regurgitation severity as an independent predictor (along with age, NT-proBNP, NYHA class, QRS duration). This underscores the importance of quantifying TR severity for risk stratification. DOI: 10.1186/s12872-025-04642-7

2. **Anesthetic Management of CTEPH with Severe TR**
   - Das & Krithika (2025) reviewed perioperative management of chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension patients undergoing surgical pulmonary endarterectomy, emphasizing TR assessment via TEE and hemodynamic management strategies. Highlights the complex interplay between pulmonary HTN, RV failure, and TR. DOI: 10.4103/joacp.joacp_362_24

3. **Early Outcomes After High-Risk Pediatric Cardiac Surgery**
   - Deng et al. (2024) identified residual severe tricuspid regurgitation as a significant independent predictor of mortality (HR: 6.06, p < 0.001) in post-operative pediatric congenital cardiac patients requiring mechanical support, reinforcing the critical role of identifying and addressing TR at time of surgery. DOI: 10.3389/fped.2024.1282275

4. **Pulmonary Artery Catheter Use in Cardiac Surgery**
   - Brown et al. (2021) demonstrated in a large series (n=3,519 matched pairs) that PAC use was not associated with improved operative mortality compared to CVP monitoring alone in patients undergoing valve surgery, including a tricuspid insufficiency subgroup. This supports a more selective approach to hemodynamic monitoring. DOI: 10.1016/j.jtcvs.2021.01.086

5. **Surgical Outcomes in Mitral Valve Surgery with Concomitant TV Surgery**
   - Ko et al. (2025) reviewed outcomes in 3,026 mitral valve surgery patients (including many with concomitant TV surgery), finding textbook outcomes in 87.1% and identifying pulmonary hypertension as an independent risk factor for morbidity. Relevant for understanding the burden of TR in left-sided valve disease. DOI: 10.1093/ejcts/ezaf422

## 9. Key Guidelines

### ACC/AHA 2020 Guidelines for the Management of Valvular Heart Disease

**Specific TR recommendations:**

| Scenario | Recommendation | Class/LOE |
|----------|---|---|
| **Intervention for primary (organic) TR** | Surgical repair or replacement for symptomatic severe TR OR asymptomatic severe TR with RV dilation/dysfunction | IIa / B |
| **Intervention for functional TR during left-sided valve surgery** | Concomitant TV annuloplasty recommended for ≥moderate-severe TR | IIa / B |
| **Functional TR in PAH/pulmonary disease** | Treat underlying pulmonary HTN/lung disease first; TV surgery if persists after optimization | Class II / B |
| **Transcatheter TR repair** | Consider for symptomatic patients with ≥moderate TR who are high surgical risk (emerging evidence) | IIb / B |

### ESC/EACTS 2021 Guidelines (focused section on TV)

- **Functional TR management**: Emphasizes medical optimization (PAH drugs, RV support, fluid management) before surgical intervention
- **Primary TR**: Valve repair preferred over replacement; early intervention for progressive lesions (IE, carcinoid)
- **Congenital TR** (Ebstein's): Individualized approach based on symptoms, RV function, arrhythmia burden; early referral to regional specialist centers

### Clinical Pearl Summary Table

| Clinical Scenario | Key Action | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Acute RV failure + severe TR, MAP 55 mmHg** | Start NE 0.5–1 mcg/kg/min; add milrinone 0.25 mcg/kg/min; iNO 15 ppm | Aggressive diuretics; negative inotropes (β-blockers); high PEEP; hypoxia |
| **Stable TR + ascites, CVP 16 mmHg** | Cautious diuresis (furosemide 20–40 mg IV q6–8h); PAH meds; monitor renal function | Rapid diuresis (risk acute RV collapse); salt restriction alone |
| **New severe TR post-cardiac surgery day 1** | TTE ± TEE; assess RV function, residual MR; if functional TR, optimize inotropy/afterload reduction | Panic → urgent TV surgery; allow time for myocardial recovery |
| **LVAD patient, moderate-severe TR pre-implant** | Concomitant TV annuloplasty at LVAD implant time | Isolated LVAD without TV repair (worsens outcomes) |
| **Carcinoid syndrome + severe TR, hemodynamic instability** | Somatostatin analog IV; vasopressor (NE); consider IV octreotide 100 mcg q8h; ICU monitor | IV fluid boluses (can precipitate carcinoid crisis); dihydroergotamine |
| **High CVP + oliguria + elevated lactate** | Low-dose dobutamine + NE + diuretic; RRT if persistent; assess for ECMO candidacy | Aggressive fluid boluses; high-dose diuretics; assume "prerenal" |
