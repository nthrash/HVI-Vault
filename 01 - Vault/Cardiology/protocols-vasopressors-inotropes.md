# Vasopressors & Inotropes

---

## 1. Receptor Pharmacology at a Glance

| Agent | α₁ | β₁ | β₂ | V1 | PDE-3 | Net Effect |
|-------|----|----|----|----|-------|------------|
| **Norepinephrine** | +++ | ++ | 0 | — | — | ↑↑ SVR, modest ↑ CO |
| **Epinephrine** | +++ | +++ | ++ | — | — | ↑↑ SVR + ↑↑↑ CO + HR |
| **Dopamine** | +++(>10) | ++(3–10) | 0 | — | — | Dose-dependent (see below) |
| **Phenylephrine** | ++++ | 0 | 0 | — | — | Pure ↑ SVR, reflex ↓ HR |
| **Vasopressin** | — | — | — | +++ | — | ↑↑ SVR (catecholamine-sparing) |
| **Dobutamine** | 0 | +++ | ++ | — | — | ↑↑ CO, ↑ HR, modest ↓ SVR |
| **Milrinone** | — | — | — | — | +++ | ↑ CO + ↓↓ SVR (inodilator) |

## 2. Norepinephrine (Levophed)

### Mechanism
Alpha-1 predominant with modest beta-1 activity. Increases MAP via peripheral vasoconstriction while preserving cardiac output better than pure alpha agents.

### Dosing
| Phase | Dose |
|-------|------|
| Starting | 0.01–0.05 mcg/kg/min |
| Therapeutic | 0.05–0.5 mcg/kg/min |
| High-dose / refractory | 0.5–2.0 mcg/kg/min |

### Clinical Evidence
- **SOAP II Trial** (De Backer et al., NEJM 2010): 1,679 patients, NE vs dopamine — no overall 28-day mortality difference, but arrhythmia rate **12.4% (NE) vs 24.1% (dopamine)** (p<0.001). In cardiogenic shock subgroup, dopamine associated with significantly higher mortality (p=0.03).
- **2024 SOAP II Reanalysis**: All analytical methods (win-ratio, Bayesian, effect-based heterogeneity) favored NE; probability of harm from dopamine = 0.95.

> *De Backer D et al. (2010). NEJM. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa0907118*

### Indications
- **First-line** for septic shock (Surviving Sepsis Campaign)
- First-line or early adjunct for cardiogenic shock
- Distributive shock of any etiology

### Side Effects / Cautions
- Peripheral ischemia at high doses (monitor extremities)
- Hypertension if over-titrated
- Extravasation risk (central line preferred; phentolamine for extravasation)

### Clinical Pearls
- Add vasopressin before escalating NE beyond 0.25–0.5 mcg/kg/min
- Central line strongly preferred; if peripheral IV used, monitor closely
- Can cause reflex bradycardia at very high doses (alpha dominance)

## 3. Epinephrine (Adrenaline)

### Mechanism
Combined alpha + beta agonist with the highest overall adrenergic potency. At low doses, beta effects predominate; at high doses, alpha vasoconstriction predominates.

### Dosing
| Indication | Dose |
|------------|------|
| Anaphylaxis (IM) | 0.3–0.5 mg IM (1:1,000); repeat every 5–15 min |
| Cardiac arrest (ACLS) | 1 mg IV/IO every 3–5 min |
| Continuous infusion (shock) | 0.05–1.4 mcg/kg/min |

### Clinical Evidence
- **Post-OHCA cohort** (2023): Epinephrine-based vasopressor in post-resuscitative shock → 90% in-hospital mortality vs 54.3% with NE (OR 6.21, p<0.001); re-arrest rate 55.7% vs 14.6%.
- **Network meta-analysis** (2025): No mortality advantage over other agents; increased risk of refractory shock (OR 8.24).

> *Post-OHCA study doi:10.1016/j.ajem.2023.12.003*
> *Network meta-analysis doi:10.7759/cureus.102204*

### Indications
- Anaphylaxis (first-line, IM)
- Cardiac arrest (ACLS)
- Refractory septic shock as second-line agent (when NE + vasopressin insufficient)
- Bradycardia with hemodynamic compromise

### Side Effects / Cautions
- **Lactic acidosis** (beta-2 mediated — misleading in shock assessment; lactate may not reflect tissue hypoperfusion)
- Significant arrhythmia risk
- Markedly increased myocardial O₂ demand
- **Avoid or minimize in cardiogenic shock** — worsens outcomes compared to NE

### Clinical Pearls
- Lactate will rise after epinephrine — do not use as sole marker of resuscitation adequacy
- In post-cardiac arrest shock, NE + dobutamine preferred over epinephrine alone
- Consider switching to NE + inotrope once hemodynamics allow

## 4. Dopamine

### Dose-Dependent Receptor Activity
| Dose | Predominant Receptor | Effect |
|------|---------------------|--------|
| <3 mcg/kg/min | Dopaminergic (D1) | Renal/splanchnic vasodilation |
| 3–10 mcg/kg/min | Beta-1 | ↑ Contractility, ↑ HR |
| >10 mcg/kg/min | Alpha-1 | Peripheral vasoconstriction |

### Clinical Evidence
- **SOAP II**: Dopamine → 24.1% arrhythmia vs 12.4% NE (p<0.001)
- Kaplan-Meier 30-day survival curves favored NE in SOAP II subgroup analyses
- "Renal-dose" dopamine concept is **not evidence-based** — multiple trials show no renal protection

### Indications (Narrow)
- Bradycardic hypotension (no suitable alternative) — exploits positive chronotropy
- Relative bradycardia in cardiogenic shock when heart block or SA dysfunction present

### Side Effects
- Higher arrhythmia burden than any other agent
- Dose-dependency makes predictable titration difficult
- Unpredictable alpha/beta balance between patients

### Clinical Pearls
- Not recommended as first-line for any shock type
- If using, monitor cardiac rhythm closely
- Transition to NE when feasible

## 5. Phenylephrine (Neo-Synephrine)

### Mechanism
Pure alpha-1 agonist. No direct cardiac effects. Reflex bradycardia occurs via baroreceptor response to acute hypertension.

### Dosing
| Route | Dose |
|-------|------|
| Push-dose (IV bolus) | 50–200 mcg IV push |
| Continuous infusion | 0.4–1.4 mcg/kg/min |

### Indications
- Peri-intubation hypotension (short-term bridge)
- Obstructive HCM with dynamic LVOT obstruction (↑ afterload reduces obstruction)
- Tachyarrhythmia-driven hypotension where ↑ HR undesirable
- SVT with hypotension (reflex bradycardia may terminate SVT)

### Absolute Avoidances
- **Cardiogenic shock** — decreases stroke volume + reflex bradycardia = worsened output
- Low cardiac output states
- Any condition where ↓ HR or ↑ afterload is harmful

### Clinical Pearls
- Excellent peri-intubation agent when a quick MAP augmentation is needed
- Available in pre-drawn syringes for rapid push dosing
- Reflex bradycardia in ~10% of push-dose uses — monitor rhythm

## 6. Vasopressin (Pitressin)

### Mechanism
V1 receptor agonist on vascular smooth muscle → vasoconstriction independent of catecholamine receptors. Restores vascular responsiveness in vasodilatory shock (catecholamines lose efficacy as receptors downregulate).

### Dosing
| Phase | Dose |
|-------|------|
| Standard adjunctive dose | **0.03–0.04 units/min** (fixed — do not titrate) |
| Maximum | 0.06 units/min (refractory shock only) |

### Clinical Evidence
- **VASST Trial**: Vasopressin 0.03 units/min vs NE in septic shock — no overall mortality difference (35.4% vs 39.3%, p=0.26), but in less severe septic shock (NE <15 mcg/min at enrollment), vasopressin showed 26.5% vs 35.7% mortality (p=0.05).
- Post-cardiotomy vasodilatory shock series: Rapid BP improvement + catecholamine sparing with vasopressin as rescue therapy.

> *VASST Trial doi:10.1186/s40560-018-0344-2*

### Indications
- Adjunctive to NE in **refractory septic shock** (catecholamine-sparing)
- **Post-cardiotomy vasodilatory shock** (bypass-induced vasodilatory state)
- Reduces required NE doses (may lower arrhythmia burden)
- Relative bradycardia — vasopressin does not increase HR

### Side Effects / Cautions
- Mesenteric/splanchnic ischemia (particularly at doses >0.04 units/min)
- Peripheral digital ischemia at high doses
- May worsen RV function (increases PVR) — use cautiously in RV failure
- Hyponatremia with prolonged use (V2 receptor effects)

### Clinical Pearls
- Add early when NE approaches 0.25–0.5 mcg/kg/min to spare catecholamine doses
- Fixed dose — do not titrate up/down like catecholamines
- Does not cause tachycardia — useful in patients with AF or SVT

## 7. Dobutamine

### Mechanism
Beta-1 dominant agonist with partial beta-2 activity → positive inotropy + modest peripheral vasodilation. Increases contractility, cardiac output, and heart rate. Slightly decreases SVR.

### Dosing
| Phase | Dose |
|-------|------|
| Starting | 2–5 mcg/kg/min |
| Therapeutic | 5–15 mcg/kg/min |
| Maximum | 20 mcg/kg/min |

### Clinical Evidence
- **SOAP II observational arm**: Dobutamine associated with worse outcomes in septic shock when used without concurrent hemodynamic support — underscores need to combine with NE.
- Ongoing **ADAPT-Dobutamine RCT**: Blinded trial for septic cardiomyopathy (LVEF ≤40%) — primary outcome: modified SOFA evolution.
- Stress echocardiography standard: 5 mcg/kg/min increments every 3 min (diagnostic context).

> *ADAPT-Dobutamine protocol doi:10.1136/bmjopen-2025-101200*

### Indications
- Low cardiac output / cardiogenic shock — always with concurrent vasopressor
- Septic cardiomyopathy with depressed LVEF
- Acute decompensated HF with low output and adequate BP
- Stress echocardiography (diagnostic)
- Bridge to mechanical circulatory support (MCS)

### Side Effects / Cautions
- **Tachycardia** (most common, dose-dependent)
- **Tolerance/Tachyphylaxis**: Beta-receptor downregulation after 48–72 hours — reduced efficacy with prolonged infusions
- Arrhythmias (especially in ischemic or dilated cardiomyopathy)
- Hypotension if used without adequate vasopressor support
- ↑ Myocardial O₂ demand

### Clinical Pearls
- Never use as monotherapy in hypotensive states — always pair with NE
- If tachyphylaxis develops, consider switching to milrinone
- For chronic bridge therapy (outpatient infusions), intermittent dosing protocols used
- Prefers lower filling pressures — if pulmonary edema present, milrinone may be better

## 8. Milrinone

### Mechanism
Phosphodiesterase-3 (PDE-3) inhibitor → ↑ intracellular cAMP → positive inotropy + lusitropy (improved diastolic relaxation) + peripheral vasodilation. Acts downstream of beta receptors — efficacy not reduced by receptor downregulation.

### Dosing
| Phase             | Dose                                             |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Loading dose**  | **OMIT in ICU** (causes precipitous hypotension) |
| Starting infusion | 0.125 mcg/kg/min                                 |
| Therapeutic       | 0.125–0.5 mcg/kg/min                             |
| Maximum           | 1.13 mcg/kg/min                                  |

### Clinical Evidence
- **Network meta-analysis** (2025): Milrinone vs dobutamine — equivalent in-hospital mortality (OR 0.90, 95% CI 0.69–1.19); choice should be guided by patient hemodynamics, not survival outcomes.
- **RV failure post-cardiac surgery**: Milrinone improves RV contractility + reduces PVR — superior to dobutamine for this indication.
- **Renal clearance**: Half-life ~47 min; accumulation in AKI — dose reduction required with eGFR <30.

> *Network meta-analysis doi:10.7759/cureus.102204*

### Indications
- Cardiogenic shock with elevated filling pressures / pulmonary edema
- **Right ventricular failure** (preferred over dobutamine — reduces PVR)
- Post-cardiac surgery low cardiac output syndrome
- Acute decompensated HF with preserved BP
- Beta-blocker overdose (acts independently of beta receptors)

### Side Effects / Cautions
- **Hypotension** (most common — nearly universal without concurrent vasopressor)
- Arrhythmias (less common than dobutamine)
- Thrombocytopenia (rare, reversible)
- **Renally cleared** — accumulation risk in AKI/CKD
- Does NOT develop tolerance (unlike dobutamine)

### Clinical Pearls
- **Always requires concurrent vasopressor** (NE ± vasopressin)
- Never give loading dose in the ICU setting
- Preferred over dobutamine when:
  - RV failure is present
  - Pulmonary hypertension component
  - Patient already on long-term beta-blockers
  - Dobutamine tachyphylaxis has developed
- Dose-reduce in renal failure

## 9. Choosing Vasopressor vs. Inotrope: Decision Framework

```
Hemodynamic Profile Assessment (Echo or PAC)
          │
          ├── ↓ SVR / vasodilatory → VASOPRESSOR first
          │         └── NE ± vasopressin
          │
          ├── ↓ CO / low output → INOTROPE (with vasopressor support)
          │         └── Dobutamine (low filling pressure)
          │             Milrinone (elevated filling pressure / RV failure)
          │
          └── Both (↓ CO + ↓ SVR) → NE + Inotrope
                    └── NE + dobutamine or NE + milrinone
```

## 10. Shock Escalation Ladder

### Septic Shock
| Step | Intervention |
|------|-------------|
| 1 | NE (titrate to MAP ≥65) |
| 2 | Add vasopressin 0.03–0.04 units/min (NE-sparing) |
| 3 | If cardiac dysfunction: add dobutamine or milrinone |
| 4 | Consider hydrocortisone 200 mg/day if refractory |
| 5 | Epinephrine as last-line catecholamine |

### Cardiogenic Shock
| Step | Intervention |
|------|-------------|
| 1 | NE (restore perfusion pressure) |
| 2 | Add inotrope: dobutamine if low filling pressure; milrinone if pulmonary edema or RV failure |
| 3 | IABP (if no contraindication) |
| 4 | MCS: Impella, ECMO, or temporary LVAD |
| 5 | Multidisciplinary shock team activation |

### Post-Cardiotomy Vasodilatory Shock
| Step | Intervention |
|------|-------------|
| 1 | NE (first-line) |
| 2 | Vasopressin 0.03 units/min (highly effective in this physiology) |
| 3 | Milrinone if low cardiac output component (with NE/vasopressin) |
| 4 | Methylene blue if refractory (off-label) |

## 11. Special Populations

### RV Failure
- **Avoid** phenylephrine (↑ RV afterload by ↑ PVR)
- **Avoid** high-dose vasopressin (↑ PVR)
- **NE** preferred vasopressor (maintains systemic perfusion, supports RV coronary perfusion)
- **Milrinone** preferred inotrope (↓ PVR + ↑ RV contractility)
- Consider inhaled NO or inhaled prostacyclin as adjuncts

### LVAD Patients
- Vasopressors maintain afterload needed for device function
- **NE preferred** vasopressor
- Inotropes used cautiously — excessive support may impair native LV filling into device
- RV function is the critical variable — milrinone preferred if RV compromise

### Beta-Blocker Overdose / Beta-Receptor Downregulation
- Dobutamine may have attenuated effect
- **Milrinone preferred** (acts downstream of beta receptors via PDE-3 — unaffected by beta-blockade)

### Atrial Fibrillation with Rapid Ventricular Response + Shock
- Avoid dobutamine and epinephrine (chronotropic — worsen rate)
- Vasopressin or phenylephrine for BP support (no chronotropy)
- Rate control + hemodynamic stabilization before cardioversion if feasible
