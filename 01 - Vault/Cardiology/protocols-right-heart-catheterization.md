## Normal Values & Intracardiac Pressures

| Location | Pressure / Saturation |
|---|---|
| Right atrium (RA) | 0–6 mmHg; O₂ sat ~70% |
| Right ventricle (RV) | 25/0–5 mmHg; O₂ sat ~70% |
| Pulmonary artery (PA) | 25/10 mmHg (mean ~15); O₂ sat ~70% |
| Pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP) | 5–10 mmHg |
| Left ventricle (LV) | 100/5 mmHg |
| Aorta / systemic | 100/60 mmHg; O₂ sat >95% |
| Pulmonary veins / LA | O₂ sat >95% |

> [!tip] Don't Forget Raw Pressures
> The measured pressures themselves carry clinical weight — e.g., CVP (RAP) was an independent predictor of SCAI profile and mortality in the Cardiogenic Shock Working Group registry.

## Cardiac Output & Cardiac Index

**Cardiac Output (CO):** Normal 4–8 L/min
**Cardiac Index (CI):** Normal 2.5–4 L/min/m² = CO / BSA

> [!warning] Low CI Bedside Clue
> A **proportional pulse pressure** ((SBP − DBP) / SBP) **< 25%** suggests a cardiac index < 2.2 L/min/m². Shock + CI < 2.2 usually indicates a "COLD" profile.

### Thermodilution (TD)

**Principle:** Washout of a temperature change induced by injection of a defined fluid volume cooler than body temperature. The faster the circulation (↑ CO), the quicker the temperature change neutralizes.

- Easy to perform but technique-dependent
- Less accurate in patients with irregular rhythms
- Previously thought to be less accurate in severe TR and extremes of CO — however, studies have shown good correlation between TD and Direct Fick in these settings

### Fick Method

**Principle:** Blood flow is proportional to the difference in oxygen concentration between arterial and venous blood and the rate of oxygen uptake in the lungs.

$$CO = \frac{\text{Oxygen Consumption}}{\text{Arterial O}_2\text{ Content} - \text{Venous O}_2\text{ Content}}$$

- **Direct Fick** is the **gold standard** for measuring CO
- Requires specialized equipment to measure oxygen consumption — generally not available in most cath labs
- **Indirect Fick** (using nomogram-based estimates of VO₂) can lead to errors as large as **40%** in CO estimates compared with Direct Fick

> [!tip] Key Assumption
> Both TD and Fick assume that **pulmonary blood flow (PBF) = systemic blood flow (SBF)** — valid only in the absence of an intracardiac shunt.

## Pulmonary Vascular Resistance (PVR)

$$PVR = \frac{mean PAP - PCWP}{CO}$$

Units: Wood units (WU); 1 WU = 80 dynes-sec/cm⁵

- PVR is the **recommended parameter** for differentiating subtypes of pulmonary hypertension (preferred over Trans-Pulmonary Gradient [TPG = mPAP − PCWP] and Diastolic Pulmonary Gradient [DPG = dPAP − PCWP])

---

## Pulmonary Hypertension Classification

Pulmonary hypertension is defined as **mean PAP > 20 mmHg**. Subtypes are identified based on PVR and PCWP (differentiation may also require provocative testing):

| | **PCWP ≥ 15 mmHg** | **PCWP < 15 mmHg** |
|---|---|---|
| **PVR ≥ 3 WU** | Combined Post- & Pre-capillary PH (CpcPH) | Isolated Pre-capillary PH |
| **PVR < 3 WU** | Isolated Postcapillary PH (IpcPH) | Consider high flow state |

---

## Pulmonary Artery Pulsatility Index (PAPi)

$$PAPi = \frac{sPAP - dPAP}{RAP}$$

| Threshold | Clinical Significance |
|---|---|
| PAPi ≤ 0.9 | Predicts RV failure and in-hospital mortality in **inferior MI** |
| PAPi < 1.85 | Predicts RV failure in patients with **LVADs** |

---

## RAP/PCWP Ratio

$$\text{RAP/PCWP Ratio} = \frac{RAP}{PCWP}$$

| Threshold | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| > 0.8 | Indicates RV dysfunction in patients with **cardiogenic shock** |
| > 0.54 (EUROMACS) / > 0.63 (Kormos et al.) | Predicts RV dysfunction in patients with **LVADs** |

An elevated RA/PCWP ratio correlates with RV dysfunction — the RAP rises relative to PCWP as the RV fails and can no longer transfer volume forward into the pulmonary circuit.

---

## Systemic Vascular Resistance (SVR)

$$SVR = \frac{(MAP - RAP)}{CO} \times 80$$

Normal: **800–1200 dynes-sec/cm⁵** (10–15 WU)

- In **cardiogenic or hypovolemic shock**, SVR ↑ as a compensatory response
- **Advanced cardiogenic shock** may paradoxically have a low or normal SVR (vasodilatory component, cytokine-mediated)

---

## Cardiac Power Output (CPO)

$$CPO = \frac{MAP \times CO}{451}$$

Units: Watts

- Reflects **hydraulic energy delivered by the LV**
- **CPO < 0.6 W** was the strongest independent hemodynamic correlate of **in-hospital mortality** in patients with cardiogenic shock in the SHOCK trial registry

> [!warning] CPO in Cardiogenic Shock
> CPO integrates both flow (CO) and pressure (MAP), making it a more comprehensive marker of LV performance than either alone. A falling CPO in a cardiogenic shock patient signals critical hemodynamic deterioration.

---

## LV Transmural Filling Pressure

$$\text{LV Transmural Filling Pressure} = PCWP - RAP$$

- Reflects the **pressure difference between the LV and its external forces**, influencing LV filling
- Increases in RAP or pericardial pressure reduce LV transmural filling pressure → reduced LV filling, stroke volume, and forward flow
- Clinically relevant in: **pulmonary embolism**, **right heart failure**, **cardiac tamponade**, **RV infarction**

## Shock Hemodynamic Profiles

| Shock Type | SVR | PCWP | CO | MVO₂ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cardiogenic** | ↑ | ↑ | ↓ | ↓ |
| **Hypovolemic** | ↑ | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ |
| **Distributive** | ↓ | ↓ | ↑ | ↑ |

> [!tip] Mixed Shock
> Advanced cardiogenic shock can develop a distributive component (↓ SVR), making the hemodynamic profile resemble distributive shock. Serial PA catheter data and trending CPO/PAPi are critical in these patients.

## Quick Reference: Key Formulas

| Parameter | Formula | Normal |
|---|---|---|
| Cardiac Index (CI) | CO / BSA | 2.5–4.0 L/min/m² |
| PVR | (mPAP − PCWP) / CO | < 3 WU |
| SVR | [(MAP − RAP) / CO] × 80 | 800–1200 dynes-sec/cm⁵ |
| PAPi | (sPAP − dPAP) / RAP | > 1.85 (LVAD threshold) |
| CPO | (MAP × CO) / 451 | > 0.6 W (shock threshold) |
| LV Transmural FP | PCWP − RAP | Varies |
| RAP/PCWP | RAP / PCWP | < 0.54 |
| TPG | mPAP − PCWP | < 12 mmHg |
| DPG | dPAP − PCWP | < 7 mmHg |
