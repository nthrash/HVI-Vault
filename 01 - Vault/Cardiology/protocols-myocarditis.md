# Myocarditis

## 1. Overview

Myocarditis is inflammation of the myocardium most commonly caused by viral infection, autoimmune processes, or toxic exposures, resulting in myocardial injury through both direct myocyte damage (e.g., viral cytotoxicity) and immune-mediated mechanisms. It can affect any age group but has a higher incidence in young adults and males.

---

## 2. Pathophysiology

Myocardial injury in myocarditis occurs via two principal mechanisms:

- **Direct myocyte injury** — viral cytotoxicity (e.g., coxsackievirus entering cardiomyocytes via CAR receptor) leads to cell lysis and necrosis.
- **Immune-mediated damage** — molecular mimicry and T-cell activation generate autoantibodies against cardiac proteins, perpetuating inflammation even after the initial insult resolves.

---

## 3. Etiology & Risk Factors

### Infectious Causes
- Viral: **Coxsackievirus B** (most common), adenovirus, parvovirus B19, **COVID-19**, EBV, CMV, HIV
- Bacterial (rare): Lyme disease (*Borrelia*), diphtheria
- Parasitic: *Trypanosoma cruzi* (Chagas disease)

### Autoimmune / Inflammatory
- Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) — lupus myocarditis
- Cardiac Sarcoidosis — granulomatous myocardial inflammation
- Eosinophilic Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (EGPA / Churg-Strauss)
- Rheumatoid Arthritis
- Polyarteritis Nodosa

### Toxic / Drug-Induced
- Anthracyclines (doxorubicin) — direct cardiotoxicity
- Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICI-myocarditis — increasingly recognized, potentially fatal)
- Alcohol, cocaine
- Radiation therapy to the chest

### Pediatric Considerations
- Kawasaki Disease — myocarditis as part of coronary and cardiac involvement
- Higher risk of **fulminant** presentation in children

---

## 4. Clinical Presentation

### Symptoms
- Chest pain — may mimic acute MI
- Dyspnea and fatigue
- Palpitations; syncope (suggests arrhythmia)
- **Flu-like prodrome** (fever, myalgias) 1–4 weeks prior — key historical clue

### Physical Exam
- Tachycardia (often disproportionate to clinical status)
- S3 or S4 gallop; muffled heart sounds
- Pericardial friction rub (if perimyocarditis)
- Signs of heart failure: JVD, bibasilar crackles, peripheral edema

### Pertinent Positives
- Recent viral illness in a young patient with new chest pain or HF
- New-onset arrhythmias without structural history

### Pertinent Negatives
- Absence of classic coronary risk factors in young patients presenting with chest pain and troponin elevation

---

## 5. Differential Diagnosis

- Acute Coronary Syndrome (STEMI / NSTEMI) — must rule out first
- Pericarditis (isolated) vs. Perimyocarditis
- Dilated Cardiomyopathy (may represent end-stage/chronic myocarditis)
- Takotsubo (Stress) Cardiomyopathy
- Sepsis-related cardiomyopathy

---

## 6. Diagnostic Evaluation

| Test | Findings in Myocarditis | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Labs** | ↑Troponin, ↑BNP, ↑ESR/CRP | Supportive but non-specific |
| **ECG** | Diffuse ST/T changes, AV block, arrhythmias | Non-territorial pattern; unlike MI |
| **CXR** | Cardiomegaly, pulmonary edema | May be normal early |
| **Echocardiogram** | ↓EF, wall motion abnormalities, pericardial effusion | Rule out structural causes |
| **Cardiac MRI** | Myocardial edema (T2), late gadolinium enhancement | **Gold standard noninvasive test** |
| **Endomyocardial Biopsy** | Lymphocytic infiltrate, myocyte necrosis | Reserved for severe or uncertain cases |

### Lake Louise Criteria (Cardiac MRI)
Diagnosis requires **≥2 of the following**:

1. **T2-weighted edema** — myocardial water content elevation
2. **Early gadolinium enhancement** — hyperemia / capillary leak
3. **Late gadolinium enhancement (LGE)** — necrosis/fibrosis in a **non-ischemic** distribution (typically subepicardial or midmyocardial, sparing subendocardium)

### Endomyocardial Biopsy (EMB)
Reserved for severe, fulminant, or diagnostically uncertain cases. Key histologic findings:

- **Inflammatory infiltrate** — lymphocytes, macrophages, or eosinophils (depending on subtype)
- **Myocyte necrosis** — required for Dallas Criteria diagnosis
- **Granulomas** — present in cardiac sarcoidosis or giant cell myocarditis

---

## 7. Severity / Risk Stratification

| Category | Mild | Moderate | Severe / Fulminant |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Symptoms** | Minimal, stable | Active HF symptoms, arrhythmias | Cardiogenic shock, refractory HF |
| **LV Function (Echo)** | Normal or mildly reduced | Moderately reduced EF | Severely reduced EF, hemodynamic compromise |
| **Management Setting** | Outpatient with close follow-up | Inpatient monitoring | ICU; consider MCS |

---

## 8. Management

### Initial / Supportive Care
- Admit for hemodynamic monitoring if: unstable, arrhythmias, moderate-severe LV dysfunction
- Supplemental oxygen, continuous telemetry
- **Strict activity restriction** — avoid competitive athletics for 3–6 months

### Medical Management

**Standard Heart Failure Therapy**
- ACE inhibitor or ARB for LV dysfunction
- Beta-blocker (once hemodynamically stable)
- Diuretics as needed for volume overload
- Consider aldosterone antagonist for EF ≤35%

**Arrhythmia Management**
- Treat per ACLS guidelines
- Temporary pacing for high-grade AV block
- Low threshold for ICD consideration if persistent LV dysfunction

**NSAIDs**
- ⚠️ **Avoid NSAIDs** in most myocarditis — may worsen outcomes and increase myocardial injury
- Exception: may be used in concomitant pericarditis (perimyocarditis) under careful monitoring

### Immunosuppressive Therapy

Reserved for **biopsy-proven** or strongly suspected autoimmune myocarditis (virus-negative). Not recommended empirically for viral myocarditis.

#### Corticosteroid Regimen

**Initial (Induction)**
- Severe/fulminant: IV methylprednisolone 1,000 mg/day × 3 days (pulse therapy)
- Moderate: Oral prednisone 1 mg/kg/day (max ~80 mg/day)

**Tapering**
- Taper by 5–10 mg every 1–2 weeks guided by clinical response and inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP)
- Monitor cardiac function with serial echocardiography during taper

**Adjunctive Immunosuppression**
- Azathioprine or mycophenolate mofetil — added in refractory cases or to allow steroid sparing
- Monitor for side effects: hyperglycemia, opportunistic infection, osteoporosis

#### Special Immunosuppressive Scenarios

| Subtype | Preferred Approach |
|---|---|
| **Giant Cell Myocarditis** | Aggressive combination immunosuppression (steroids + cyclosporine ± azathioprine); high mortality without treatment |
| **Eosinophilic Myocarditis** | Typically responds rapidly to corticosteroids |
| **ICI-Myocarditis** | Permanently discontinue checkpoint inhibitor; high-dose IV methylprednisolone emergently |
| **Cardiac Sarcoidosis** | Prednisone 40–60 mg/day; taper over 12+ months |
| **Viral Myocarditis** | Supportive only — immunosuppression not routinely indicated |

### Procedural / Mechanical Support
- **Temporary pacing** — for high-grade AV block or symptomatic bradycardia
- **IABP** — for moderate hemodynamic compromise as bridge
- **VA-ECMO** — for fulminant myocarditis with refractory cardiogenic shock; bridge to recovery, LVAD, or transplant

### Special Populations

- **Pregnancy:** Multidisciplinary (cardiology + MFM) approach; avoid teratogenic medications (ACE-I, ARBs contraindicated); consider delivery timing based on hemodynamics
- **Pediatrics:** Higher risk of fulminant disease; lower threshold for MCS; IVIG sometimes used (though evidence limited — see Trials)

---

## 9. Complications

- Acute/chronic heart failure and cardiogenic shock
- Life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias (VT/VF)
- High-grade AV block
- Sudden cardiac death
- Progression to **chronic dilated cardiomyopathy**
- Pericardial effusion / tamponade

---

## 10. Prognosis & Follow-Up

- **Most patients (>50%) recover full LV function** with supportive care, particularly in lymphocytic/viral myocarditis
- Poor prognostic features: giant cell subtype, fulminant presentation without recovery, sustained LV dysfunction
- **Serial echocardiography** at 3–6 months post-discharge to assess recovery
- **Restrict competitive sports for 3–6 months** (or until full recovery confirmed); Class IA recommendation per ACC/AHA
- Long-term follow-up in EP clinic if arrhythmias or persistent LV dysfunction

---

## 11. Key Clinical Trials

| Trial | Focus | Key Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **IMAC-2** | IVIG in recent-onset dilated cardiomyopathy (including myocarditis) | No significant benefit of IVIG over placebo in LV recovery — routine use not supported |
| **TIMIC** | Immunosuppression (prednisone + azathioprine) in virus-negative inflammatory cardiomyopathy | Significant improvement in LVEF and symptoms — supports immunosuppression in virus-negative cases |
| **Myocarditis Treatment Trial** | Prednisone + cyclosporine in biopsy-proven myocarditis | No survival or LV function benefit vs. placebo — highlights need for careful patient selection |
| **COVID-19 Myocarditis Trials** | Incidence, mechanisms, and treatment of SARS-CoV-2 myocarditis | Ongoing; anti-inflammatory and antiviral strategies under evaluation |
