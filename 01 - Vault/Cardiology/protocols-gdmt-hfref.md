# GDMT for HFrEF

Inpatient optimization of Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy for Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (EF ≤40%).

---

## 1. Stabilize & Stratify (Admission Day)

Before initiating GDMT, confirm the following:

- HFrEF confirmed (EF ≤40%) — identify precipitating factors
- Hemodynamic profile stratified: warm/wet · cold/wet · warm/dry · cold/dry
- Symptomatic NYHA II–IV HFrEF or HFimpEF that still qualifies for GDMT
- Decongestion achieved or actively underway with IV loop diuretics
- Baseline labs documented: SCr, eGFR, K⁺, BNP/NT-proBNP, glucose

---

## 2. Gate to GDMT Initiation

All criteria should be met before starting any GDMT pillar:

| Criteria | Target |
|----------|--------|
| SBP | ≥100 mmHg for ≥6 h without IV vasodilator |
| Inotropes | None in prior 24 h |
| Diuresis | Transitioning toward oral diuretics; stable weight and urine output |
| Oxygenation | Adequate; no symptomatic hypotension or shock |

---

## 3. Pillar 1 — RAAS Inhibition (Prefer ARNI)

### Sacubitril/Valsartan (Entresto) — First Choice

| SBP | Starting Dose |
|-----|--------------|
| 100–120 mmHg | 24/26 mg BID |
| >120 mmHg | 49/51 mg BID |

- **ACEi washout:** Enforce **36-hour washout** before switching from any ACEi to ARNI (angioedema risk)
- **Renal:** eGFR >45 preferred; acceptable down to ~30 with close monitoring
- **Creatinine:** Tolerate up to ~30% SCr rise before dose adjustment
- **Recheck BMP** within 24 h of initiation
- Up-titrate every 1–2 days as tolerated; advance to next GDMT pillar once moderate dose achieved

### If SBP Persistently <100 mmHg

Start low-dose ACEi or ARB as bridge:
- Lisinopril 5 mg daily **or** Valsartan 20 mg BID
- Plan to upgrade to ARNI once hemodynamics allow

> *PARADIGM-HF: Sacubitril/valsartan reduced CV death/HF hospitalization by 20% vs enalapril*

---

## 4. Pillar 2 — Beta-Blocker

**Initiate once ARNI/ACEi/ARB is on board and patient is warm, non-hypotensive, not on inotropes.**

### Starting Dose

| Condition | Agent & Dose |
|-----------|-------------|
| SBP 90–120 mmHg **or** weight <85 kg | Carvedilol **3.125 mg BID** |
| SBP >120 mmHg **or** weight ≥85 kg | Carvedilol **6.25 mg BID** |
| Intolerant to carvedilol | Metoprolol succinate 12.5–25 mg daily |
| Alternative | Bisoprolol 1.25 mg daily |

- **Continue** pre-admission beta-blocker unless: shock, severe pulmonary edema, or true intolerance
- Up-titrate every 1–2 days as tolerated
- **Relative contraindication:** HR <50 bpm
- If symptomatic hypotension develops, reduce beta-blocker **before** reducing other pillars
- Consider switching carvedilol → metoprolol succinate if hypotension is problematic

> *MERIT-HF, COPERNICUS, CIBIS-II: Three evidence-based agents only — carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, bisoprolol*

---

## 5. Pillar 3 — Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonist (MRA)

**Initiate after RAAS inhibitor + beta-blocker are established.**

### Eligibility

| Parameter | Threshold |
|-----------|-----------|
| SCr (men) | ≤2.5 mg/dL |
| SCr (women) | ≤2.0 mg/dL |
| eGFR | >30 mL/min/1.73 m² |
| Potassium | <5.0 mEq/L |

### Starting Dose

- Spironolactone **12.5 mg daily** (titrate weekly toward 25–50 mg)
- Eplerenone **25 mg daily** (preferred in men with gynecomastia risk)

### Monitoring

- Recheck BMP within **7 days** of initiation
- Repeat at 1–2 months
- MRAs have minimal BP effect — safe in borderline SBP
- Consider potassium binder (patiromer) or SGLT2i co-initiation to mitigate hyperkalemia

> *RALES (spironolactone) and EMPHASIS-HF (eplerenone): Both demonstrated significant mortality and hospitalization reduction in HFrEF*

---

## 6. Pillar 4 — SGLT2 Inhibitor

**Initiate before discharge (can precede or follow MRA).**

### Agents & Dosing

| Agent | Dose | eGFR Threshold |
|-------|------|----------------|
| Dapagliflozin | 10 mg daily | ≥30 mL/min/1.73 m² |
| Empagliflozin | 10 mg daily | ≥20 mL/min/1.73 m² |

### What to Expect

- Modest BP drop: ~1–4 mmHg
- Initial eGFR dip: ~4–6 mL/min/1.73 m² — typically benign and reversible
- Adjust background insulin/sulfonylureas to avoid hypoglycemia
- Reinforce sick-day rules and DKA warning signs

### Contraindications

- Type 1 diabetes
- eGFR <30 (dapagliflozin) or <20 (empagliflozin)

> *DAPA-HF and EMPEROR-Reduced: Both showed significant reduction in HF hospitalization and CV death, independent of diabetes status*

---

## 7. Loop Diuretic Transition

- Trial oral diuretics **≥24 h** before discharge with liberalized fluid intake to simulate home conditions
- Target the **lowest effective dose** that maintains euvolemia
- Consider **torsemide** if poor furosemide response or adherence issues (better bioavailability, once daily)
- Minimizing diuretic dose maximizes room for GDMT up-titration

---

## 8. Barrier Mitigation

### Acute Kidney Injury

| Strategy | Action |
|----------|--------|
| First-line | Reduce/hold RAAS-I/ARNI; reduce diuretic to lowest effective dose; retrial RAAS once SCr improves |
| Second-line | Switch RAAS-I/ARNI to hydralazine/isosorbide dinitrate only after multiple failed attempts |

### Hyperkalemia

| Strategy | Action |
|----------|--------|
| First-line | Stop K⁺ supplements; add potassium binder (patiromer); low-K⁺ diet; add SGLT2i; retrial ARNI |
| Second-line | Reduce/hold RAAS-I/ARNI or MRA one at a time; hydralazine/nitrate only after multiple failed attempts |

### Symptomatic Hypotension

| Strategy | Action |
|----------|--------|
| First-line | Remove non-GDMT BP agents; stagger GDMT dosing (morning/evening); reduce diuretic burden; reduce GDMT dose based on symptoms, not BP numbers alone |
| Second-line | Prioritize beta-blocker; switch carvedilol → metoprolol succinate; reduce RAAS-I/ARNI dose; reduce SGLT2i and retrial at regular dose later |

### Adherence / Cost

| Strategy | Action |
|----------|--------|
| First-line | Pill organizers, smartphone apps, once-daily formulations; submit prior auths early in hospitalization |
| Second-line | Post-discharge telehealth; refer to HF medication titration clinic; reassess ability to pay; institution-specific cost analysis |

---

## 9. Pre-Discharge Checklist

- [ ] All four GDMT pillars active — or contraindications documented
- [ ] Oral diuretic plan confirmed (agent, dose, instructions)
- [ ] Iron deficiency addressed if present (ferritin <100 or 100–300 with low TSAT)
- [ ] Labs ordered: BMP, glucose, BNP/NT-proBNP
- [ ] Repeat labs scheduled within 1–2 weeks post-discharge
- [ ] Follow-up: telehealth or in-person within **7 days**; HF specialty clinic referral
- [ ] Cardiac rehab referral placed
- [ ] Patient education completed: medication purpose, symptom diary, daily weights/BP, sodium/fluid guidance, sick-day rules, smoking cessation

---

## 10. Post-Discharge Trajectory

| Timeframe | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| 7 days | Telehealth or clinic follow-up; check vitals, weight, labs |
| 1–2 weeks | BMP recheck (K⁺, SCr, eGFR); adjust GDMT as needed |
| 1–2 months | BNP/NT-proBNP for response stratification |
| Every 1–2 weeks (ongoing) | GDMT dose escalation via clinic or telehealth until guideline-max doses reached |
| ~3 months post-GDMT optimization | Repeat echocardiogram to reassess EF and evaluate device candidacy (ICD/CRT) if EF remains ≤35% |
