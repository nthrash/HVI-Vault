## 0. Foundations (Brief Refresher)

- **0a. The 10-second systematic read** — Rate → rhythm → axis → intervals → chambers → ischemia/infarct. A reproducible approach so nothing gets missed on rounds. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/ecg-exam-template/)
- **0b. Rate & rhythm quick-hits** — 300/150/100/75/60/50 method, 6-second strip for irregular rhythms, and the three questions that sort every rhythm. [LITFL rate](https://litfl.com/ecg-rate-interpretation/) · [LITFL rhythm](https://litfl.com/ecg-rhythm-evaluation/)
- **0c. Intervals that change management** — PR >200, QRS >120, QTc >500 (>480 in women); Bazett vs. Fridericia; why QTc matters more than raw QT in tachycardia. [LITFL PR](https://litfl.com/pr-interval-ecg-library/) · [LITFL QT](https://litfl.com/qt-interval-ecg-library/) · [LITFL QRS](https://litfl.com/qrs-interval-ecg-library/)
- **0d. Axis — the deeper dive** — Hexaxial system, the I/aVF quadrant method, lead II to separate physiologic LAD from LAFB, and axis as a diagnostic clue for hemiblocks, LVH, PE, WPW, VT, hyperkalemia, and lead misplacement. Northwest axis favors VT over SVT with aberrancy. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/ecg-axis-interpretation/)
- **0e. Lead placement & artifact** — V1/V2 placed too high mimics Brugada or anterior STEMI; LA/RA reversal flips lead I and fakes inferior MI or dextrocardia. [LITFL lead positioning](https://litfl.com/ecg-lead-positioning/) · [LITFL limb lead reversal](https://litfl.com/ecg-limb-lead-reversal-ecg-library/) · [LITFL V1/V2 misplacement](https://litfl.com/misplacement-of-v1-and-v2/)

## I. OMI / Occlusion MI & STEMI Mimics

1. **OMI vs. STEMI paradigm shift** — Why STEMI criteria miss ~25–30% of true occlusions; how to reframe activation decisions. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/omi-replacing-the-stemi-misnomer/)
2. **Wellens syndrome (Type A & B)** — Pain-free patient with biphasic/deep inverted T waves in V2–V3 = critical proximal LAD stenosis. Do not stress test. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/wellens-syndrome-ecg-library/)
3. **De Winter T waves** — Upsloping ST depression with tall symmetric T waves = LAD occlusion equivalent. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/de-winter-t-wave-ecg-library/)
4. **Aslanger pattern** — Inferior OMI with concurrent multivessel/subendocardial ischemia; isolated STE in III with STD in lateral leads + STE in V1. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/aslanger-pattern/)
5. **South African Flag sign / high lateral STEMI** — Subtle STE in I, aVL, V2 with reciprocal STD in III. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/high-lateral-stemi-ecg-library/)
6. **Left main / proximal LAD occlusion — aVR elevation** — STE in aVR with diffuse STD; triage vs. three-vessel disease. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/lmca-occlusion-st-elevation-in-avr/)
7. **Posterior MI recognition** — Tall R in V1–V2, STD + upright T, and the role of posterior leads (V7–V9). [LITFL](https://litfl.com/posterior-myocardial-infarction-ecg-library/)
8. **RV infarction with inferior STEMI** — Right-sided leads (V4R); why nitroglycerin is contraindicated; fluid-responsive shock. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/right-ventricular-infarction-ecg-library/)
9. **Sgarbossa & modified Sgarbossa (Smith) criteria** — Diagnosing AMI in LBBB or ventricular-paced rhythm. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/sgarbossa-criteria-ecg-library/)
10. **LV aneurysm vs. acute anterior STEMI** — Persistent STE weeks-months after MI; avoiding false cath lab activation. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/left-ventricular-aneursym-ecg-library/)

## II. Wide-Complex Tachycardia Differentiation

11. **VT vs. SVT with aberrancy** — Brugada criteria, Vereckei aVR algorithm, concordance, AV dissociation, fusion/capture beats. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/vt-versus-svt-ecg-library/)
12. **Monomorphic VT workup** — Ischemic scar vs. idiopathic; ICD vs. ablation referral. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/ventricular-tachycardia-monomorphic-ecg-library/)
13. **Polymorphic VT & Torsades de Pointes** — QT-triggered vs. ischemic; magnesium, overdrive pacing, isoproterenol. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/polymorphic-vt-and-torsades-de-pointes-tdp/)
14. **Bidirectional VT** — Digoxin toxicity vs. CPVT — very different management. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/bidirectional-ventricular-tachycardia-bvt-ecg-library/)
15. **Fascicular VT (Belhassen) & RVOT VT** — Narrow-ish VT in young patients; verapamil-sensitive; don't treat like ischemic VT. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/idiopathic-fascicular-left-ventricular-tachycardia/)
16. **AIVR (accelerated idioventricular rhythm)** — The benign reperfusion rhythm; often does NOT need treatment post-STEMI. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/accelerated-idioventricular-rhythm-aivr/)

## III. Perioperative & Post-Cardiac Surgery Rhythms

17. **Post-op atrial fibrillation** — ~30% incidence after CABG/valve; rate vs. rhythm, anticoagulation, amiodarone prophylaxis. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/atrial-fibrillation-ecg-library/)
18. **Atrial flutter recognition & management** — Typical vs. atypical; 2:1 conduction masquerading as sinus tach; CTI ablation. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/atrial-flutter-ecg-library/)
19. **Junctional rhythms post-cardiac surgery** — Accelerated junctional, AV dissociation, and when atrial kick matters for a stiff post-op LV. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/accelerated-junctional-rhythm-ajr/)
20. **Temporary epicardial pacing troubleshooting** — Failure to capture, failure to sense, oversensing; the "post-op pacing puzzler." [LITFL](https://litfl.com/pacemaker-malfunction-ecg-library/)
21. **Pacemaker rhythm interpretation (DDD, VVI, AAI)** — Reading the paced EKG; distinguishing normal function from malfunction. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/pacemaker-rhythms-normal-patterns/)
22. **IABP-related EKG/waveform artifact & timing** — Early/late inflation and deflation recognition at the bedside. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/intra-aortic-balloon-pump-trouble-shooting/)

## V. Structural & Inherited Heart Disease

28. **Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM)** — LVH + deep Qs, apical (Yamaguchi) variant giant T inversions, SCD risk-stratification. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy-hcm-ecg-library/)
29. **ARVC / ARVD** — Epsilon wave, TWI V1–V3, late potentials; young patient with exertional VT. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/arrhythmogenic-right-ventricular-cardiomyopathy-arvc/)
30. **Takotsubo / stress cardiomyopathy** — Mimics anterior STEMI with diffuse deep TWI and prolonged QT; apical ballooning on echo. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/tako-tsubo-cardiomyopathy-ecg-library/)
31. **Brugada pattern (Type 1 vs. 2/3)** — Coved vs. saddleback, provocation, fever-unmasking, ICD considerations. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/brugada-syndrome-ecg-library/)
32. **Long QT & Short QT syndromes** — Congenital vs. acquired; the QT danger list — drugs, electrolytes, bradycardia. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/qt-interval-ecg-library/)
33. **WPW / pre-excitation and AF with pre-excitation** — Do-not-give-AV-nodal-blockers emergency; procainamide/cardioversion. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/pre-excitation-syndromes-ecg-library/)

## VI. Critical Care Adjunct ECGs

34. **Pulmonary embolism patterns** — S1Q3T3, RV strain, incomplete RBBB, sinus tach; what a negative ECG does NOT mean. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/ecg-changes-in-pulmonary-embolism/)
35. **Pericarditis vs. benign early repolarization vs. STEMI** — Spodick sign, PR depression, global concavity; the big 3-way differential. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/pericarditis-ecg-library/)
36. **Tamponade & massive effusion** — Low voltage, electrical alternans, sinus tach. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/ecg-findings-in-massive-pericardial-effusion/)
37. **Myocarditis ECG clues** — ST/T changes mimicking MI, arrhythmia burden, conduction disease as prognostic markers. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/myocarditis-ecg-library/)
38. **Hyperkalemia progression** — Peaked Ts → PR prolongation → QRS widening → sine wave → arrest. The CVICU must-recognize pattern. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/hyperkalaemia-ecg-library/)
39. **Hypokalemia & hypomagnesemia** — U waves, long QT, torsades; why post-op diuresis sets patients up for trouble. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/hypokalaemia-ecg-library/)
40. **Hypocalcemia post-cardiac surgery** — Long QT from citrate in massive transfusion; ionized calcium monitoring. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/hypocalcaemia-ecg-library/)
41. **Hypothermia (Osborn / J waves)** — Targeted temperature management, post-arrest, bradyarrhythmias. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/osborn-wave-j-wave-ecg-library/)
42. **Neurogenic / "cerebral" T waves** — SAH, ischemic stroke, raised ICP — and why not to activate the cath lab. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/raised-intracranial-pressure-ecg-library/)

## VII. Toxicology / Drug Effects in the Critically Ill

43. **Sodium-channel blocker toxicity (TCA, cocaine, flecainide)** — Wide QRS, terminal R in aVR; sodium bicarb indication. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/tricyclic-overdose-sodium-channel-blocker-toxicity/)
44. **Beta-blocker / calcium-channel blocker overdose** — Bradycardia + hypotension; HIET, glucagon, lipid emulsion. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/beta-blocker-and-calcium-channel-blocker-toxicity/)
45. **Digoxin effect vs. digoxin toxicity** — "Salvador Dalí" ST scoop, bidirectional VT, atrial tach with block; DigiFab indications. [LITFL](https://litfl.com/digoxin-toxicity-ecg-library/)
