# Antiarrhythmic Drugs: Classification, Mechanisms, and Clinical Use

## 1. Overview

Antiarrhythmic drugs are classified using the **Vaughan Williams system**, which divides agents into four major classes based on their primary mechanism of action on cardiac ion channels. Understanding these classes — along with their pharmacokinetics, safety profiles, and monitoring requirements — is essential for safe and effective arrhythmia management.

---

## 2. Vaughan Williams Classification

| Class | Mechanism | Key Drugs |
|---|---|---|
| **1A** | Sodium channel blockade (intermediate kinetics) | Procainamide, quinidine, disopyramide |
| **1B** | Sodium channel blockade (fast kinetics) | Lidocaine, mexiletine |
| **1C** | Sodium channel blockade (slow kinetics) | Flecainide, propafenone |
| **2** | Beta-adrenergic blockade | Metoprolol, carvedilol, esmolol |
| **3** | Potassium channel blockade | Sotalol, dofetilide, amiodarone, dronedarone |
| **4** | Calcium channel blockade | Verapamil, diltiazem |

---

## 3. Mechanisms of Action

Antiarrhythmic drugs interact with cardiac ion channels in a **state-dependent** manner — during the resting, activation, or inactivation phases of the action potential:

- **Depolarization** is primarily influenced by sodium and calcium channel blockers (Classes 1 and 4).
- **Repolarization** is prolonged by potassium channel blockers (Class 3), which lengthens the QT interval.

### Key Pharmacodynamic Concepts

- **Use Dependence** (e.g., flecainide): Drug effect increases at higher heart rates because more channels cycle through the blocked state. This makes Class 1C agents more effective — but also more toxic — during tachyarrhythmias.
- **Reverse Use Dependence** (e.g., sotalol): Drug effect is *greater* at slower heart rates, meaning QT prolongation worsens at low heart rates — increasing proarrhythmic risk when the patient is bradycardic.

---

## 4. Pharmacokinetics and Elimination

Elimination pathway determines when dose adjustment is required:

| Drug | Primary Elimination | Clinical Note |
|---|---|---|
| Sotalol | Renal | Dose-adjust for CrCl; QT monitoring required |
| Dofetilide | Renal | Strict renal dosing protocol; see Section 6 |
| Digoxin | Renal | Narrow therapeutic window in renal impairment |
| Amiodarone | Hepatic | Extremely long half-life (40–55 days); tissue accumulation |
| Flecainide | Hepatic/Renal | Avoid in structural heart disease |
| Lidocaine | Hepatic | IV only; short duration |

> ⚠️ Always check renal function before initiating renally-cleared antiarrhythmics.

## 5. Drug Class Deep Dive

### Class 1A — Procainamide, Quinidine, Disopyramide
- **Uses:** Ventricular tachycardia (VT) in ischemic heart disease; rarely used as first-line
- **Notes:** Procainamide is the most commonly used IV agent in this subclass for acute VT

### Class 1B — Lidocaine, Mexiletine
- **Uses:**
  - **Lidocaine (IV):** Refractory ventricular arrhythmias in the acute/ICU setting
  - **Mexiletine (oral):** Adjunct therapy for refractory VT; also used in **Long QT syndrome type 3 (LQT3)** to reduce late sodium current
- **Notes:** Fast on/off kinetics — minimal effect on normal tissue; preferentially suppress ischemic tissue

### Class 1C — Flecainide, Propafenone
- **Uses:** Atrial fibrillation (AF) conversion and maintenance; most effective in structurally normal hearts
- **Contraindications:** ⛔ Ischemic heart disease, structural heart disease, or heart failure — high proarrhythmic risk (increased mortality demonstrated post-MI)
- **Combination Therapy:** Must be combined with an **AV nodal blocking agent** (beta blocker or calcium channel blocker) to prevent 1:1 conduction in atrial flutter. Avoid 1C monotherapy in high-risk patients.

### Class 3 — Sotalol, Dofetilide, Amiodarone, Dronedarone
- **Mechanism:** Potassium channel blockade → prolonged repolarization → prolonged QT interval
- **Sotalol:** Also has Class 2 (beta-blocking) properties. Reverse use dependence. Renally cleared — requires QT monitoring.
- **Dofetilide:** Pure potassium channel blocker. Strict initiation protocol required (see Section 6).
- **Amiodarone:** Most effective antiarrhythmic overall but limited by extensive multi-organ toxicity (see Section 5a). Long half-life requires extended washout before initiating interacting drugs.
- **Dronedarone:** Similar to amiodarone but without iodine moiety — fewer thyroid/pulmonary effects but less effective; **contraindicated in permanent AF and advanced heart failure**.

#### 5a. Amiodarone Toxicity Profile
| System | Toxicity |
|---|---|
| Thyroid | Hypothyroidism or hyperthyroidism |
| Pulmonary | Pulmonary toxicity / pneumonitis |
| Hepatic | Elevated LFTs, hepatotoxicity |
| Neurologic | Peripheral neuropathy, ataxia, tremor |
| Ocular | Corneal microdeposits, optic neuropathy |
| Dermatologic | Photosensitivity, blue-gray discoloration |
| Drug Interactions | Increases warfarin effect; interacts with digoxin, statins |

> ⚠️ Amiodarone half-life is 40–55 days. Patients transitioning from amiodarone to dofetilide must be off amiodarone for **at least 3 months** prior to initiation.

## 6. Dofetilide (Tikosyn) Initiation Protocol

Dofetilide requires a structured, monitored inpatient initiation due to its proarrhythmic risk (Torsades de Pointes). This is a **regulatory and safety requirement**.

### Pre-Initiation Checklist
- [ ] Screen for absolute contraindications (see below)
- [ ] Obtain baseline labs: **BMP (electrolytes + creatinine clearance)**
- [ ] Correct electrolyte abnormalities (K⁺ and Mg²⁺ must be within normal limits)
- [ ] Review medication list — discontinue interacting drugs for **≥3 half-lives** prior (or **3 months** if transitioning from amiodarone)
- [ ] Obtain baseline **12-lead EKG** with manual QTc measurement
- [ ] **Hard Stop:** Do NOT initiate if QTc > 440 ms (or > 500 ms in the presence of bundle branch block)
- [ ] Ideally start first dose in the **morning (no later than 10 AM)** to allow monitoring and dose adjustments within a normal workday

### Dosing Based on Renal Function

| CrCl (mL/min) | Dofetilide Dose |
|---|---|
| > 60 | 500 mcg BID |
| 40–60 | 250 mcg BID |
| 20–40 | 125 mcg BID |
| < 20 | **Contraindicated** |

### Post-Dose Monitoring
1. Reassess QTc **2–3 hours after each dose**
2. If QTc increases by **>15%** from baseline, or exceeds 500 ms (550 ms with BBB) → **reduce dose**
3. If already on the lowest dose (125 mcg BID) and QTc remains elevated → **discontinue**
4. Continuous cardiac monitoring required for a minimum of **3 days** (6 doses) or **12 hours post-cardioversion**

### Absolute Contraindications
- QTc > 440 ms at baseline (> 500 ms with BBB)
- Concurrent **verapamil** use ⛔
- Concurrent **hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ)** use ⛔
- Other QT-prolonging drugs (unless cleared)
- CrCl < 20 mL/min
- Hypokalemia or hypomagnesemia

### Substances to Avoid
- Grapefruit juice (CYP3A4 inhibition)
- Cannabinoids
- Over-the-counter medications that prolong QT

### Discharge Planning
- Patient must have a **stable, fixed dose** established before discharge
- Prescribe **at least a 3-month supply** — therapy interruption requires hospital readmission and protocol restart
- Do NOT discharge patients with multiple tablet strengths or unclear instructions
- If dose is **adjusted over the weekend**, patient may require an extended hospitalization to complete the full 3-day monitoring period
- Arrange **EP clinic follow-up within 2 weeks** of discharge

### EKG Measurement Tips
- Automated QT measurements are unreliable — **always measure manually**
- Measure QT in **leads II and V5**
- Check EKG **before each dose** and again at discharge

## 7. Ablation vs. Antiarrhythmic Drug Therapy in Atrial Fibrillation

| Stage | Preferred Strategy |
|---|---|
| Early paroxysmal AF (structurally normal heart) | **Catheter ablation** is first-line — highly effective for maintaining sinus rhythm |
| Persistent or advanced AF | Combination of ablation **plus** antiarrhythmic drug therapy often necessary |
| Adjunct use | Antiarrhythmics (e.g., dofetilide) may be used post-ablation to suppress recurrence |

---

## 8. Key Safety Principles

- Avoid Class 1C agents in any patient with **ischemic heart disease, structural heart disease, or heart failure**
- Avoid Class 3 agents (especially sotalol/dofetilide) without close **QT and renal monitoring**
- Do not combine Class 1C drugs without an AV nodal blocker — risk of 1:1 atrial flutter conduction
- Amiodarone requires baseline and annual monitoring of **TFTs, LFTs, PFTs, and ophthalmology**
- Understand drug interactions: amiodarone significantly increases warfarin effect and raises digoxin levels
