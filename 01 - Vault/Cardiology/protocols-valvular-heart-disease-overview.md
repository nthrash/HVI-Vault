## Why This Matters in the ICU

VHD in the critical care setting presents in two broad scenarios: patients with **known VHD who decompensate** due to a superimposed illness, and patients presenting **acutely with new or catastrophic valve dysfunction** (e.g., acute MR from papillary muscle rupture, acute AR from endocarditis or aortic dissection). Both demand rapid hemodynamic assessment, tailored medical stabilization, and early cardiology and cardiothoracic surgery involvement.

Unlike elective VHD management, the ICU setting offers limited time for optimization — decisions about escalation to surgery or transcatheter intervention must be made under hemodynamic duress.

> [!warning] Core Principle
> Mismanagement — particularly inappropriate vasopressors, aggressive diuresis, or delay in surgical referral — can be fatal. **Know the physiology, then treat accordingly.**

## ICU Precipitants of Decompensation

| Precipitant | Lesion Most Affected | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Atrial fibrillation (rapid ventricular response) | MS, AR, MR | Loss of atrial kick; shortened diastolic filling |
| Systemic infection / sepsis | Any | ↑ CO demands + systemic vasodilation |
| Anemia | AS (unmasked by stress) | Masks symptoms; ↑ demand with fixed obstruction |
| Acute myocardial infarction | Acute MR (papillary muscle rupture), functional MR | Structural disruption or LV dilation |
| Aortic dissection | Acute AR | Leaflet disruption / annular involvement |
| Infective endocarditis | Acute MR, Acute AR | Leaflet destruction |
| Volume overload (e.g., massive transfusion) | MS, MR | ↑ preload into non-compliant LA |
| Aggressive diuresis | AS, MS | Preload collapse with fixed obstruction |
| Vasodilators (empiric) | Severe AS | SVR drop → refractory hypotension |

## Pathophysiology at a Glance

Understanding each lesion's hemodynamic fingerprint is prerequisite to safe management. The same intervention (e.g., vasodilator, vasopressor, rate-slowing agent) can be life-saving in one lesion and lethal in another.

| Lesion | Primary Hemodynamic Problem | LV Response | Key Vulnerability |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Severe AS** | Fixed outflow obstruction | Concentric hypertrophy, diastolic dysfunction | Preload reduction, vasodilation, tachycardia, loss of atrial kick |
| **Acute MR** | Sudden volume load into non-compliant LA | Normal (hasn't dilated) | ↑ SVR worsens regurgitant fraction; pulmonary edema from ↑ LA pressure |
| **Chronic MR** | Chronic volume overload, LV dilation | Eccentric hypertrophy | LV failure over time; well-tolerated until EF falls |
| **Acute AR** | Sudden diastolic volume load, normal-sized LV | Cannot accommodate | LVEDP rises sharply → pulmonary edema; tachycardia is compensatory |
| **Chronic AR** | Chronic diastolic volume load | Eccentric hypertrophy, maintained until late | Compensates until LV fails; wide pulse pressure |
| **MS** | Fixed obstruction at mitral valve | Underfilled LV; RV strain | Tachycardia (shortens diastole); AF (loss of atrial kick) |
| **TR (functional)** | RV pressure/volume overload | RV dilation, interventricular dependence | RV failure; pulmonary hypertension; hepatic congestion |
| **PR** | RV volume overload (chronic) | RV dilation | RV failure; clinically silent for decades, then rapid decompensation |

## Shared Diagnostic Framework

### Echocardiography — The Cornerstone

**TTE first** for all suspected VHD. Establishes valve anatomy, severity, LV/RV function, LA size, PA pressures, and pericardial effusion.

**TEE** when:
- TTE is inadequate (ARDS, post-sternotomy, obesity, mechanical ventilation)
- Suspected endocarditis or perivalvular abscess
- Pre-procedural planning (TEER/MitraClip, TAVR, surgical repair)
- Prosthetic valve dysfunction

### Hemodynamic Monitoring

**PA catheter (right heart catheterization)** in complex VHD with shock — distinguishes cardiogenic from distributive, guides vasoactive management.

| Finding | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Giant V-waves on PCWP | Acute or severe MR (non-compliant LA with rapid filling); also seen with any LA non-compliance |
| Elevated PCWP, gradient PCWP > PA diastolic | Mitral stenosis |
| Low CI + low SVR | Distributive shock (not primary VHD) |
| Low CI + high SVR | Cardiogenic shock — consider fixed obstruction (AS, MS) |

**Arterial line** — standard for hemodynamic instability and vasoactive titration.

### Laboratory Studies

BNP/NT-proBNP (trend), troponin (demand ischemia), CBC (anemia worsens AS and MS), BMP/CMP (renal function, electrolytes), blood cultures ×2 if endocarditis suspected, coagulation if emergent surgery anticipated, type and screen for all decompensated VHD with potential intervention.

### Additional Imaging

- **CT aortogram** — acute AR with chest/back pain (rule out dissection)
- **Coronary CTA or invasive cath** — pre-op in severe AS (>40 years) and acute MR
- **Cardiac MRI** — gold standard for regurgitant volume quantification; rarely practical in ICU
- **Aortic valve calcium scoring (CT)** — preferred modality in paradoxical low-flow, low-gradient AS (LVEF ≥50%, SVI <35 mL/m²)

## General ICU Management Principles

1. **Echo before vasoactives** — do not manage hemodynamics without knowing the anatomy
2. **Avoid empiric vasodilators or aggressive diuretics** until diagnosis is established
3. **Cardiology and cardiac surgery early** — the window for intervention narrows with each hour of shock
4. **Treat precipitants** — rate control, infection source control, anemia correction
5. **Anticoagulation for AF** — apply CHA₂DS₂-VASc with caution in the setting of impending surgery

### Vasoactive Drug Logic by Lesion

| Lesion | Vasopressors | Vasodilators | Rate Control | Inotropes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Severe AS | Phenylephrine or NE (↑ SVR, avoid tachycardia) | **AVOID** | Maintain 60–80; restore sinus rhythm | Only if LV dysfunction present |
| Acute MR | Minimize — worsen regurgitation | Nitroprusside (↓ SVR, ↑ forward flow) | Allow mild tachycardia | Dobutamine for low output |
| Acute AR | Minimize | Nitroprusside | **AVOID** rate-slowing agents (BB, non-DHP CCB) | Dobutamine (↑ HR beneficial) |
| MS | Vasopressors if shocked | Cautious | **Rate control is therapeutic** (HR <80) | Avoid — may worsen tachycardia |
| Functional TR | NE if RVF shock | Cautious — reduce RV afterload (PH meds) | Maintain sinus rhythm | Dobutamine ± milrinone for RVF |

### Mechanical Support Considerations

- **IABP** — beneficial in acute MR (reduces afterload, improves forward flow); **contraindicated in severe AR** (worsens diastolic regurgitation); limited role in severe AS
- **Impella / VA-ECMO** — bridge in refractory cardiogenic shock from MR or AS; avoid Impella across stenotic valves
- **PPV (noninvasive or invasive)** — dual benefit in acute MR: reduces work of breathing **and** reduces LV afterload/regurgitant fraction; PEEP particularly effective

## Severity Grading Reference

**Aortic Stenosis (ACC/AHA 2020)**

| Stage | AVA | Mean Gradient | Vmax |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moderate | 1.0–1.5 cm² | 20–40 mmHg | 3.0–4.0 m/s |
| Severe | < 1.0 cm² | > 40 mmHg | > 4.0 m/s |
| Very Severe | < 0.6 cm² | > 60 mmHg | > 5.0 m/s |

> [!tip] Low-Flow, Low-Gradient AS
> In low-output states, gradients may be falsely low despite severe AS.
> - **Classical LFLG-AS** (LVEF <50%): Use dobutamine stress echo to differentiate true severe AS from pseudo-severe AS
> - **Paradoxical LFLG-AS** (LVEF ≥50%, SVI <35 mL/m²): Use **CT aortic valve calcium scoring** — dobutamine stress echo is unreliable in preserved EF

**Mitral Stenosis Severity**

| Parameter | Mild | Moderate | Severe |
|---|---|---|---|
| MVA (cm²) | > 1.5 | 1.0–1.5 | < 1.0 |
| Mean gradient (mmHg) | < 5 | 5–10 | > 10 |
| PASP (mmHg) | < 30 | 30–50 | > 50 |

## Prosthetic Valve Dysfunction in the ICU

**Thrombotic obstruction** — suspect with sudden hemodynamic deterioration in anticoagulated patient. Echo: elevated gradients, restricted leaflet motion.

| Factor | Favors Surgery | Favors Fibrinolysis |
|---|---|---|
| Surgical expertise / risk | Low risk, available | High or prohibitive |
| NYHA class | IV | I, II, or III |
| Thrombus size | Large (>0.8 cm²) | Small (≤0.8 cm²) |
| Valve position | Left-sided (when feasible) | Right-sided (first-line) |
| Episode | Recurrent | First-time |
| Bleeding risk | — | Low |

Fibrinolysis regimen: **25 mg tPA over 6–24 hours without bolus** (ultraslow, low-dose). Achieves >90% hemodynamic success; embolic and major bleeding events <2% each (SAFE-PVT trial: no significant efficacy difference vs. surgery, but fewer complications with fibrinolysis — 7.5% vs. 28.2%).

**Prosthetic valve endocarditis** — high in-hospital mortality; early surgical consultation for HF, perivalvular abscess/fistula, persistent bacteremia, or large vegetation with emboli.

## Cross-Cutting Clinical Pearls

> [!tip] Cross-Cutting Clinical Pearls
> 1. **Know the lesion before treating the pressure:** Hypotension in AS is treated differently than in acute MR. Do not reflexively start vasodilators for "hypertensive pulmonary edema" without ruling out AS. A bedside TTE takes 5 minutes and changes everything.
> 2. **The quiet murmur of acute MR:** Acute severe MR may have a soft or absent murmur due to rapid LA-LV pressure equalization. Flash pulmonary edema + hypotension within 1–7 days of MI = papillary muscle rupture until proven otherwise.
> 3. **V-waves on the PA catheter:** Giant V-waves on PCWP are not pathognomonic for MR — they occur whenever the LA is non-compliant and filling rapidly. Always correlate with echo.
> 4. **IABP is not universal:** Helpful in acute MR (reduces afterload), contraindicated in severe AR (worsens regurgitation in diastole), and of limited benefit in severe AS. Know the physiology before placing.
> 5. **Rate control in MS is therapeutic:** In decompensated MS with rapid AF, rate control (HR <80) often improves hemodynamics more reliably than diuresis alone. Treat the rate first.
> 6. **Don't delay surgical consultation:** Acute MR and acute AR are surgical emergencies. The window for successful intervention narrows with every hour of shock. Call CT surgery early.
> 7. **Urgent TAVR is now an option:** For acute decompensated severe AS, urgent TAVR within the index hospitalization is increasingly performed. Longer ICU stays before TAVR independently predict worse outcomes — involve the structural heart team early.
> 8. **Endocarditis masquerades:** New or worsening valve regurgitation in any febrile ICU patient warrants blood cultures and echocardiography. Leaflet destruction from endocarditis can cause acute hemodynamic collapse.
> 9. **PPV is therapeutic in acute MR:** Positive pressure ventilation (NIV or invasive) reduces LV afterload, decreases regurgitant fraction, and improves forward cardiac output. PEEP is particularly effective. Don't delay.
> 10. **RV-PA coupling in functional TR:** TAPSE/PASP <0.19 mm/mmHg suggests RV-PA decoupling and poor prognosis. Target the cause of RV failure (PH, volume overload), not the TR in isolation.

## Key Guidelines

**ACC/AHA 2020 Guideline for the Management of Valvular Heart Disease** *(Otto CM et al., Circulation 2021)*
Class I: AVR for symptomatic severe AS regardless of surgical risk. Class I: TAVR for inoperable AS. TAVR or SAVR for all risk groups based on shared decision-making. Class IIa: MitraClip for secondary MR refractory to GDMT (LVEF 20–50%, LVESD ≤70mm, PASP ≤70mmHg); MitraClip for high-risk primary MR.

**2021 ESC/EACTS Guidelines for VHD** *(Vahanian A et al., Eur Heart J 2022)*
Heart Valve Team for all moderate-severe VHD decisions. TAVI preferred >75 years or high/prohibitive surgical risk. PMBC first-line for pliable MS with favorable anatomy.

**ESC 2025 Guidelines for VHD** *(Eur Heart J 2025)*
Updated TAVI data across risk groups with longer-term follow-up. Increasing recognition of transcatheter mitral and tricuspid interventions. Reinforcement of multidisciplinary Heart Valve Team.

**2021 AHA/ACC Focused Update: Mechanical Complications of Acute MI**
Emergency surgical MV repair/replacement for post-MI papillary muscle rupture. MCS (IABP, Impella) to stabilize prior to surgery. MitraClip as rescue in selected high-risk surgical candidates.
