# Vasoplegia Syndrome

---

## 1. Overview

Vasoplegia is a state of profound vasodilation and distributive shock occurring in the setting of adequate or elevated cardiac output. It is most commonly encountered following cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) and cardiac surgery.

**Diagnostic Criteria (all required):**
- Cardiac index (CI) **>2.2 L/min/m²**
- Systemic vascular resistance (SVR) **<800 dynes·s/cm⁵**
- Mean arterial pressure (MAP) **<65 mmHg**
- Onset **within 24 hours** of CPB separation
- Requiring vasopressor support

**Incidence:** 5–50% of cardiac surgery patients; wide range reflects variable definitions and patient populations.

---

## 2. Pathophysiology

| Mechanism | Effect |
|-----------|--------|
| CPB → systemic inflammatory response | ↑ IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α → iNOS upregulation |
| Excess nitric oxide (NO) production | ↑ cGMP → myosin light chain dephosphorylation → smooth muscle relaxation |
| Depletion of endogenous vasopressors | ↓ NE, ↓ AVP (vasopressin), ↓ Angiotensin II |
| K-ATP channel hyperpolarization | Refractory vasodilation independent of NO |
| Methemoglobin formation | Blunts NO signaling (target of methylene blue therapy) |

---

## 3. Risk Factors

| Risk Factor | Notes |
|------------|-------|
| LVEF <35% | Independent predictor of vasoplegia |
| Pre-op ACE inhibitor use | Blunts RAAS response; reduce/hold pre-op |
| Pre-op calcium channel blocker use | Additive vasodilatory effect |
| Pre-op beta-blocker use | ↓ adrenergic reserve |
| Pre-op vasopressor dependence | Exhausted vasopressor reserves |
| Longer CPB duration | Greater inflammatory burden |
| Warmer CPB temperatures | ↑ inflammatory activation |
| Complex/redo surgery | Longer bypass, greater tissue exposure |

---

## 4. Treatment Agents

*Dosing based on Busse et al., Critical Care 2020.*

| Agent | Mechanism | Starting Dose | Max Dose | Key Notes |
|-------|-----------|--------------|----------|-----------|
| **Norepinephrine** | α1/β1 agonist | 0.01 mcg/kg/min | 3 mcg/kg/min | First-line; backbone of vasoplegia management |
| **Vasopressin** | V1 receptor (NO-independent) | 0.01 U/min | 0.1 U/min | Add early; synergistic with NE; ↓ NE requirements |
| **Methylene Blue** | Inhibits iNOS / guanylate cyclase | 1–2 mg/kg IV over 15–20 min | 2 mg/kg/dose | Repeat in 4–6h if needed; may run infusion 0.5–1 mg/kg/h |
| **Hydroxocobalamin** | Scavenges NO and H₂S | 5 g IV over 15 min | 5 g | Turns urine/secretions red-orange; alternative to MB |
| **Angiotensin II** | AT1 receptor (RAAS) | 10 ng/kg/min | 40 ng/kg/min | ATHOS-3 trial; useful in refractory high-output shock |
| **Vitamin C** | Antioxidant; preserves NE sensitivity | 1.5 g IV q6h | — | Part of metabolic resuscitation bundle |
| **Thiamine** | Cofactor for aerobic metabolism | 100 mg IV q6h | — | Adjunct in thiamine-deficient states |
| **Hydrocortisone** | ↑ Adrenergic receptor sensitivity | 50 mg IV q6h | — | Relative adrenal insufficiency; upregulates vasopressor receptors |

---

## 5. Treatment Escalation Algorithm

*Threshold for escalation: NE-equivalent ≥0.2 mcg/kg/min*

```
Step 1: Norepinephrine (titrate to MAP ≥65)
          ↓ (if inadequate or NE ≥0.2 mcg/kg/min)
Step 2: Add Vasopressin (target 0.03–0.06 U/min)
          ↓ (if vasopressin ≥0.06 U/min still required)
Step 3: Methylene Blue 1–2 mg/kg IV
          ↓ (if refractory)
Step 4: Angiotensin II OR Hydroxocobalamin
          ↓ (throughout)
Adjunct: Vitamin C + Thiamine + Hydrocortisone bundle
```

---

## 6. NE-Equivalent Titration Table

*Use to guide escalation timing based on total vasopressor burden.*

| NE Dose (mcg/kg/min) | Action |
|---------------------|--------|
| <0.1 | NE monotherapy; optimize volume |
| 0.1–0.2 | Add vasopressin |
| ≥0.2 + vasopressin ≥0.04 | Consider methylene blue |
| ≥0.25 + vasopressin ≥0.06 | Escalate to Ang II or hydroxocobalamin |
| Refractory to all | Consider ECMO/MCS; reassess for reversible causes |

---

## 7. Differential Diagnosis / Confounders

Before attributing distributive shock to vasoplegia, exclude:
- Bleeding / hypovolemia (CI may be falsely elevated)
- Tamponade (obstructive, not distributive)
- Acute RV failure (↑ CVP, ↓ CI)
- Anaphylaxis (protamine, latex, antibiotic)
- Sepsis (especially if delayed presentation >24h post-op)
- Adrenal insufficiency

---

## 8. Special Considerations

- **Methylene Blue contraindications:** G6PD deficiency (hemolytic crisis), serotonin syndrome risk (if on serotonergic drugs — avoid), pregnancy relative contraindication
- **Hydroxocobalamin** is preferred alternative to MB in serotonergic patients; interferes with colorimetric lab assays (creatinine, bilirubin) for ~24h
- **Angiotensin II (Giapreza):** Currently FDA-approved for high-output distributive shock; data strongest in ATHOS-3; expensive — typically reserved for refractory cases
- **Vasopressin + NE synergy:** Target NE sparing; vasopressin has additional benefit in CPB-associated AVP depletion
