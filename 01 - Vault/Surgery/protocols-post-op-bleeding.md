# Post-Operative Bleeding: CV Surgery Protocol

## 1. Overview

Post-operative bleeding is one of the most common and serious complications following cardiac surgery, occurring in approximately 5% of patients and requiring surgical re-exploration in a significant proportion. Bleeding after cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) is multifactorial — resulting from residual heparin, platelet dysfunction, consumption of coagulation factors, fibrinolysis, hypothermia, and surgical causes. Effective management requires rapid identification, targeted laboratory-guided therapy, and timely escalation to surgical re-exploration when indicated.

> ⚠️ Bleeding after cardiac surgery is frequently mixed in etiology. Avoid empiric blood product administration without coagulation testing — viscoelastic-guided therapy reduces mortality, transfusion burden, and reoperation rates compared to conventional management.

---

## 2. Definitions: What Constitutes Excessive Bleeding

### Chest Tube Output Thresholds

| Time Frame | Threshold Warranting Urgent Workup |
|---|---|
| Any single hour | >200–300 mL/hr |
| First 4 hours total | >500–1,000 mL cumulative |
| Sudden increase | Abrupt rise after period of low output (consider tamponade or clot dislodgement) |
| Cessation of output | No output with hemodynamic deterioration — consider clot obstruction/tamponade |

> Thresholds should trigger immediate coagulation assessment and initiation of the algorithm below — not reflexive blood product administration.

---

## 3. Initial Assessment

### Step 1 — Stabilize and Assess

- Confirm adequate IV access and arterial line monitoring
- Review operative note: CPB time, heparin dose given, protamine administered, estimated blood loss, surgical bleeding concerns
- Assess for **surgical vs. coagulopathic** source:
  - Sudden high-volume bright red output → suspect surgical source
  - Diffuse ooze, multiple sites → suspect coagulopathy
  - Output cessation + hemodynamic decline → consider tamponade

### Step 2 — Immediate Labs

Order simultaneously:

- **Viscoelastic testing (TEG or ROTEM)** — primary guide to targeted therapy
- CBC with platelets
- PT / INR, aPTT
- Fibrinogen level
- ACT (if heparin rebound suspected)
- BMP (electrolytes, renal function)
- Ionized calcium
- Temperature (correct hypothermia — target >36°C)

> **TEG/ROTEM-guided transfusion algorithms** reduce mortality (RR 0.76, 95% CI 0.63–0.92), FFP use (RR 0.52), platelet use (RR 0.69), and reoperation rates (RR 0.63) compared to conventional coagulation testing alone. (Meta-analysis of 35 RCTs, PMID 41004381)

### Step 3 — Correct Reversible Causes First

| Cause | Action |
|---|---|
| Hypothermia (temp <36°C) | Active rewarming — bear hugger, warm IV fluids, warm ventilator circuit |
| Hypocalcemia (iCa <1.1 mmol/L) | Calcium gluconate 1–2 g IV or Calcium chloride 1 g IV |
| Acidosis (pH <7.2) | Correct underlying cause; consider sodium bicarbonate if severe |
| Heparin rebound (elevated ACT) | Additional protamine 25–50 mg IV; see Section 4 |

## 4. Heparin and Protamine Management

### Protamine Dosing

- **Standard reversal:** 1 mg protamine per 100 units of heparin administered during CPB
- Titrate to ACT normalization (target ACT <120–140 sec or institution-specific baseline)
- Individualize dosing using heparin concentration assays when available

### Heparin Rebound

Heparin rebound — return of anticoagulant activity after apparent adequate protamine reversal — occurs in up to **40% of off-pump CABG patients** and is associated with increased 24-hour bleeding and reoperation rates (PMID 39755648).

**Suspect heparin rebound when:**
- ACT re-elevates >10% from post-protamine baseline (typically 2–4 hours post-CPB)
- TEG/ROTEM shows prolonged clot initiation time in setting of clinical bleeding

**Management:**
- Administer additional protamine 25–50 mg IV
- Recheck ACT 10–15 minutes after supplemental dose
- Use TEG/ROTEM to assess overall coagulation — heparin rebound rarely acts in isolation; concurrent coagulopathy is common
- Note: Clinical significance of isolated heparin rebound is debated; the presence of coexisting factor deficiency or platelet dysfunction amplifies its effect

> ⚠️ Andexanet alfa use (factor Xa inhibitor reversal) can cause severe heparin resistance requiring extraordinary UFH doses and prolonged heparin rebound post-operatively. Alert perfusionist and surgical team if patient received andexanet alfa prior to surgery. (PMID 39052758)

## 5. Targeted Blood Product Therapy

Use TEG/ROTEM results to guide product selection. Treat the specific defect identified — avoid empiric FFP or platelet transfusion without supporting coagulation data.

### 5a. Red Blood Cell Transfusion

| Clinical Context | Hemoglobin Threshold |
|---|---|
| Hemodynamically stable post-op | ≤7 g/dL (restrictive strategy) |
| Active coronary ischemia / ongoing hemodynamic instability | ≤8–9 g/dL |
| Severe cardiac dysfunction / cardiogenic shock | Individualize — target Hb 8–10 g/dL |

> A restrictive strategy (threshold 7–8 g/dL) is supported by large observational data in ICU patients. Each additional RBC unit transfused is independently associated with worse composite outcomes (OR 2.18; PMID 41413913). Transfuse only what is necessary.

### 5b. Fibrinogen / Cryoprecipitate

**Target fibrinogen >200–250 mg/dL** in the setting of active bleeding (equivalent to 2.0–2.5 g/L).

| Product | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cryoprecipitate | 10–15 units (adult) | Replaces fibrinogen, factor VIII, vWF, factor XIII — multifactorial benefit |
| Fibrinogen concentrate | 4–8 g IV | Equivalent clinical outcomes to cryoprecipitate (meta-analysis of 4 RCTs, PMID 41179562); faster preparation; no volume overload |

> Cryoprecipitate and fibrinogen concentrate have equivalent outcomes in cardiac surgery for mortality, blood loss, and transfusion requirements. Use based on institutional availability. Cryoprecipitate has additional benefit of replacing vWF and factor XIII. **Unit note:** Fibrinogen at this institution is reported in mg/dL — target >200–250 mg/dL (equivalent to 2.0–2.5 g/L).

### 5c. Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP)

**Indications:** Factor deficiency with INR >1.5–2.0 in the setting of active bleeding; use when PCC is unavailable.

- Dose: 10–15 mL/kg IV
- Risks: Volume overload, TRALI, transfusion reactions, slow administration
- Preferred alternative: 4-factor PCC when available (see Section 5f)

> FFP is selected by 38–46% of practitioners in cardiac bleeding scenarios; PCCs are increasingly preferred for factor replacement due to faster onset and less volume. (PMID 40939733)

### 5d. Platelet Transfusion

**Thresholds:**

| Context | Platelet Threshold |
|---|---|
| Active bleeding post-cardiac surgery | <50–100 × 10⁹/L (consider transfusion) |
| Platelet dysfunction (normal count, abnormal function on TEG) | Transfuse regardless of count |
| Prophylactic (no active bleeding) | <20 × 10⁹/L |

**DDAVP (Desmopressin) 0.3 mcg/kg IV over 30 minutes:**

Selectively indicated — do NOT use routinely in all post-cardiac surgery patients. Evidence does not support routine use in unselected CPB patients. (PMID 24263581)

Use DDAVP when:
- Aspirin or P2Y12 inhibitor within 7 days preoperatively
- CPB time >140 minutes
- Documented platelet dysfunction on TEG platelet mapping or platelet function analyzer

> Cryopreserved platelets should **not** be used in cardiac surgery — the CLIP-II RCT (n=202) demonstrated failure to meet noninferiority for hemostatic effectiveness and higher intraoperative blood loss (RR 1.42; PMID 41360717). Use liquid-stored platelets.

### 5e. Factor XIII

- Suspect when clot forms but is weak/dissolves quickly on TEG (low amplitude, short clot life)
- Factor XIII deficiency leads to unstable cross-linked fibrin
- Cryoprecipitate provides factor XIII replacement; consider factor XIII concentrate if available

### 5f. Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC)

**Preferred over FFP for:**
- Warfarin reversal (4-factor PCC preferred by 62.5–71.5% of practitioners)
- DOAC reversal (4-factor PCC or specific reversal agents)
- Rapid factor replacement when volume is a concern

**Dosing (4-factor PCC):**

| INR | Dose |
|---|---|
| 2–4 | 25 units/kg IV |
| 4–6 | 35 units/kg IV |
| >6 | 50 units/kg IV |

**Specific DOAC Reversal Agents:**

| DOAC | Reversal Agent | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| Dabigatran | Idarucizumab (Praxbind) | 5 g IV (two 2.5 g doses) |
| Rivaroxaban / Apixaban | Andexanet alfa (Andexxa) | 400–800 mg IV bolus + infusion (see prescribing info) |
| Rivaroxaban / Apixaban (if andexanet unavailable) | 4-factor PCC | 25–50 units/kg IV |

> ⚠️ Caution with andexanet alfa in patients going to OR — documented cases of severe heparin resistance and prolonged heparin rebound post-operatively. Communicate use to OR team and perfusionist. (PMID 39052758)

## 6. Antifibrinolytics

### Tranexamic Acid (TXA) — Preferred Agent

TXA is superior to aminocaproic acid (EACA) for reducing 24-hour blood loss and RBC transfusion in CPB patients with no difference in adverse events. (Meta-analysis of 9 RCTs, PMID 41250264)

**Low-dose TXA Protocol (preferred — high quality evidence):**
- **Bolus:** 10–15 mg/kg IV over 15–20 minutes (intraoperatively or immediately post-op)
- **Infusion:** 1–2 mg/kg/hr IV for 2–4 hours post-operatively
- **Maximum total dose:** 100 mg/kg (reduce dose and monitor in elderly/renal impairment — seizure risk at high doses)
- **Topical TXA:** 500 mg – 1 g applied to mediastinum before sternal closure reduces postoperative blood loss by ~174 mL and PRBC requirements (meta-analysis of 26 studies, PMID 41330796)

> Low-dose TXA reduces reoperation incidence (RR 0.52, 95% CI 0.39–0.69, high-quality evidence) and perioperative MI (RR 0.64) compared to placebo. (Meta-analysis of 81 studies, PMID 40773597)

### Aminocaproic Acid (EACA / Amicar) — Alternative

- Dose: 5 g IV loading dose, then 1 g/hr infusion for 8 hours (or until bleeding controlled)
- Use when TXA is unavailable or contraindicated
- Less effective than TXA but reasonable alternative

**Contraindications to antifibrinolytics:**
- Active thromboembolic disease (DVT/PE/acute MI with thrombus)
- Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) with fibrinolytic predominance is relative contraindication — assess with TEG/ROTEM

## 7. Massive Transfusion Protocol (MTP)

### Activation Criteria

Activate MTP when ANY of the following:
- Anticipated or actual transfusion of ≥4 units pRBC within first 4 hours post-operatively
- Hemodynamic collapse attributable to hemorrhage
- Uncorrectable coagulopathy despite initial targeted therapy
- Surgeon-identified uncontrollable surgical bleeding

### MTP Component Ratios

| Component | Ratio | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| pRBC | 1 | Primary oxygen-carrying support |
| FFP | 1 | Factor replacement (target ratio 1:1 with pRBC) |
| Platelets | 1 (apheresis) or 1.5 (pooled) | Maintain functional hemostasis |
| Cryoprecipitate | 10–15 units per cycle | Fibrinogen and factor XIII replacement |

> 92.9% of cardiac surgery programs maintain an active MTP; 56.3% include fibrinogen concentrate and 44.3% include cryoprecipitate. Viscoelastic testing should be used alongside MTP to individualize component delivery as soon as results are available. (PMID 40939733)

### MTP De-Escalation

- Reassess with TEG/ROTEM after each MTP cycle
- De-escalate to targeted therapy as soon as coagulopathy pattern is identified
- Recheck fibrinogen every 30–60 minutes during active MTP
- Monitor for transfusion complications: TRALI, TACO, hypocalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypothermia

## 8. Surgical Re-Exploration

### Indications

Consider surgical re-exploration when:

| Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Chest tube output (sustained) | >200–300 mL/hr for ≥2–4 consecutive hours despite optimized coagulation management |
| Total early output | >1,000 mL in first 4 hours with ongoing coagulopathy correction |
| Hemodynamic instability | Evidence of tamponade physiology (elevated CVP, equalization of pressures, hypotension, tachycardia, decreased chest tube output with clinical deterioration) |
| Cardiac arrest | Pericardial effusion or tamponade on bedside echo |
| Refractory coagulopathy | Failure to respond to appropriate targeted transfusion therapy |

> Approximately 50% of re-explorations for bleeding identify no clear surgical source — pure coagulopathy. This underscores the importance of aggressive medical management before surgical return. However, delay in re-exploration when indicated increases morbidity and mortality.

### Pre-Re-Exploration Checklist

- [ ] Notify CV surgery attending immediately
- [ ] Activate MTP if not already active
- [ ] Ensure adequate IV access (≥2 large-bore peripheral or central lines)
- [ ] Type and cross for ≥4–6 units pRBC
- [ ] Correct coagulopathy as much as possible en route to OR
- [ ] Notify OR team, anesthesia, perfusionist
- [ ] Bedside echo to assess for tamponade
- [ ] Document last TEG/ROTEM results and communicate to OR team

### Delayed Sternal Closure (DSC)

In cases of uncontrollable hemorrhage with coagulopathy at time of initial surgery:
- DSC allows continued resuscitation in ICU with open sternum
- Indications: hemodynamic instability, uncontrolled bleeding/coagulopathy, severe myocardial edema
- Time to closure: typically 22 hours to 8.7 days based on clinical stability
- VAC dressing endorsed for mediastinal management during open sternum period
- Antimicrobial prophylaxis required throughout open sternum period
- Schedule re-exploration within 24–48 hours when coagulopathy is corrected (PMID 39435488)

## 9. Monitoring and Reassessment

| Parameter | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Chest tube output | Every 15–30 minutes while bleeding |
| Vital signs + hemodynamics | Continuous |
| TEG/ROTEM | At presentation, then after each major intervention or blood product administration |
| CBC, fibrinogen, INR/aPTT | Every 1–2 hours while actively bleeding |
| Ionized calcium | Every 1–2 hours during MTP (hypocalcemia with citrate load) |
| Temperature | Continuous |
| Bedside echo | If tamponade suspected or hemodynamic instability |

## 10. Special Populations

### Preoperative Anticoagulation

| Anticoagulant | Timing Held Before Surgery | Reversal Agent |
|---|---|---|
| Warfarin | ≥5 days (elective); 4-factor PCC + Vitamin K for urgent | 4-factor PCC 25–50 units/kg + Vitamin K 10 mg IV |
| Dabigatran | ≥24–48 hours (normal renal function) | Idarucizumab 5 g IV |
| Rivaroxaban / Apixaban | ≥24–48 hours | Andexanet alfa or 4-factor PCC (see caution above) |
| UFH infusion | Stop 4–6 hours prior; ACT-guided | Protamine |
| LMWH | Stop ≥12–24 hours; last dose >24 hrs preferred | Protamine (partial reversal) |

### Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT)

- Holding DAPT >2 days preoperatively associated with less blood loss and trend toward reduced transfusion/reoperation in CABG patients (PMID 37838696)
- Balance antiplatelet cessation against thrombotic risk (recent DES stenting)
- Platelet transfusion for persistent platelet dysfunction post-CPB in DAPT patients
- DDAVP may benefit patients on aspirin within 7 days of surgery

### Minimally Invasive Cardiac Surgery

- Re-exploration for bleeding rates are higher in double-valve minimally invasive procedures (15.2% vs 3.8%; PMID 36229944)
- Maintain lower threshold for re-exploration and early escalation in MIS patients
- Retrograde autologous priming and cell salvage reduce allogeneic blood product needs
