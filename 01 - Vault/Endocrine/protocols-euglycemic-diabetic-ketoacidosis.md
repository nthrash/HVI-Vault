## Overview

Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) is a variant of DKA in which significant ketoacidosis develops in the **absence of the hallmark hyperglycemia** typically used to recognize the diagnosis — defined by a blood glucose **<200 mg/dL** (ADA 2026) in the setting of an elevated anion gap metabolic acidosis and ketonemia. euDKA represents approximately **~10% of all DKA admissions** per current ADA estimates. Once considered rare, it has become an increasingly recognized entity driven largely by the widespread adoption of **sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT-2) inhibitors** — a drug class now used not only for type 2 diabetes but for heart failure, CKD, and cardiovascular risk reduction. SGLT-2 inhibitor use carries an approximately **2- to 3-fold increased risk** of DKA (updated 2025 meta-analysis: RR 2.32, 95% CI 1.64–3.27; ADA 2026: RR 2.46, 95% CI 1.16–5.21), with an overall incidence of approximately 0.1%.

The clinical danger of euDKA is diagnostic delay. Clinicians who anchor on "normal blood sugar = no DKA" will miss this diagnosis repeatedly. In a cardiac ICU population, where SGLT-2 inhibitors are increasingly prescribed, this is not an academic concern — it is a bedside hazard.

> [!warning] **Bottom Line** euDKA can occur in patients on SGLT-2 inhibitors _regardless of diabetic status_. Normal or mildly elevated blood glucose does NOT exclude DKA. Any patient on a gliflozin presenting with unexplained metabolic acidosis, anion gap elevation, or ketosis requires euDKA on the differential immediately.

## Etiology & Risk Factors

### Primary Etiology: SGLT-2 Inhibitor Use

The SGLT-2 inhibitor drug class is the dominant precipitant of euDKA in modern practice. Agents implicated include:

|Agent|Trade Name|FDA Indications|Pre-procedure Hold|
|---|---|---|---|
|Empagliflozin|Jardiance|T2DM, HF (HFrEF & HFpEF), CKD|≥3 days|
|Dapagliflozin|Farxiga|T2DM, HF (any EF), CKD (regardless of diabetes status)|≥3 days|
|Canagliflozin|Invokana|T2DM, CKD|≥3 days|
|Ertugliflozin|Steglatro|T2DM|**≥4 days**|

Canagliflozin has the highest representation in case series, likely reflecting its earlier approval and longer market presence. Importantly, SGLT-2 inhibitor effects can persist well beyond their nominal half-lives — glucosuria and ketonemia have been documented **8–10 days after discontinuation** in some cases, a consideration relevant to perioperative planning and treatment duration.

Beyond glucosuria, SGLT-2 inhibitors contribute to ketogenesis through two additional mechanisms: **direct stimulation of pancreatic glucagon release** and **increased renal ketone reabsorption**, both of which amplify the pro-ketogenic baseline state.

### Precipitating Factors (Triggers on a Background of SGLT-2 Use)

Even in a patient on a stable gliflozin dose, the following triggers can precipitate euDKA by creating a state of relative insulin deficiency or increased ketone production:

- **Reduced carbohydrate intake / fasting** — most common trigger; includes perioperative NPO status, illness-associated anorexia, low-carbohydrate or ketogenic diets
- **Surgical stress** — surgery is the single most commonly identified precipitant in case series
- **Acute illness / sepsis** — counter-regulatory hormone surge increases insulin resistance and ketogenesis; septic shock can also impair renal ketone excretion
- **Insulin dose reduction or omission** — particularly relevant in T1DM
- **Alcohol ingestion** — promotes ketogenesis and inhibits gluconeogenesis
- **Pregnancy** — placentally derived hormones (glucagon, cortisol, human placental lactogen) create an insulin-deficient milieu; insulin resistance worsens with gestational age. In the third trimester, significant FFA and β-hydroxybutyrate production can occur after as little as **12 hours of fasting**. Recent data suggest **up to 35% of DKA cases in pregnancy present with euglycemia** — making pregnancy one of the highest-risk settings for this diagnosis (Rowe et al., _Obstet Gynecol_ 2025).
- **Drug intoxication (cocaine, stimulants)** — catecholamine surge drives cortisol and epinephrine release, promoting ketogenesis; cocaine also suppresses appetite via feeding center inhibition, compounding carbohydrate deficit
- **Renal impairment** — prolonged drug half-life due to reduced renal clearance

### euDKA in Non-Diabetic Patients

An emerging and underappreciated category. As SGLT-2 inhibitors are now routinely prescribed to non-diabetic heart failure patients, euDKA in euglycemic, non-diabetic individuals is being reported. In this population, the typical trigger is decreased oral intake during acute illness or a perioperative state. The mechanism is identical — no diabetes required.

### Risk Factors

- Female sex (over-represented in case series, ~67%)
- Type 2 diabetes (though T1DM carries higher baseline ketogenic risk)
- Metformin co-prescription (reported in >60% of cases; may contribute to lactic acidosis or mild ketosis)
- Low carbohydrate diet or intentional caloric restriction
- Physically demanding activity (marathon running described)
- Scheduled elective surgery (NPO protocol)
- Prior DKA history

## Pathophysiology

Understanding euDKA requires understanding why the glucose stays normal despite full-blown ketoacidosis — which is mechanistically counterintuitive unless you work through the gliflozin pharmacology.

### Step 1: SGLT-2 Inhibition Creates a Baseline Pro-Ketogenic State

SGLT-2 inhibitors block glucose reabsorption in the proximal tubule, inducing glucosuria. This produces several simultaneous effects:

- **Mild lowering of ambient glucose** → mild lowering of endogenous insulin secretion
- **Relative glucagon predominance** → shift toward fatty acid oxidation and hepatic ketogenesis
- **Mild baseline ketonemia** — a normal fasting state on these drugs includes mildly elevated β-hydroxybutyrate

The body is primed, but not yet in crisis.

### Step 2: A Precipitating Stressor Amplifies Ketogenesis

When a catabolic stressor (illness, fasting, surgery) is added, the balance tips:

- **Cortisol and catecholamines rise** → counter-regulatory hormones promote lipolysis
- **Free fatty acid release increases** → hepatic β-oxidation accelerates
- **Ketone production overwhelms buffering capacity** → progressive ketonemia and anion gap metabolic acidosis develop

### Step 3: Glucosuria Prevents Compensatory Hyperglycemia

In standard DKA, gluconeogenesis driven by counter-regulatory hormones produces significant hyperglycemia (usually >300 mg/dL). In euDKA, the SGLT-2 inhibitor continues to dump glucose into the urine, preventing the rise in serum glucose. The net result: **full-blown ketoacidosis with normal blood sugar**.

This dissociation of glucose and ketone levels is the root cause of missed and delayed diagnosis.

### Step 4: Prolonged Duration Due to Drug Half-Life

SGLT-2 inhibitors have elimination half-lives of 10–18 hours under normal conditions but can persist significantly longer with renal impairment. The ongoing glucosuric and pro-ketogenic effect means that euDKA may be more protracted and prone to relapse during treatment compared to typical DKA.

## Clinical Presentation

### Symptoms

euDKA presents with the metabolic consequences of acidosis and ketosis, but without the osmotic symptoms typically attributable to hyperglycemia:

- **Nausea and vomiting** — often the dominant presenting complaint
- **Abdominal pain** — can mimic surgical abdomen
- **Generalized weakness and malaise**
- **Dyspnea / Kussmaul respirations** — compensatory hyperventilation for metabolic acidosis
- **Altered mental status** — in severe cases
- **Decreased appetite / anorexia**

**Notably absent or attenuated:** polydipsia, polyuria (the hallmarks of hyperglycemic DKA are blunted because glucose is in the normal range)

### Physical Exam

- Tachycardia, tachypnea
- Kussmaul breathing (deep, labored, sighing respirations)
- Fruity or acetone breath (from acetone)
- Dry mucous membranes, decreased skin turgor (volume depletion)
- Diffuse abdominal tenderness (may not reflect intraabdominal pathology)
- Low-grade fever or hypothermia possible

### History Pearls

- **Always ask about SGLT-2 inhibitor use by drug class name** — patients often don't know the pharmacologic class; ask "are you on Jardiance, Farxiga, Invokana, or any other diabetes pill that makes you urinate more sugar?"
- Ask about recent NPO periods, low-carb diets, alcohol use, surgical procedures
- Elicit sick days and self-management during illness
- In non-diabetics: ask about heart failure medications specifically

### Red Flags

> [!warning] **Immediate Action Required**
> 
> - pH < 7.1 or bicarb < 10 → severe euDKA requiring ICU-level care
> - Depressed mental status, Kussmaul breathing, hemodynamic instability
> - Unexplained anion gap elevation in any patient on an SGLT-2 inhibitor — do NOT dismiss because "glucose is normal"

## Diagnostic Workup

### Diagnostic Criteria

euDKA should be diagnosed when **all three** of the following are present:

|Criterion|Threshold|
|---|---|
|Blood glucose|**< 200 mg/dL** (ADA 2026 definition)|
|Metabolic acidosis|pH < 7.3 AND/OR serum bicarb < 18 mEq/L|
|Ketonemia/ketonuria|Elevated serum β-hydroxybutyrate OR urine ketones 2+ or greater|

An elevated anion gap (>10–12 mEq/L, corrected for albumin) should be present. A normal anion gap acidosis suggests an alternative or concurrent etiology.

### Classic DKA vs. euDKA: Laboratory Comparison

|Measurement|Reference Range|Classic DKA|euDKA|
|---|---|---|---|
|Blood glucose (nonfasting)|80–130 mg/dL|> 250 mg/dL|**< 200 mg/dL**|
|Arterial pH|7.35–7.45|< 7.3|< 7.3|
|Serum bicarbonate|22–26 mEq/L|< 18 mEq/L|< 18 mEq/L|
|Anion gap|8–12 mEq/L|Elevated|Elevated|
|Serum ketones|None|Present|Present|
|Urine ketones|None|Present|Present (may be falsely low — see Pearls)|
|Osmotic symptoms|—|Prominent (polyuria, polydipsia)|Absent or attenuated|

### Severity Classification

Based on Chow et al. (2023):

|Severity|pH|Bicarb|
|---|---|---|
|Mild|7.25–7.30|15–18 mEq/L|
|Moderate|7.00–7.24|10–14 mEq/L|
|Severe|< 7.00|< 10 mEq/L|

### Laboratory Studies

**Essential:**

- **Basic metabolic panel** — glucose, CO₂, anion gap (correct for albumin: add 2.5 mEq/L per 1 g/dL below 4), BUN/Cr (renal function and drug clearance)
- **Venous or arterial blood gas** — pH, pCO₂ (assess compensatory response; Winter's formula expected pCO₂ = 1.5 × [HCO₃⁻] + 8 ± 2)
- **Serum β-hydroxybutyrate** — preferred over urine ketones; more quantitative and better for monitoring treatment response. Note: urine dipstick detects acetoacetate, NOT β-OHB — a patient with predominantly β-hydroxybutyrate ketonemia may have a **falsely negative urine dip**
- **Serum ketones** (if β-OHB not available)
- **Phosphate** — euDKA treatment depletes phosphate; check at baseline and serially
- **Magnesium, calcium** — commonly depleted
- **CBC with differential** — leukocytosis common even without infection (stress response); marked left shift or bandemia raises infectious concern
- **Lactate** — rule out concurrent lactic acidosis (metformin-associated, shock states)
- **HbA1c** — assess baseline glycemic control; incidentally elevated in undiagnosed/poorly controlled DM
- **Urinalysis** — glucose may be negative or trace (already filtered/excreted by SGLT-2 inhibition) — do not use glucosuria to confirm or exclude euDKA

**Targeted workup for precipitant:**

- Blood cultures, procalcitonin, CXR if infection suspected
- Lipase if abdominal symptoms prominent
- Troponin, BNP, EKG — particularly in cardiac patients (hypokalemia on presentation)
- Pregnancy test in females of childbearing age

### Imaging

- **EKG** — essential for detecting hyperkalemia or hypokalemia-related changes before insulin is started
- CXR — baseline and to evaluate for precipitating pneumonia
- CT abdomen/pelvis if surgical abdomen cannot be excluded on clinical grounds

## Management

### Overarching Principles

euDKA management differs from classic DKA in one critical and counterintuitive way: **dextrose-containing fluids are administered first and early, often before or instead of insulin**, because the goal is to stimulate endogenous insulin secretion and terminate ketogenesis — not to lower blood sugar (which is already normal).

### 1. Discontinue the SGLT-2 Inhibitor

This is mandatory and immediate. Do not restart until the acidosis has fully resolved and the patient is hemodynamically stable. Given drug half-life considerations, the pharmacologic effect persists for 12–18+ hours after the last dose.

### 2. Fluid Resuscitation with Dextrose

Fluid deficits in euDKA typically range from **6–9 L**, comparable to classic DKA. Aggressive resuscitation is required:

- **Initial rate:** 1–1.5 L/hr of isotonic crystalloid (NS or lactated Ringer's) for the first 1–2 hours per ADA guidance, then transition to dextrose-containing fluids
- **Maintenance:** **D5NS or D5 0.45% NaCl** — the glucose infusion serves dual purpose: volume replacement AND substrate delivery to stimulate endogenous insulin secretion → suppress glucagon → reduce ketogenesis
- Continue IV fluids until the anion gap closes and acidosis resolves
- This is the most important initial intervention and should not be delayed

### 3. Insulin

Unlike classic DKA, insulin initiation in euDKA requires **glucose co-administration** to prevent hypoglycemia:

- **Threshold to start insulin:** anion gap >16 mEq/L, moderate-severe acidosis (pH <7.2), or inadequate response to dextrose alone within 1–2 hours
- **Insulin infusion:** 0.05–0.1 units/kg/hr. Note: rates at the lower end (~0.05 units/kg/hr) may be insufficient — retrospective data (Albert et al. 2023) found that rates of **0.08–0.1 units/kg/hr correlated better with anion gap and bicarbonate correction**. Titrate dextrose infusion to allow adequate insulin dosing without inducing hypoglycemia.
- **Maintain glucose 150–200 mg/dL** during treatment — increase dextrose concentration (D10W) or rate if glucose drops below 150
- Monitor glucose every 1–2 hours

### 4. Electrolyte Repletion

|Electrolyte|Target|Considerations|
|---|---|---|
|Potassium|4.0–5.0 mEq/L|Do NOT start insulin if K⁺ < 3.5 — replete first. Insulin drives K⁺ intracellular.|
|Phosphate|> 1.0 mg/dL|Replete if < 1.0 or symptomatic (weakness, respiratory failure)|
|Magnesium|> 2.0 mg/dL|Hypomagnesemia causes refractory hypokalemia|
|Sodium|Correct for glucose (not typically needed at euglycemic glucose levels)||

### 5. Monitoring and Resolution Criteria

|Parameter|Frequency|Target|
|---|---|---|
|Serum β-hydroxybutyrate|**Hourly** until < 0.6 mmol/L|< 0.6 mmol/L|
|Anion gap|Every 2 hours|≤ 12 mEq/L|
|Electrolytes (BMP)|Every 2 hours until stabilized|K⁺ 4.0–5.0, bicarb ≥ 18|
|Blood glucose|Every 1–2 hours on insulin infusion|150–200 mg/dL|
|pH / bicarb|Every 2–4 hours|pH ≥ 7.3|

**Resolution criteria (all must be met):**

- pH ≥ 7.3
- Serum bicarb ≥ 18 mEq/L
- Anion gap ≤ 12 mEq/L
- β-hydroxybutyrate < 0.6 mmol/L (or trending to normal)
- Patient tolerating oral intake

### 6. Transition to Subcutaneous Insulin

> [!warning] **Critical Pitfall: Premature Transition** Transitioning to SC insulin too early is a major cause of euDKA relapse. The SGLT-2 inhibitor effect persists even after discontinuation, maintaining a pro-ketogenic state.

Protocol for transition:

1. Patient must be eating and tolerating PO intake
2. Administer long-acting or first SC dose of insulin **2–4 hours before** stopping the insulin infusion (ADA 2026 recommendation; longer overlap than classic DKA given persistent pro-ketogenic state)
3. Continue monitoring anion gap and β-OHB for 12–24 hours after transition
4. **SGLT-2 inhibitor restart:** The ADA 2026 Standards note that SGLT-2 inhibitors **may be restarted in the hospital** for a heart failure indication once nutritional intake is resumed and the patient is stable — this is more permissive than waiting for full recovery in the outpatient setting. For other indications, hold until the precipitating cause is addressed and reassess at follow-up.

### 7. Perioperative Management (Prevention)

For elective procedures, hold times should be **drug-specific**:

|Agent|Hold Prior to Procedure|
|---|---|
|Canagliflozin, Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin|≥ 3 days|
|Ertugliflozin|≥ 4 days|

- Check point-of-care urinary or serum ketones on the **day of surgery** before proceeding — if elevated, delay elective procedure
- Do not restart until patient is eating, hemodynamically stable, and volume replete
- Note: pharmacologic effects can persist **8–10 days** after the last dose in some patients — a longer hold may be warranted in renal impairment

### Special Population: Non-Diabetic Heart Failure Patients

- These patients are at risk any time they have decreased oral intake or intercurrent illness
- Have a low threshold to hold gliflozin during hospitalization for acute decompensated heart failure
- euDKA in this population can be confused with other metabolic derangements; maintain index of suspicion
- **Important context:** An updated 2025 meta-analysis found that SGLT-2 inhibitors did **not significantly increase DKA risk in heart failure trials** (RR 1.23, 95% CI 0.51–2.96), in contrast to the elevated risk seen in CKD and high-ASCVD-risk populations. The absolute individual risk is low, but the diagnosis remains clinically important in any patient with unexplained anion gap acidosis.

## Clinical Pearls

> [!tip] **Pearl 1: Urine Ketone Dip is Unreliable** The urine dipstick detects acetoacetate, not β-hydroxybutyrate. In SGLT-2 inhibitor–associated euDKA, β-OHB predominates. A negative or trace urine ketone does NOT exclude euDKA. Always check **serum β-hydroxybutyrate**.

> [!tip] **Pearl 2: Glucosuria Paradox** Unlike classic DKA where glucosuria supports the diagnosis, in euDKA the SGLT-2 inhibitor may cause glucosuria even at normal glucose levels — and a negative urine glucose does not exclude a gliflozin effect. Don't use glucosuria pattern to confirm or refute the diagnosis.

> [!tip] **Pearl 3: euDKA in Non-Diabetics is Real** Any heart failure patient on empagliflozin or dapagliflozin who presents with unexplained anion gap metabolic acidosis, even with normal glucose and no diabetes, can have euDKA. The label "diabetic" in the name is increasingly a misnomer.

> [!tip] **Pearl 4: Dextrose Before Insulin** The counter-intuitive management principle: start D5-containing fluids first. This stimulates endogenous insulin and halts ketogenesis faster than exogenous insulin alone in a euglycemic patient, while avoiding hypoglycemia.

> [!tip] **Pearl 5: Watch for Relapse** euDKA has a higher relapse rate than classic DKA. The prolonged drug half-life means the pro-ketogenic state persists. Overlap SC insulin with the infusion, continue monitoring the anion gap, and don't restart the SGLT-2 inhibitor prematurely.

> [!tip] **Pearl 6: Anion Gap > Ketones for Monitoring** Serial anion gap is your primary monitoring tool. Serum β-OHB confirms ketone clearance. Do not declare resolution based solely on improving pH or "feeling better." The anion gap must close.

> [!tip] **Pearl 7: Screen the Medication List on Every Admission** Any hospitalized patient on an SGLT-2 inhibitor who is being made NPO for any reason (procedure, GI illness, cardiac cath, surgery) is at risk. Have a standing awareness and consider prophylactic discontinuation.

> [!tip] **Pearl 8: Metformin + SGLT-2 = Layered Risk** Metformin alone rarely causes lactic acidosis but in the setting of renal impairment, dehydration, or contrast exposure, the combination of metformin-related lactate and SGLT-2–related ketones can create a complex mixed picture. Check lactate in these patients.

## Evidence & Landmark Trials

### Historical Context

euDKA was first formally described by Munro et al. in **1973** (Lancet) in patients with T1DM who developed DKA with minimal hyperglycemia in the setting of food restriction and insulin continuation. The condition remained obscure until the SGLT-2 inhibitor era — FDA approval of canagliflozin in 2013 was followed by a rapid accumulation of case reports.

**2015:** The FDA issued a Drug Safety Communication warning that SGLT-2 inhibitors can cause DKA with lower-than-expected blood glucose levels, mandating labeling updates across the drug class.

**2016 ADA/EASD Position Statement** acknowledged euDKA as a class effect of SGLT-2 inhibitors and recommended withholding the drugs in the perioperative setting.

### Key Clinical Trial Context

The major outcomes trials for SGLT-2 inhibitors — **EMPA-REG OUTCOME** (2015), **CANVAS** (2017), **DECLARE-TIMI 58** (2019), **DAPA-HF** (2019), **EMPEROR-Reduced** (2020) — demonstrated profound cardiovascular and renal benefits for this drug class. These trials were not designed to estimate euDKA risk specifically; DKA was an adjudicated safety event in most, with absolute event rates low but elevated vs. placebo. An updated 2025 meta-analysis of 22 RCTs (80,235 patients) placed the pooled DKA risk at **RR 2.32 (95% CI 1.64–3.27)** — more modest than earlier estimates, and notably non-significant in heart failure–specific trials (RR 1.23). The immense benefit-to-risk ratio of these drugs means they are not going away — the clinical imperative is safer use, not avoidance.

## Key Guidelines

- **FDA Drug Safety Communication (2015, updated 2020):** Warns of euDKA risk with all SGLT-2 inhibitors; recommends withholding in perioperative setting and educating patients on sick-day management.
- **ADA Standards of Medical Care in Diabetes (annually updated):** Acknowledges euDKA as class effect; recommends holding SGLT-2 inhibitors ≥3–4 days before elective surgery; recommends ketone monitoring on day of surgery before proceeding.
- **ADA/EASD Consensus Report on DKA Management:** Addresses euDKA explicitly; recommends glucose-containing IV fluids as primary intervention and emphasizes anion gap (not glucose) as the primary monitoring parameter.
- **SCCM/ADA Joint Statement (perioperative glucose management):** Recommends holding SGLT-2 inhibitors preoperatively and post-procedure until tolerating full PO intake and hemodynamically stable.
- **Heart Failure Society of America / ESC 2023 HF Guidelines:** Endorse SGLT-2 inhibitor use across HFrEF and HFmrEF; acknowledge DKA as an adverse event requiring patient education and perioperative management protocols.
- **2024 AHA/ACC Perioperative Cardiovascular Management Guidelines:** Give a **Class I recommendation (C-LD)** to discontinue SGLT-2 inhibitors **3–4 days before surgery** to reduce perioperative metabolic acidosis risk (Thompson et al., _JACC_ 2024).
- **2026 SPAQI Multidisciplinary Consensus Statement:** Society for Perioperative Assessment and Quality Improvement provides updated perioperative SGLT-2 inhibitor management using a modified Delphi process, with a tailored approach based on diabetes status, comorbidities, and dietary considerations — the most current perioperative guidance available (Oprea et al., _Br J Anaesth_ 2026).
