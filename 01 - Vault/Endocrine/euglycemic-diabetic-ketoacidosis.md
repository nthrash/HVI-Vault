# Euglycemic Diabetic Ketoacidosis (EDKA)

## 1. Introduction
- **Definition:** Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (EDKA) is a rare, acute, life-threatening emergency characterized by the triad of euglycemia (blood glucose <250 mg/dL), metabolic acidosis, and ketoacidosis.
- **Incidence:** Approximately 2.6% to 3.2% of DKA admissions are cases of EDKA. The risk of EDKA from SGLT2 inhibitors is increased sevenfold in patients with T2DM, with an estimated overall incidence of approximately 0.1%.
- **Clinical Significance:** Unlike classic DKA, EDKA is often overlooked because of the absence of hyperglycemia, leading to delayed diagnosis and treatment. Failure to recognize EDKA can result in cerebral edema, coma, and death.

## 2. Pathophysiology
- **Mechanism:** EDKA involves a general state of starvation that results in ketosis while normoglycemia is maintained. In the presence of a carbohydrate deficit, there is a reduction in serum insulin and an excess of counterregulatory hormones (glucagon, epinephrine, cortisol).
- **Metabolic Shift:** The increase in glucagon and decrease in insulin shift metabolism toward lipolysis, increasing free fatty acids (FFAs) and promoting ketoacidosis. Ketone bodies (acetoacetic acid, beta-hydroxybutyric acid, acetone) are released, resulting in metabolic acidosis.
- **Contributing Factors:**
  1. Decreased hepatic glucose production during fasting when glycogen stores are depleted
  2. Increased urinary excretion of glucose

## 3. DKA vs. EDKA: Laboratory Values

| Measurement | Reference | DKA | EDKA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blood glucose (nonfasting) | 80-130 mg/dL | >250 mg/dL | <250 mg/dL |
| Arterial pH | 7.35-7.45 | <7.3 | <7.3 |
| Serum bicarbonate | 22-26 mEq/L | <18 mEq/L | <18 mEq/L |
| Urine ketones | None present | Present | Present |
| Serum ketones | None present | Present | Present |
| Anion gap | 0 mEq/L | 10-12 mEq/L | 10-12 mEq/L |

## 4. Precipitating Factors

### SGLT2 Inhibitors
- SGLT2i (canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin) prevent reabsorption of glucose in the proximal convoluted tubule, enhancing urinary glucose excretion
- Lower plasma glucose creates a carbohydrate deficit and volume depletion, stimulating glucagon secretion and suppressing insulin production
- SGLT2i also directly stimulate pancreatic glucagon release and increase ketone reabsorption
- **FDA Warning (May 2015):** SGLT2i may cause DKA in T1DM and T2DM patients
- **Perioperative Considerations:**
  - Canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin: stop at least **3 days** prior to a procedure
  - Ertugliflozin: stop at least **4 days** prior to a procedure
  - Effects may persist beyond five half-lives (2-3 days), with glucosuria and ketonemia present even 8-10 days after discontinuation

### Low Caloric Intake or Starvation
- A low-caloric diet or starvation in patients with or without diabetes can trigger EDKA
- Reduced carbohydrate intake leads to FFAs and ketones becoming the primary energy source
- Glycogen stores become entirely depleted, resulting in ketoacidosis with euglycemia

### Pregnancy
- 0.8% to 1.1% of DKA cases in pregnant women are EDKA
- Placentally derived hormones (glucagon, cortisol, human placental lactogen) create an insulin-deficient state
- Insulin resistance increases with gestational age and is exacerbated by prolonged fasting in the second and third trimesters
- Significant production of FFAs and beta-hydroxybutyrate occurs after 12 hours of fasting in pregnant women in their third trimester

### Acute Onset of Infection
- Sepsis can increase counterregulatory hormones and insulin resistance, inducing ketogenesis even in non-diabetic patients
- Septic shock can induce renal dysfunction, leading to decreased excretion of ketones

### Drug-Induced Intoxication
- Cocaine and other substances can trigger EDKA through stimulatory effects on cortisol, epinephrine, and norepinephrine release
- Cocaine can also suppress feeding centers, resulting in anorexia

### Surgery
- Surgical stress combined with SGLT2i use or fasting can precipitate EDKA
- AACE/ACE recommends stopping SGLT2i at least 24 hours before anticipated surgery, procedure, or stressful physical activity
- Case reports suggest effects persist well beyond typical elimination half-lives

## 5. Treatment: Stepwise Approach

### Step 1 - Stop Inciting Agent, if Applicable
- Discontinue SGLT2i or other inciting agent(s) as soon as EDKA is diagnosed
- Perform a comprehensive medication reconciliation to assist in establishing differential diagnoses and optimal management

### Step 2 - Start Fluid Replacement With Monitoring of Electrolytes and Ketones
- Fluid resuscitation is the focus of initial management
- Fluid loss due to EDKA can range from **6 L to 9 L**
- **ADA Recommendation:** 1 L/hour to 1.5 L/hour of normal saline or lactated Ringer's solution during the first 1 to 2 hours
- Continue IV fluids until the anion gap closes and acidosis resolves
- **Monitoring:**
  - Ketones: monitored **hourly** until blood ketones <0.6 mmol/L
  - Electrolytes: monitored **every 2 hours** until stabilized

### Step 3 - Start Continuous Insulin Infusion
- Insulin suppresses ketone formation by promoting glucose utilization via decreasing gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis
- Start continuous insulin infusion at **0.05 U/kg/hour to 0.1 U/kg/hour**
- **Prerequisite:** Serum potassium must be >3.3 mEq/L before starting insulin
  - Insulin causes intracellular potassium shift; if hypokalemia is present, delay insulin until potassium normalizes
- Monitor potassium levels every 2 hours until electrolyte stabilization
- **Transition:** Once EDKA resolves, start SC long-acting basal insulin and premeal rapid-acting insulin. Continue insulin infusion for at least 1 hour after SC insulin is administered.

### Step 4 - Start Dextrose Administration
- Add **D5W** to IV fluids because blood glucose is <250 mg/dL
- Dextrose restores normal cellular glucose utilization, enhancing clearance and reducing production of ketone bodies
- D5W also prevents hypoglycemia during insulin infusion
- If ketoacidosis persists despite D5W, escalate to **D10W**

## 6. Prevention and Patient Education
- **Medication Adherence:** Patients should maintain consistent use of prescribed medications
- **Adequate Nutrition:** Patients should consume adequate meals throughout the day, especially carbohydrates
- **SGLT2i Precautions:** Counsel patients on circumstances requiring SGLT2i to be withheld:
  - Prior to procedures (3-4 days depending on agent)
  - Stressful physical activity (e.g., marathon running)
  - Acute illness
  - Low caloric intake or fasting
- **Symptom Recognition:** Educate patients on EDKA symptoms despite normal blood glucose:
  - Nausea and vomiting
  - Shortness of breath
  - Lethargy and fatigue
  - Loss of appetite
  - Abdominal pain

## 7. Key Takeaways
- EDKA should be suspected in **any patient presenting with metabolic acidosis and ketoacidosis**, even with normal blood glucose
- The most common iatrogenic cause is **SGLT2 inhibitors**, but EDKA can also occur with starvation, pregnancy, infection, surgery, and drug intoxication
- Treatment follows a stepwise approach: **stop inciting agent, fluid resuscitation, insulin infusion, dextrose administration**
- Unlike classic DKA, **dextrose must be started early** because blood glucose is already <250 mg/dL
- **Delay insulin if potassium <3.3 mEq/L** to prevent life-threatening hypokalemia
