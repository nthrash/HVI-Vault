# Intra-Aortic Balloon Pump (IABP)

---

## 1. Overview & Mechanism

The IABP uses **counterpulsation** — balloon inflation and deflation timed to the cardiac cycle — to augment coronary perfusion and reduce cardiac workload.

| Phase | Timing | Mechanism | Benefit |
|-------|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Inflation** | Diastole — at dicrotic notch (aortic valve closure) | Balloon inflates → displaces blood retrograde → ↑ diastolic aortic pressure | ↑ Coronary perfusion pressure; ↑ MAP |
| **Deflation** | Just before systole — aortic valve opening | Rapid deflation → ↓ aortic end-diastolic pressure → ↓ resistance to ejection | ↓ Afterload; ↓ cardiac work; ↑ forward CO |

---

## 2. Hemodynamic Effects

| Parameter | Effect |
|-----------|--------|
| Coronary perfusion | ↑ |
| Cardiac output | ↑ (indirect — afterload reduction) |
| Heart rate | ↓ (↑ O₂ supply → ↓ demand) |
| PAWP (wedge) | ↓ |
| SVR | ↓ |
| Systolic BP | ↓ |
| Diastolic BP | ↑ (augmented) |
| End-diastolic BP | ↓ (deflation creates vacuum) |
| MAP | ↑ |
| MVO₂ (myocardial O₂ demand) | ↓ |

---

## 3. Indications

| Indication | Notes |
|------------|-------|
| Acute MI / refractory unstable angina | Bridge to revascularization; support during high-risk PCI |
| Cardiogenic shock | Temporary stabilization before definitive therapy |
| Weaning from CPB | Post-cardiac surgery low output syndrome |
| High-risk CABG / PCI | Pre-procedural placement for hemodynamic support |
| Mechanical bridge to advanced MCS | Bridge to VAD or heart transplant evaluation |

---

## 4. Contraindications

| Contraindication | Reason |
|-----------------|--------|
| **Severe aortic insufficiency** | Balloon inflation worsens AI; ↑ LVEDP, pulmonary edema |
| **Aortic aneurysm / dissection** | Catastrophic rupture risk |
| **Severe peripheral vascular disease / calcific aorto-iliac disease** | Cannot safely advance catheter |
| Uncontrolled sepsis (relative) | Risk/benefit consideration |

---

## 5. Placement

- **Tip position:** 1–2 cm distal to left subclavian artery origin
- **Base position:** Above renal arteries (typically T9–T10 on CXR)
- **Confirm with:** Fluoroscopy during insertion **or** post-insertion CXR
- **Approach:** Usually femoral artery; can be placed via brachial or axillary (alternative access)

---

## 6. Timing

### Correct Timing

| Event | Balloon Action | ECG Correlation |
|-------|--------------|-----------------|
| Aortic valve closure (dicrotic notch) | **Inflate** | End of T-wave / beginning of diastole |
| Aortic valve opening (end of diastole) | **Deflate** | R-wave of next cycle |

### Timing Errors

| Error | Waveform Finding | Consequence |
|-------|-----------------|-------------|
| **Early Inflation** (before dicrotic notch) | Inflation before incisura on arterial waveform | ↑ LVEDP; aortic valve closes prematurely; ↑ MVO₂; potential AR worsening |
| **Late Inflation** (after dicrotic notch) | Delayed diastolic augmentation peak | Suboptimal coronary perfusion; reduced diastolic augmentation |
| **Early Deflation** (too early in diastole) | Diastolic waveform drops prematurely | Suboptimal afterload reduction; retrograde coronary flow |
| **Late Deflation** (into systole) | Elevated aortic end-diastolic pressure | Increased afterload; ↑ MVO₂; prolonged isovolumetric contraction phase; impedes ejection |

---

## 7. Trigger Modes

| Mode | Trigger Source | Use |
|------|--------------|-----|
| **ECG (R-wave)** | QRS complex | **Preferred** — most reliable |
| **Pressure (systolic upstroke)** | Arterial waveform | Backup if ECG signal poor |
| **Pacer V** / **Pacer A-V** | Ventricular pacer spike | For paced rhythms |
| **Pacer A** | Atrial-sensed R-wave | For atrial-paced rhythms |
| **Internal (asynchronous)** | Fixed rate ~80 BPM | Cardiac arrest or CPB only |

**Ratio settings:**
- **1:1** — every beat (standard support)
- **1:2** — every other beat (weaning)
- **1:3** — weaning trial before removal

---

## 8. Complications

| Complication | Notes |
|-------------|-------|
| **Limb ischemia** | Most common; check distal pulses q1–2h; remove and reassess if ischemia develops |
| **Insertion site bleeding** | Monitor access site; avoid anticoagulation if not indicated |
| **Thrombocytopenia** | Mechanical platelet destruction; monitor CBC daily |
| **Balloon rupture / gas leak** | Blood in extracorporeal tubing or "Gas Loss in IAB Circuit" alarm → **immediately stop pump and remove catheter** |
| **Aortic dissection** | Rare; typically at insertion; consider if sudden hemodynamic deterioration |
| **Infection** | Daily site inspection; standard central line precautions |
| **Renal artery occlusion** | Tip migrated distally; confirm position with CXR |
| **Mesenteric ischemia** | If balloon occludes celiac/SMA; check CXR position |

---

## 9. Balloon Leak Protocol

⚠️ **Gas Loss Alarm / Blood in Tubing = Balloon Rupture**

1. **Stop the pump immediately**
2. Do NOT inflate again
3. Place patient supine
4. Remove catheter promptly (helium enters blood → gas embolism risk if delayed)
5. Notify physician immediately

*Balloon rupture is most often caused by plaque abrasion. Majority of ruptures identified by blood tracking back into extracorporeal tubing or helium gas loss alarm.*

---

## 10. Weaning & Removal

- Wean by reducing ratio: 1:1 → 1:2 → 1:3 over several hours
- Assess hemodynamics at each step (MAP, CO, cardiac filling pressures)
- If tolerated on 1:3 for 1–2 hours → remove
- **Do not leave on 1:2 or 1:3 for >4–6 hours** — risk of thromboembolism on a partially inflating balloon without anticoagulation (Fully anticoagulated)
- Hold anticoagulation 4–6h before removal; apply manual pressure ≥30 min post-removal
