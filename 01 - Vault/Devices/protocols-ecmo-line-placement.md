# ECMO Line Placement

## Overview

Extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) cannulation requires precise vascular access. The cannulation strategy depends on the type of ECMO support — veno-arterial (V-A) or veno-venous (V-V) — and the clinical scenario. Proper line placement is critical for circuit function, patient safety, and limb perfusion.

## Cannulation Configurations

### Veno-Arterial (V-A) ECMO

V-A ECMO provides both cardiac and respiratory support. Blood is drained from the venous system and returned to the arterial system.

| Cannula | Common Site | Typical Size |
|---|---|---|
| Drainage (venous) | Right femoral vein or right internal jugular vein | 21–25 Fr |
| Return (arterial) | Right or left femoral artery | 15–21 Fr |
| Distal perfusion catheter (DPC) | Ipsilateral superficial femoral artery (antegrade) | 6–10 Fr |

- **Femoral arterial return** is the most common peripheral V-A configuration
- A **distal perfusion catheter** is placed in the superficial femoral artery to prevent limb ischemia on the cannulated side
- The DPC should be placed **prophylactically at the time of cannulation** — prophylactic placement is associated with significantly lower rates of limb ischemia compared to reactive placement
- Smaller arterial cannulas (≤15 Fr) combined with NIRS monitoring may further reduce ischemic complications

### Antegrade DPC Site Selection

Proper DPC placement requires understanding the relationship between the arterial cannula and the femoral bifurcation:

- The DPC is inserted **antegrade** (directed distally toward the foot) into the **superficial femoral artery (SFA)** on the same side as the arterial return cannula
- The insertion site should be **distal to the femoral bifurcation** — the SFA is the target vessel, not the common femoral artery
- If percutaneous access fails, **surgical cut-down** for direct DPC insertion should be performed rather than leaving the limb without distal perfusion
- The DPC is connected to the ECMO circuit's arterial limb (or a side port) to supply oxygenated blood to the distal leg
- After placement, confirm flow with **Doppler ultrasound** of the dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial arteries
- DPC size should be selected based on vessel caliber — larger DPCs (8–10 Fr) may provide more adequate flow, though evidence suggests benefit regardless of size

### Veno-Venous (V-V) ECMO

V-V ECMO provides respiratory support only. Blood is drained and returned to the venous system.

| Cannula | Common Site | Typical Size |
|---|---|---|
| Drainage (venous) | Right femoral vein | 23–25 Fr |
| Return (venous) | Right internal jugular vein | 19–23 Fr |
| Dual-lumen single cannula | Right internal jugular vein (Avalon/Crescent) | 27–31 Fr |

- Drainage and return cannulas should be separated by at least **8–10 cm** to minimize recirculation
- Dual-lumen cannulas are positioned with the tip in the IVC and the return port directed toward the tricuspid valve

## Femoral Anatomy for Arterial Cannulation

Understanding the femoral vascular anatomy is essential for safe arterial cannulation and distal perfusion catheter placement.

![](99%20-%20Meta/Assets/Images/Femoral%20anatomy%20for%20ecmo%20cannulation-optimized%202.webp)

**Key landmarks:**
- **External iliac artery** transitions to the **common femoral artery** below the inguinal ligament
- The **common femoral artery access point** is the target for arterial cannulation — located between the inguinal ligament and the femoral bifurcation
- Below the bifurcation, the artery divides into the **superficial femoral artery** (where the DPC is placed) and the **deep femoral artery**
- The **inferior epigastric artery** branches off near the inguinal ligament — cannulation should occur **below** this landmark
- The **external iliac vein** and **common femoral vein** run medial to the artery

## Nursing Considerations for Line Placement

### Assessment After Cannulation

- **Limb checks** every 1 hour: pulses (palpation and Doppler), color, temperature, sensation, and capillary refill on the cannulated extremity
- Monitor **regional oximetry (NIRS)** on bilateral lower extremities if available
- Verify **DPC flow** — ensure it is not kinked, clamped, or clotted
- Document cannula insertion depth (cm at skin) and secure dressing

### Signs of Limb Ischemia

| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| Cool, pale, or mottled extremity | Notify provider immediately |
| Absent or diminished distal pulses | Verify DPC patency; Doppler assessment |
| NIRS StO2 < 50% for > 4 minutes, or > 15% differential between legs | Urgent provider notification |
| Pain or paresthesia in cannulated limb | Assess compartment pressures if indicated |

### Line Security & Site Care

- ECMO cannulas must be **sutured and secured** — any migration can be life-threatening
- Dressing changes per institutional protocol (typically every 48–72 hours or if soiled)
- Monitor insertion sites for signs of bleeding, hematoma, or infection
- **Never** adjust cannula position without a physician or ECMO specialist present
- Ensure adequate **slack** in tubing to prevent accidental dislodgement during patient repositioning
