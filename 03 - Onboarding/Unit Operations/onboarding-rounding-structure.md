# Rounding Structure

> Revised structure effective July 2026. Applies to **HFIMU** and **SHIMU** units.

## Overview

Rounds are conducted in a **multidisciplinary format** involving the attending physician, fellows, APPs, and the bedside RN for each patient. The goal is to strengthen interdisciplinary collaboration, improve patient safety, support nursing education, and optimize outcomes including reduced length of stay and timely escalation of care.

## Rounding Workflow

### Starting Rounds

- The fellow, APP, or attending should **call the bedside RN** at the start of each patient discussion
- Directly engage the bedside RN during rounds — nurses' firsthand insights are critical for patient-centered care and shared decision-making
- This is especially important for **newer nurses** who may be less familiar with the complexities of our patient population

### Communicating Timing

- When possible, communicate an **estimated round start time** to nursing staff so they can "soft block" their workflow
- Fellows and APPs can relay timing to the **charge nurses**, who will disseminate to floor RNs

### When the Bedside RN Is Unavailable

If the RN is caring for an unstable patient, off-unit for diagnostics, or on break:

1. The fellow or APP should communicate the RN's unavailability and reason (when known)
2. Notify the **charge nurse**, who can join rounds as a liaison to ensure key information is exchanged

## Goals of Rounds

- **Care plan review** — medications, diagnostics, daily clinical goals, and barriers to discharge
- **Safety checks** — identify and mitigate risks
- **Clinical concerns** — open discussion of any issues related to ongoing care
- **Education** — incorporate teaching moments, particularly for junior staff

## Nursing Documentation of Major Clinical Events

Bedside RNs are expected to document significant clinical events or changes in a timely and structured manner.

### Events Requiring Documentation

- New or worsening neurological symptoms (e.g., signs of stroke)
- Acute change in mental status
- Development of pressure injuries
- Significant alterations in vital signs
- Escalation of pain or other concerning symptoms

### Recommended Format

1. **Date and time** of the event
2. **Description** of the clinical change
3. **Vital signs** and relevant physical exam findings
4. **Nursing interventions** initiated in response
5. **Patient response** to those interventions
6. **Communication** — time physician team was notified and their recommendations/plan
