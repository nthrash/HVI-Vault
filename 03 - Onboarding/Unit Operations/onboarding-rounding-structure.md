## HFICU Night

> See also: [[onboarding-overnight-expectations|Overnight Expectations]] · [[onboarding-call-tree|HFICU Call Tree]]

### Team Composition

- **Nurse Practitioner** — covers HFICU overnight
- **Heart Failure Fellow** — in-house, covers all other heart failure patients throughout the hospital
- **Heart Failure Attending** — on call from home; refer to the **On Call tab** for the current attending
- **PCCM Fellow** — in-house, available for ventilator management, sedation, procedures, and admissions; covers **all hospital critical care**, not exclusively HFICU
- **PCCM Attending** — in-house, available for escalation and co-signature on central access lines; also covers all hospital critical care

### Rounding Expectations

- Rounding is performed **room by room** following the workflow outlined in the [[onboarding-overnight-expectations#Overnight Rounding (1930 - 2200/2300)|Overnight Rounding]] section
- **Bedside nursing expectations during APP rounds:**
  - Present any **acute concerns** communicated during the day-to-night nursing handoff
  - Provide **flat hemodynamic numbers** — either documented in the chart or available as a photo to show during rounds
  - If flat hemodynamics have not yet been performed, they should be **in progress** at the time of rounding
- The APP must **record hemodynamics** in order to discuss patients with **Dr. Patel around 2200** for the nightly patient discussion

### Nursing Communication

- **Non-emergent communication** — use **Epic Secure Chat** for proper triaging and documentation
- **Emergent communication** — call the Spectralink phone at **44057**
- All non-emergent communication should go through Epic Chat; reserve phone calls for emergencies

### Nursing Responsibilities

- **Intake and outputs** — nursing staff must document I&Os **hourly** so APPs can properly review charts during rounding and overnight assessments
- **Notification to APPs** — nursing staff have their own escalation protocol, but APPs must be notified of any **drip (gtt) changes**, changes in **clinical condition**, or any other significant patient status changes
- **Vasopressors and inotropes** — all vasopressor and inotrope titrations are **regulated by provider staff**; nursing staff should **not** titrate these medications unless explicitly instructed by the APP or physician team

### APP Documentation Expectations

- **Overnight events** — document all significant clinical events occurring on shift
- **Post-operative patients** — any patient arriving from the OR on your shift requires a **post-op note**
  - Post-op note templates can be obtained from your preceptor and through **Epic templates**
- **Procedure notes** — document all bedside procedures performed overnight
- **Billing** — no billing is expected overnight
- **Central access lines** — require a **PCCM attending co-signature**
- **New hires** — some supervising APPs who are orienting new staff may require co-signatures on all notes; confirm with your preceptor

### Escalation

Refer to the [[onboarding-call-tree|HFICU Call Tree]] for the full overnight escalation protocol. Key points:

- An **in-house PCCM fellow and attending** are available overnight but cover **all hospital critical care**, not exclusively HFICU
- For surgical, cardiology, and PCCM escalation pathways, follow the call tree documentation

## HFIMU and SHIMU

### Overview

Rounds are conducted in a **multidisciplinary format** involving the attending physician, fellows, APPs, and the bedside RN for each patient. The goal is to strengthen interdisciplinary collaboration, improve patient safety, support nursing education, and optimize outcomes including reduced length of stay and timely escalation of care.

### Rounding Workflow

#### Starting Rounds

- The fellow, APP, or attending should **call the bedside RN** at the start of each patient discussion
- Directly engage the bedside RN during rounds — nurses' firsthand insights are critical for patient-centered care and shared decision-making
- This is especially important for **newer nurses** who may be less familiar with the complexities of our patient population

#### Communicating Timing

- When possible, communicate an **estimated round start time** to nursing staff so they can "soft block" their workflow
- Fellows and APPs can relay timing to the **charge nurses**, who will disseminate to floor RNs

#### When the Bedside RN Is Unavailable

If the RN is caring for an unstable patient, off-unit for diagnostics, or on break:

1. The fellow or APP should communicate the RN's unavailability and reason (when known)
2. Notify the **charge nurse**, who can join rounds as a liaison to ensure key information is exchanged

### Goals of Rounds

- **Care plan review** — medications, diagnostics, daily clinical goals, and barriers to discharge
- **Safety checks** — identify and mitigate risks
- **Clinical concerns** — open discussion of any issues related to ongoing care
- **Education** — incorporate teaching moments, particularly for junior staff

### Nursing Documentation of Major Clinical Events

Bedside RNs are expected to document significant clinical events or changes in a timely and structured manner.

#### Events Requiring Documentation

- New or worsening neurological symptoms (e.g., signs of stroke)
- Acute change in mental status
- Development of pressure injuries
- Significant alterations in vital signs
- Escalation of pain or other concerning symptoms

#### Recommended Format

1. **Date and time** of the event
2. **Description** of the clinical change
3. **Vital signs** and relevant physical exam findings
4. **Nursing interventions** initiated in response
5. **Patient response** to those interventions
6. **Communication** — time physician team was notified and their recommendations/plan
